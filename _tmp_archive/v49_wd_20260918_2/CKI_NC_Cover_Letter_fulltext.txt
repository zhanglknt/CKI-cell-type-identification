Dear Editors,
On behalf of my co-author, Dr. Xianming Wu (Chinese Institute for Brain Research, Beijing), I submit our manuscript, “CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics”, for consideration as an Article in Nature Communications. CKI (Cell-type Ka/Ks-inspired Index) is, to our knowledge, the first framework to make a principled separation of functional divergence from background drift in transcriptomic comparisons: adapting the Ka/Ks ratio’s logic, it decomposes divergence into a housekeeping baseline k_n and an identity-gene rate k_f (ω = k_f/k_n). The work fits Nature Communications’ scope of computational methods validated on large-scale real data.
The manuscript rests on three pillars. First, in ground-truth simulation (1,750 replicates), ω alone did not false-trigger on neutral housekeeping drift (false-positive rate 0.00 versus 0.55–0.58 for JS and cosine) and ranked first for functional-versus-neutral discrimination (AUC = 0.80). Second, in real-data drift calibration, ω misreported none of 30 cross-lane technical-replicate pairs (Kang IFN-β PBMC data; raw JS 36.7%, cosine 23.3%) and, on 2,161 brain technical-drift pairs, misreported least of seven metrics (28.6% versus 44–45%, lowest in all ten cell classes), with the shallowest drift-ladder gradient (1.04 → 1.76 → 1.80 versus 1.07 → 3.32 → 2.98 for raw JS). Third, across 3,596 TCGA samples in five cancer types, tumor specimens were consistently less divergent than adjacent non-tumor tissue (NN/TT ω ratio 1.13–2.46, bootstrap CIs excluding 1 in four of five)—reflecting a 2.1–3.6-fold elevated housekeeping baseline, not reduced functional divergence; and in lung adenocarcinoma the k_f/k_n decomposition separates KRAS-mutant tumors (functional component, P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷) from EGFR-mutant tumors (baseline-driven).
Previously raised concerns that (i) the index does not outperform existing metrics and (ii) the work may hold limited interest for a broad biological readership have been addressed directly. On (i): the classification benchmark (AUC = 0.680, 5th of 5) is by design—CKI trades global-identity sensitivity for identity-gene divergence, and classification is outside its question domain; on the appropriate benchmark, misreporting control, ω misreports technical and donor drift least among all compared metrics while retaining sensitivity to biology. On (ii): the pan-cancer divergence map and the LUAD driver-class decomposition give the work direct cancer-biology relevance, and the drift-ladder calibration speaks to anyone who has seen batch effects masquerade as biology.
