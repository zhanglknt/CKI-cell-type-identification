新小节标题（替换现 541 行标题）：

> **A pan-cancer map of tissue-level functional divergence in tumors**

替换 543/545/547/549 四段为以下三段（引用编号沿用现稿：TCGA 数据 [10,11]，Edmondson [15] 不再需要，PAM50 [16,17] 移入 supplementary 表述可省略）：

**第一段（泛癌排名 + k_n 机制）：**

> We applied CKI to TCGA bulk RNA-seq data across five cancer types (LUAD, LUSC, LIHC, KIRC, BRCA) [10,11], totalling 3,596 samples, and asked how tissue-level functional divergence is reorganized when cancer develops. Because bulk RNA-seq averages over tumor, stromal, and immune compartments, ω here quantifies divergence between tissue states rather than between cell types; we therefore compare tumor–tumor (TT), normal–normal (NN), and tumor–normal (TN) pairs within each cancer type (Fig. 4). A consistent pan-cancer reversal emerged: in all five cancer types, NN pairs were more divergent than TT pairs—the ratio of mean NN to mean TT ω ranked LUAD 2.46 (95% CI 2.13–2.86; cluster-bootstrap, B = 1,000), KIRC 1.90 (1.65–2.17), LUSC 1.82 (1.46–2.25), BRCA 1.57 (1.35–1.83), and LIHC 1.13 (0.97–1.34)—with the CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers (Mann-Whitney P = 1.2 × 10⁻²³⁸ to 2.6 × 10⁻⁷⁵; LIHC P = 9.9 × 10⁻³, CI including 1). Component decomposition identified the mechanism: in every cancer type the housekeeping baseline was elevated in tumors—TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 2.1–3.6-fold at the median (mean-ratio 95% CIs excluding 1 in all five cancers, e.g. KIRC 3.21 [2.51, 4.28], LIHC 1.41 [1.08, 1.98])—whereas TT k_f was equal to or higher than NN k_f (e.g. LUAD 0.282 versus 0.226). The reversal therefore reflects a more stable housekeeping baseline among adjacent non-tumor specimens, not smaller functional-gene divergence of tumor cells (Fig. 4a).

**第二段（LUAD 突变梯度 + 机制分解）：**

> To ask whether the ratio resolves biologically distinct tumor states, we stratified LUAD tumors by driver mutation (EGFR-mutant n = 61, KRAS-mutant n = 120, wild-type n = 311; double mutants, n = 2, excluded) using per-tumor ω (the mean over each tumor's within-cohort TT pairs; Fig. 4b). KRAS-mutant tumors showed the highest tissue-level divergence (mean ω = 136.9), exceeding wild-type (115.4; Kruskal-Wallis across groups P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷; Dunn–Holm P = 3.6 × 10⁻⁷) and EGFR-mutant tumors (122.2; Dunn–Holm P = 0.008), while EGFR-mutant and wild-type tumors did not differ significantly (Dunn–Holm P = 0.39; bootstrap difference 6.8, 95% CI −2.1 to 15.2). The k_f/k_n decomposition separated the two drivers' associations mechanistically (Fig. 4c,d): the KRAS contrast carried a functional component—k_f was elevated in KRAS-mutant tumors (KRAS versus EGFR, Dunn–Holm P = 0.015; KRAS−WT k_f difference 0.011, 95% CI 0.002–0.019)—accompanied by a lower k_n (WT > KRAS, Dunn–Holm P = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴), both of which raise the ratio; the EGFR association, by contrast, showed no k_f difference (all Holm-adjusted P > 0.09) and was visible only in the k_n baseline (WT > EGFR, P = 0.09). That a single bulk-level statistic, read through its components, distinguishes a functionally divergent driver class (KRAS) from a baseline-driven one (EGFR) is precisely the decomposition CKI is designed to provide—although these are observational associations without patient-level covariate adjustment (no age, sex, or smoking metadata were available locally), and tumor purity differences between driver classes cannot be excluded.

**第三段（bulk 局限与三重混杂控制）：**

> Three controls bound the interpretation of the pan-cancer reversal. First, a four-panel marker composition check (immune, myeloid, stromal, epithelial) with sample-level cluster bootstrap found the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged after composition adjustment (−1.2%, bootstrap 95% CI −4.1% to +2.6%; per cancer type −16% to +37%), so marker-measurable composition explains little of the pooled reversal, though as a noisy proxy it bounds rather than settles the question (Supplementary Note 8). Second, all reported TCGA statistics were recomputed under a linear probability mapping (p_i = (TPM + 1)/Σ(TPM + 1), replacing the softmax-over-log2 map): the reversal, its ranking, the k_n elevation, and the LUAD mutation gradient all survived with quantitatively similar effect sizes (Methods). Third, the k_f/k_n decomposition itself localizes the reversal in the denominator, which composition adjustment of k_n leaves intact. Remaining limitations are inherent to bulk resolution: TCGA "normal" samples are adjacent non-tumor tissues from cancer patients, not healthy donor tissue, so NN comparisons quantify heterogeneity among adjacent non-tumor specimens; RNA quality differences between tumor and normal specimens cannot be excluded; and per-tumor ω was not associated with overall survival in LIHC (Cox HR per SD 1.06, 95% CI 0.85–1.32, P = 0.59, adjusted for stage, grade, age, and sex; Methods), so the reversal is a descriptive property of tissue-state divergence, not a prognostic marker. We report all TCGA findings as tissue-level functional divergence at bulk resolution, pending single-cell or deconvolution-based validation.

## 2. Methods 增补段（英文，加在现有 "Clinical severity analysis" 小节处替换/扩展）

> **Per-sample divergence and group statistics (TCGA).** Per-tumor statistics were derived from the linear-normalization pair table (35,306 pairs: 2,000 TT pairs per cancer type drawn by seeded subsampling, complete NN pairs, and 2,000 TN pairs per cancer type; sample-labelled), as the mean of ω, k_f, and k_n over all pairs in which a given sample participates (median 4–11 pairs per tumor across cancer types). Group-level NN/TT and k_n ratios are reported as ratios of means with 95% confidence intervals from a sample-level cluster bootstrap (B = 1,000; seed 42): tumor and normal samples were resampled with replacement independently, each pair reweighted by the product of its endpoint resampling weights, and the ratio recomputed per resample. LUAD driver groups were assigned by matching cBioPortal mutation labels to the expression matrix by 15-character TCGA barcode (75 EGFR-labelled and 161 KRAS-labelled aliquots, of which 62 EGFR and 122 KRAS matched the matrix; 2 double mutants excluded), yielding 61 EGFR, 120 KRAS, and 311 wild-type tumors with pair coverage. Between-group differences were tested with Kruskal-Wallis followed by Dunn post-hoc tests with Holm correction (manual implementation, tie-corrected rank variances); group mean differences are reported with within-group bootstrap 95% CIs (B = 1,000). The LIHC survival analysis used Cox proportional-hazards regression (statsmodels PHReg) with per-tumor ω standardized to unit SD (hazard ratios per SD), adjusted for AJCC stage (I–IV), Edmondson grade (G1–G4), age, and sex, with listwise deletion of missing covariates; k_f-only, k_n-only, and tumor–normal-ω exposures were run as sensitivity models. Scripts: notebooks/nc49_tcga_main.py, notebooks/nc49_pilot_lihc_cox.py; outputs: results/nc49_tcga_pancancer.csv, results/nc49_tcga_luad_mutation.csv, results/nc49_pilot_lihc_cox.csv.
