Four controls bound the interpretation of the pan-cancer reversal. First, a four-panel marker composition check (immune, myeloid, stromal, epithelial) with sample-level cluster bootstrap found the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged after composition adjustment (−1.2%, bootstrap 95% CI −4.1% to +2.6%; per cancer type −16% to +37%), so marker-measurable composition explains little of the pooled reversal, though as a noisy proxy it bounds rather than settles the question (Supplementary Note 8). A gene-signature purity sensitivity settles the direction: scoring every sample with the ESTIMATE stromal and immune signatures (combined admixture; Methods), per-tumor k_n correlated negatively with admixture in every cancer type (r = −0.23 to −0.42), so admixture can only weaken, not create, the TT k_n elevation—and restricting tumor–tumor comparisons to the low-admixture (high-purity) half of tumors increased the NN/TT ratio in all five cancer types (LUAD 2.46 → 2.86, 95% CI 2.41–3.45; LIHC 1.10 → 1.17, its CI still including 1, so the four-of-five CI caliber is preserved; Section 3.13 of the Supplementary Information). The reversal is therefore not an artefact of stromal or immune admixture. Second, all reported TCGA statistics were recomputed under a linear probability mapping (p_i = (TPM + 1)/Σ(TPM + 1), replacing the softmax-over-log2 map), and this linear re-computation is the authoritative caliber throughout: the reversal, its ranking, the k_n elevation, and the LUAD mutation gradient were preserved in direction and rank order, although the LIHC effect size is mapping-sensitive and quantitatively weaker under the linear mapping (NN/TT mean ratio 1.10 versus 1.31 under the softmax mapping; softmax-caliber values are archived as a sensitivity analysis in Section 1.7 of the Supplementary Information). The pan-cancer reversal—tumor specimens less divergent than adjacent non-tumor tissue (mean NN/TT 1.10–2.46, cluster-bootstrap CIs excluding 1 in four of five cancers)—is a robust tissue-level observation whose mechanistic reading remains cautious: at bulk RNA-seq resolution the apparent convergence could be driven by cell-composition shifts (tumor purity, stromal and immune infiltration), peritumoral inflammation, or systematic RNA quality differences rather than genuine transcriptional convergence of tumor cells. Component decomposition reinforces this caution: the NN/TT reversal is predominantly a k_n effect—normal-normal pairs show systematically lower housekeeping-gene divergence than tumor-tumor pairs in all five cancer types, while tumor pairs show the higher functional-gene divergence (k_f)—so the data do not indicate smaller functional divergence of tumor cells. A marker-panel composition check quantifies the composition confound directly (Supplementary Note 8): tumor-tumor pairs do show larger composition differences than normal-normal pairs (median |Delta z| 1.33–1.46-fold higher across the immune, stromal, and epithelial panels, and 1.24-fold with the myeloid panel included; all P < 10⁻⁷⁷), and within tumor-tumor pairs k_n correlates with the overall four-panel composition difference (Spearman ρ = 0.387 pooled; 0.23–0.52 per cancer type); however, in regressions of log k_n on pair type with the four composition deltas as covariates—including a myeloid panel (CD68, CD163, LST1, FCGR3A, C1QA)—and sample-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (B = 200), the tumor-pair coefficient attenuates by −1.2% pooled (bootstrap 95% CI [−4.1%, +2.6%]), with marked per-cancer heterogeneity (−16% to +37%; linear-normalization caliber, the authoritative caliber throughout; the superseded softmax-caliber estimates are archived in Section 1.7 of the Supplementary Inform
ation). Because marker panels are noisy proxies for true composition, these attenuation estimates are lower bounds on the true composition contribution; the pooled tumor-pair k_n excess is therefore not fully explained by a noisy marker-panel proxy, but neither can a marker-panel regression exclude composition as the driver—plausibly reflecting tumor-specific housekeeping dysregulation 38 or RNA-quality differences instead, and single-cell or deconvolution-based validation remains necessary. Two further limitations bound the bulk analysis: within each cancer type, tumor and adjacent non-tumor aliquots differ systematically in tissue-source site and processing batch (a known TCGA property), so part of the NN/TT contrast could track these technical strata—we did not test a batch-matched subset; and adjacent non-tumor tissue from cancer patients is not healthy tissue, so separating tumor-specific housekeeping elevation from field effects will ultimately require an external healthy reference (e.g., GTEx), which we leave to future work. Within LUAD, the KRAS enrichment among ever-smokers mirrors known lung-adenocarcinoma epidemiology, yet smoking status itself does not carry the divergence association, so the KRAS-linked functional divergence is unlikely to be a smoking-field effect; and although the purity adjustment relies on an ESTIMATE-style signature score rather than a consensus-purity estimate, the score is monotonically equivalent to published ESTIMATE purity, leaving the adjusted conclusions unchanged. The PAM50 gradient cannot be read as a functional-divergence ordering: under k_f-only analysis the subtype ordering largely reverses (Luminal A lowest mean k_f, Basal-like highest; Supplementary Note 9), so the ω gradient is predominantly a baseline effect, and the proliferation-confound explanation (aggressive subtypes proliferate fastest) remains. The raw ω scale 
also varies substantially across datasets and computation schemes (e.g., BRCA Luminal A ω ≈ 142.0 versus mean brain regional ω = 38.55). In TCGA, per-pair k_n is comparable to the single-cell datasets (mean 0.005, range 1.6 × 10⁻⁴–5.6 × 10⁻², versus a mean per-pair k_n of 0.0035 in the brain single-nucleus dataset), so the higher raw ω in TCGA is driven primarily by a larger k_f: bulk tumor-versus-normal expression differences span thousands of genes. Cross-dataset ω comparisons should therefore be interpreted as rank-based rather than absolute.
