## Minor issues

**M1. MK "neutrality index" direction is convention-inverted (one clause fix).**
MS Discussion states that ω_cal = ω/7.70 "parallels the direction of the neutrality index: between-population divergence in the numerator, within-population baseline in the denominator." By the canonical McDonald–Kreitman definition the neutrality index is NI = (P_nonsyn/P_syn)/(D_nonsyn/D_syn) — polymorphism (within-population) in the *numerator*. ω_cal as constructed is therefore the *reciprocal* of NI (equivalently, the subtractand of the MK α statistic, α = 1 − (P_n/P_s)/(D_n/D_s)). The formula given in the text is unambiguous, so this is a wording problem, not a conceptual error; but as written the sentence inverts the standard convention and will trip population-genetics readers. Suggest: "ω_cal corresponds to the reciprocal of the MK neutrality index (equivalently, the term subtracted in the MK α statistic)".

**M2. Aggregation-order default: no same-data quantification of the order effect, and a MS/GUIDE wording mismatch.**
The new Methods paragraph and Guide §2 paragraph correctly state that absolute ω is not comparable across the two aggregation orders and that cross-order comparisons must be rank-based. However: (a) no same-dataset number quantifies the effect (e.g., recomputing the 15 mouse-pilot pairs under the brain order and reporting the rank correlation and median fold change of ω would cost little and would convert a qualitative warning into a calibrated one); (b) MS Methods attributes the legacy `softmax(mean(log1p))` order to "the mouse (Tabula Muris pilot) and human (Tabula Sapiens) pipelines", while GUIDE §2 attributes the legacy order to "the TCGA bulk pipeline" without mentioning mouse/human. Both may be true, but as written a reader cannot tell which reported analyses used which order from the Guide alone. A consequence worth one explicit sentence: making the brain order the package default means `pip install`-ed defaults will *not* reproduce the reported mouse-pilot and Tabula Sapiens absolute ω values (rankings presumably preserved, but this is asserted, not shown).

**M3. Rounding that flips an interval's reading (CC-exclusion k_n CI).**
MS Results: "TT k_n/NN k_n 1.34, 95% CI [1.00, 1.88]" for the CC-excluded LIHC sensitivity. The authoritative file (`results/nc49_cc_excl_sensitivity.csv`) gives 1.337, CI [0.997, 1.880]. Printing the lower bound as "1.00" converts an interval that *includes* 1 into one that appears to touch/exclude it. The sentence's claim ("null result unchanged") is unaffected and actually supported by the inclusion, but the printed bound should be 0.997 (or "[1.0, 1.9], including 1").

**M4. LOO framing tension (cosmetic).**
Team communication framed the LOO analysis as evidence the baseline is "not single-population driven", while the MS text itself (correctly and honestly) states the opposite nuance: "a leave-one-population-out sensitivity shows the constant depends on one outlier population". The MS version is the right one; I note this only to confirm the manuscript does not overclaim. No change needed beyond possibly softening the Cover Letter's silence on the ±12–14% ω_cal shift (the CL does not mention calibration robustness at all, which is acceptable).

## Assessment of the four v49.13 changes (as requested)

1. **LOO calibration sensitivity — adequate.** The analysis directly answers my Round-3 concern that 7.70 could be a single-population artifact. The honest framing ("the constant depends on one outlier population"; ω_cal shifts ≤ ~14%; all ratio statements baseline-invariant) is exactly the right conclusion; verified numerically.
2. **Aggregation-order default — substantively adequate, with residual gaps (M2).** The disclosure is now in both MS and Guide and the recommendation (rank statistics across orders) is correct. What is missing is a single same-data quantification of the order effect and a fix to the MS/Guide attribution mismatch.
3. **Top-200 N sensitivity — adequate as disclosure.** The statement correctly links the un-swept N to the selection floor (median baseline k_f = 0.025, verified) and to bounded ω power. Combined with SI 1.4's explicit ascertainment-asymmetry point and the fixed-panel ablation, the circularity/ascertainment problem is now disclosed to a standard I can accept without further simulation demands.
4. **Fourth MK correspondence — improves the analogy, one wording fix needed (M1).** Framing ω_cal as an MK-type between/within contrast is more defensible than the earlier loose dN/dS framing, and the manuscript correctly flags that the reference class is constrained, not neutral, and that all NI caveats are inherited. The only defect is the inverted "direction of the neutrality index" phrase.
