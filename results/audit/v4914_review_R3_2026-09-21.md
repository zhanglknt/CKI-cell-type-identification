## Statistical assessment of the new analyses

**C1 (B = 1,000)**. The pooled bootstrap median shifted from −1.2% to −1.3% and the CI narrowed slightly asymmetrically ([−5.0, +2.3] → [−4.8, +2.0]) — the expected behavior of a Monte-Carlo refinement, and the authors quote the median rather than the point estimate (−1.26%), which is the more stable summary. Per-cancer CIs widened where B = 200 had understated endpoint noise (LUSC upper endpoint +4.6% → +6.8%); this is honest and changes no conclusion. LIHC and KIRC CIs still exclude 0; the heterogeneity disclosure stands.

**C2 (ex-CC Cox)**. Refitting the full M1–M6 family after excluding the 32 ILSBio cell-line tumors is the correct sensitivity for the barcode-reassignment concern. The ω null is unchanged (HR/SD 1.083 vs 1.075, both P ≈ 0.48, CIs overlapping widely), and events drop 101 → 79 proportionally. No multiplicity issue: this is a sensitivity, not a new test family, and is reported as such.

**C3 (aggregation order)**. Same-data quantification on 15 mouse-pilot comparisons is the right design — both pipelines on identical cells, so the comparison isolates the order effect. The report is balanced: rank preservation (ρ = 0.78) versus absolute shifts (up to 9.8×) and a moving calibration baseline (6.46 → 10.94), with an explicit non-transferability warning. My hand recomputation of ρ from the archived 15 rows reproduces 0.779 exactly.

**A2**. The ex-CC LIHC k_n ratio CI [0.997, 1.880] now includes 1, and the text says so. This marginally weakens the LIHC component claim post-reassignment, but disclosure rather than omission is the correct choice, and the ω-level conclusion (LIHC CI includes 1 throughout) is unchanged.
