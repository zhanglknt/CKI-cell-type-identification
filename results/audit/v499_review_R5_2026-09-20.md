# Review — Reviewer 5 (Neuroscience single-cell atlases)

**Manuscript:** "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics" (v49.9)
**Track:** Nature Communications — Methodology
**Review date:** 2026-09-20
**Reviewer expertise:** Brain single-cell/nucleus atlases (Allen/BRAIN Initiative–style data), regional cell-type heterogeneity

---

## Summary

The authors propose CKI, a heuristic ratio ω = k_f/k_n of JS divergences computed on identity genes versus housekeeping (HK) genes at pseudobulk level, positioned as a "specificity-first" divergence index. The brain application uses the Siletti et al. (2023) human brain atlas (non-neuronal nuclei only; 888,263 nuclei; 886,808 after filtering; 108 regions; 4 donors; 606 10x libraries) to compute ω for 31,764 same-cell-type cross-region pairs across 10 non-neuronal supercluster classes. Headline results: a 6.10-fold cross-class gradient of mean ω (astrocytes 82.75 highest; Bergmann glia and vascular cells 13.56 lowest), class-level significance under a library block-shuffle null for 4/10 classes (3/10 after BH), and a multiplicative-residual screen yielding 39 "Strong" anomalously-similar (cell type, region pair) candidates — of which 31 reach raw permutation P < 0.05 and **none survives FDR correction** (minimum q = 0.520; the Strong rule is in fact *anti-enriched* relative to its design-matched null: 148.3 expected vs 39 observed).

A note on version numbers before proceeding: the review assignment characterized the residual analysis as "30 Strong / 16 FDR-significant out of m = 31,764." These figures do **not** appear in v49.9. The manuscript reports **39 Strong / 0 FDR-significant** (minimum q = 0.520); 16 is the microglia *subset* of the Strong tier, and 30 is the implied count of raw-P < 0.05 candidates across the five classes enumerated in the Results (see Major Concern 4). MS and SI are mutually consistent at 39/31/0.520. My assessment below uses the v49.9 numbers throughout.

The manuscript is unusually self-critical: class-size confounding, donor sharing, circular gene selection, permutation resolution, and null calibration are all probed and disclosed. Nevertheless, from a brain-atlas standpoint the central brain claims rest on (i) a cross-*class* comparison that the manuscript's own v49.9 scope statement disclaims, (ii) a 4-donor atlas in which region and donor are heavily entangled, (iii) a per-pair screen that is structurally incapable of FDR discovery at B = 1,000, and (iv) a candidate catalogue with no residual statistical support. I recommend **Major Revision**.

## Major concerns

### 1. The brain headline is a cross-cell-type comparison that v49.9's own scope statement disclaims — and ~70% of it is class-size artefact

The v49.9 Discussion states: "the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type, so limited cross-type discrimination of ω is expected by design and delineates, rather than limits, the index's scope." Yet the brain headline — a "6.10-fold regional gradient" from Bergmann glia/vascular cells (13.56) to astrocytes (82.75) — is precisely a **cross-class comparison of mean ω**, and it anchors the abstract ("Brain analysis revealed a 6.10-fold regional gradient"), Fig. 6b, and the Discussion. Two problems compound:

- **Class-size confounding.** The manuscript itself shows class-mean k_n correlates with log10(class size) (Spearman ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043) and that equal-n downsampling (4,118 nuclei/class) "abolishes the class-level k_n ordering" (full vs equal-n ρ = −0.055, P = 0.88), attenuating the ω gradient from 6.10 to **1.74** (95% CI [1.64, 1.84]). By the authors' own accounting, "~70% of the magnitude reflects class-size imbalance." Class sizes span 4,118 (committed OPCs) to 490,246 (oligodendrocytes) nuclei — nearly two orders of magnitude. Reporting the 6.10 figure as the headline with the 1.74 as a parenthetical is, in my view, inverted: the size-balanced estimate is the only defensible cross-class number, and at 1.74-fold the "gradient" is modest.
- **Scope tension.** If cross-type ω comparison is "expected by design" to be uninformative, a cross-class ω ranking cannot be the brain paper's centerpiece. The class-specific split-half baseline correction (gradient 5.99 [4.43, 7.69]) partially addresses differential calibration, but it does not resolve the scope contradiction, and it does not address the class-size mechanism at all (the correction changes baselines, not sampling noise).

**Required:** either (a) re-headline the brain section on the within-class analyses (the 31,764 same-class pairs, which *are* within scope) and demote the cross-class gradient to the size-balanced 1.74 estimate, or (b) justify explicitly why cross-class comparison of class-mean ω is valid here despite the stated scope — including a formal treatment of why the ratio cancels class-size-dependent noise when the equal-n control shows it does not for k_n.

### 2. Donor–region entanglement: with 4 donors and median top-donor share 0.61 per region, "regional" signal is substantially donor signal; the donor-stratified null was not applied to the pair-level screen

The manuscript discloses the key numbers: "The brain atlas contains nuclei from four donors, and 94.5% of region pairs share at least one donor (median top-donor share within a region: 0.61)." In other words, most regions are dominated by a single donor, so a large fraction of the 31,764 cross-region comparisons are effectively cross-*donor* comparisons. Regions are nested within donors; donors are the biological replicates; n = 4 is far below any threshold for donor-level inference.

The authors' mitigations are real but incomplete:

- The within-donor control (same-donor pairs only) preserves the gradient extremes (astrocytes 75.18; Bergmann glia 16.73; 4.50-fold) — but this subset is restricted to regions co-sampled within a donor, a non-random, anatomically biased subset of the 108 regions, and for Bergmann glia it comprises only **10 pairs**.
- The **donor-stratified block-shuffle was applied only to the class-level test (m = 10)**, not to the 31,764-pair residual screen. The free block shuffle permutes library-to-region assignments across donor boundaries, so its null destroys donor structure that the observed data retain; for a screen whose candidates are anatomically concentrated (thalamo-temporal axis; LG/Pul/TF recurrence), donor-confounded regions could masquerade as anatomical structure. The pseudo-region negative control validates *marginal* tail calibration, not anatomical specificity.
- No donor × region coverage matrix is reported anywhere (MS or SI). The reader cannot tell how many of the 108 regions have ≥ 2 donors, or how many of the 39 Strong candidates involve only single-donor regions at one or both endpoints.

**Required:** (i) a donor × region coverage table (per class); (ii) a donor-stratified (or donor-held-out) null for the candidate screen, or an explicit demonstration that the 39 candidates' endpoints are multi-donor; (iii) softening of "regional biology" language wherever the supporting pairs are single-donor at either endpoint.

### 3. The per-pair screen is structurally incapable of FDR discovery at B = 1,000, and the surviving "hypothesis list" is anti-enriched relative to its own null

Two distinct statistical facts, both disclosed but neither given the weight it deserves:

- **Resolution bound.** The manuscript states the BH threshold for the smallest of m = 31,764 P-values (≈ 1.6 × 10⁻⁶) lies "~600-fold below the smallest resolvable P" at B = 1,000 (floor 9.99 × 10⁻⁴), so "q < 0.05 is unattainable unless ~635 of the 31,764 P-values sit at the permutation floor … or B ≈ 6 × 10⁵ permutations are run." This means "no candidate survives FDR correction" is a **foregone conclusion of the implementation**, not an empirical finding. A screen that cannot in principle produce a positive result should not be presented as a screen with an FDR verdict. Adaptive permutation (large B for the ~200 smallest residuals only) is computationally trivial relative to the 72 core-hours already spent; the top candidates (e.g., microglia A14–Pul, ω = 7.94, residual 0.196) could be tested at B = 10⁶ in minutes.
- **Anti-enrichment.** Under the design-matched null that recomputes the full selection rule, the Strong rule expects 148.3 candidates; 39 are observed (P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0). The observed candidates are therefore **3.8-fold depleted** relative to chance expectation of the rule itself. The manuscript frames the catalogue as "a prioritized hypothesis list, not discoveries," which is honest, but the stronger implication is under-discussed: the multiplicative-expectation model plus block shuffle makes the Strong rule fire *less* often on real data than on region-destroyed shuffles — i.e., the regional ω landscape is *more uniform* than the null's, and the 39 "most anomalous" pairs are the least anomalous tail of a depleted rule. What residual prior should a reader attach to, say, the microglial LG/Pul/TF concentration, beyond literature plausibility? At minimum this needs a head-on discussion.

### 4. Internal accounting gap: the Strong-candidate class enumeration sums to 37, not 39; OPC-lineage candidates are silently dropped

MS (Results, candidate screen): "Candidates concentrate in microglia (16 Strong) and oligodendrocytes (10), with fibroblasts (6), astrocytes (3), and ependymal cells (2) contributing the rest; vascular cells, choroid plexus, and Bergmann glia contributed none." These sum to **37**, and "contributing the rest" is arithmetically wrong: OPCs and committed OPCs are mentioned in neither the contributing nor the non-contributing list. Cross-checking against Supplementary Fig. 8b ("12 of 39 Strong" are oligodendrocyte-lineage) implies the missing 2 are OPC/committed-OPC candidates. The per-class raw-P counts given in the text (microglia 13/16, oligodendrocytes 10/10, fibroblasts 4/6, astrocytes 3/3, ependymal 0/2) sum to 30, while the text states "31 of the 39 Strong candidates showed raw one-sided P < 0.05" — again reconcilable only if one of the two unmentioned OPC-lineage candidates has raw P < 0.05. This is exactly where the erroneous "30 Strong / 16 FDR-significant" characterization could have originated. The enumeration must be completed and the totals made to sum explicitly.

### 5. Class-level biology: the ordering partially contradicts the best-established regional heterogeneity in the glial literature, and no positive-control class is pre-specified

The class-level test finds astrocytes, OPCs, and committed OPCs above the null, but **microglia (P = 0.904), oligodendrocytes (P = 0.883), and Bergmann glia (P = 0.998) at or below the null expectation**. Microglial regional heterogeneity is among the best-documented findings in brain single-cell biology (the manuscript itself cites Tan et al. 2020, ref. 19, and microglial regional programs are established in both mouse and human), and mature-oligodendrocyte regional enrichment in adult CNS is likewise established (cited refs 39, 40; Foerster et al. 2024, ref. 25). The manuscript's biological-discussion paragraph nonetheless reads "Microglia showed intermediate divergence: regional phenotypes vary but core surveillance machinery is shared" — language that leans on the literature to imply a signal the class-level test explicitly does not support (mean below null). Either (a) CKI at pseudobulk scale lacks sensitivity to known glial regional programs (a sensitivity limitation that should be stated as such), or (b) the field's regional-heterogeneity estimates are inflated by donor/library structure (a strong claim needing direct evidence). The T3 drift-ladder tier (same donor, different regions; calibration 1.80 vs 1.04 at T1) demonstrates metric-level sensitivity to regional biology in aggregate, but no class-level positive control with a literature-grounded expectation is shown. At minimum: pick one or two class × region-set combinations with strong prior evidence (e.g., astrocyte cortex-vs-cerebellum; microglia cortex-vs-white-matter) and show CKI recovers the expected direction with the design-matched null.

### 6. Exclusion of all neurons removes the principal axis of brain regional variation

"We focused on 888,263 non-neuronal nuclei in 10 major classes …; neurons were excluded because supercluster_term does not resolve neuronal subtypes, violating the same-cell-type assumption." The rationale is internally consistent with the same-cell-type scope — but it excludes the very compartment for which regional specialization is best established (in Siletti et al. itself, neuronal subtypes are the dominant axis of regional variation; cortical area–specific excitatory subtypes and region-restricted inhibitory classes are textbook). The Siletti annotation hierarchy is not limited to supercluster_term: subclass- and cluster-level labels exist, and broad neuronal classes (e.g., deep-layer IT, cortical inhibitory subclasses) would satisfy same-cell-type cross-region comparison at least as well as "vascular cells" and "fibroblasts" do — which the manuscript itself flags as "heterogeneous superclusters aggregating multiple subtypes" whose ω values are "supercluster averages, not properties of one coherent cell type." It is difficult to defend a same-cell-type standard that admits vascular/fibroblast superclusters but excludes all neurons. The consequence for the reader: a brain-region divergence paper in which ~2.4 million of ~3.3 million nuclei — the neurons — are absent, which must be stated in the abstract, not only in Results.

## Minor concerns

1. **SI Section 3.2 reports superseded intervals without flagging them.** SI 3.2 quotes pair-level bootstrap CIs (astrocytes [81.56, 83.89]; Bergmann glia [11.31, 16.14]) while the MS reports region-clustered studentized bootstrap-t CIs (astrocytes [75.66, 89.53]; Bergmann glia [5.76, 28.59]) and states the cluster-aware intervals "are the preferred uncertainty summary." SI Note 2 marks the percentile intervals as superseded for Bergmann glia/choroid plexus but SI 3.2's i.i.d. pair-level intervals (which the MS calls "anti-conservative") remain unmarked. Unify.
2. **Supplementary Fig. 6a criterion vs text.** The figure marks classes significant only at "p < 0.05 and |SES| ≥ 2," but the text claims 4 classes with significantly elevated mean ω, including fibroblasts (P = 0.030, SES = 1.83), which fails the figure's SES ≥ 2 bar. Align the criterion or the claim (post-BH, only 3/10 survive anyway).
3. **SI 1.6 pseudobulk minimum.** SI Section 1.6 states a "minimum of 10 cells per group" for pseudobulk construction, whereas MS Methods states ≥ 20 cells per entry (and the brain pipeline uses ≥ 20 nuclei per (region, class) group, ≥ 50 per region). Clarify that 10 is a package default never used in the reported analyses, if that is the case.
4. **Scheme-specific tier thresholds.** Strong/Moderate/Weak cutoffs (residual < 0.3/0.5/0.75 with ω < 15/25/35) are calibrated to the circular per-pair selection scale (grand mean 38.55 vs 26.5 under leave-pair-out; SI Note 7 states cutoffs "do not transfer across schemes"). This caveat should accompany the tier definitions at first use in the Results, not only in the supplement.
5. **Residual model fit on the same data.** μ_ct, μ_pair, and μ_grand (38.55) are estimated on the same 31,764 pairs that are then tested; expected values and residuals are thus not independent of the tested observations, and (per SI Note 13) the tier variable and per-pair P are "both monotone functions of the same observed and null ω values." The disclosure in Note 13 is appreciated; the main text should carry one sentence to that effect where the 79.5%/35.6%/16.9% tier-graded raw-P enrichment is quoted, since that enrichment is partly guaranteed by construction.
6. **Bergmann-glia stratified-BH family (m = 21, min q = 0.042).** All 21 Bergmann-glia pairs are intra-cerebellar across only 7 regions, and the drift ladder shows unusually large within-donor library effects for this class (block-shuffle null mean 21.9 vs split-half baseline 9.7). A nominally significant family confined to this class is more parsimoniously library structure than regional biology; the MS mentions the family but should state this caveat explicitly.
7. **Post-mortem confounds.** The PMI/RNA-integrity caveat (MS Discussion) is appropriate, but donor-level PMI/RIN "not available" is not the end of the line: library-level QC metrics (median detected genes/UMIs per library per region) exist in the atlas and could serve as covariates in a sensitivity re-fit of the 39 candidates' residuals. Given that HK-set k_n is exactly where differential degradation would act, this is worth the modest effort.
8. **"Aligns with known cell biology" overstates alignment.** For 3 of 10 classes the observed mean ω sits *below* the null expectation; the paragraph "The gradient aligns with known cell biology" is selectively true for astrocytes and vascular/fibroblasts. Please temper.
9. **Fig. 6b** displays the 6.10-fold gradient as the visual headline. Given Concern 1, the size-balanced estimate (1.74 [1.64, 1.84]) deserves at least equal visual prominence.
10. **Computational claim.** "q < 0.05 would require B ≈ 6 × 10⁵ permutations" is used to frame the FDR outcome as a resolution artifact — but B = 6 × 10⁵ is infeasible only for the full landscape; an adaptive two-stage scheme (B = 1,000 screen → B = 10⁶ for the top few hundred pairs) is standard and cheap. Either implement it or remove the implication that resolution, rather than evidence, explains the null FDR result.

## Questions to authors

1. Provide the donor × region (× class) coverage matrix. How many of the 108 regions have ≥ 2 donors per class? How many of the 39 Strong candidates have both endpoints multi-donor?
2. Why was the donor-stratified null restricted to the class-level test? What is the class-level and candidate-level outcome under a donor-held-out design (e.g., recompute the entire landscape excluding each donor in turn)?
3. Which two classes contribute the 2 Strong candidates missing from the Results enumeration (16 + 10 + 6 + 3 + 2 = 37)? Confirm from Supplementary Table 4 and correct the text.
4. Given v49.9's scope statement that k_n baselines are "only directly comparable within the same cell type," on what formal basis is the cross-class gradient (Fig. 6b) licensed? Does the equal-n result (k_n ordering abolished; gradient 6.10 → 1.74) change which number should be the headline?
5. For the Strong rule's anti-enrichment (148.3 expected vs 39 observed): is this depletion driven by the multiplicative model overfitting pair structure, by library-level correlation raising null residuals, or by genuine cross-region uniformity? What does it imply for the catalogue's prior?
6. Can you run B ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ permutations for the top ~200 residual pairs (two-stage adaptive), and report whether any candidate then reaches q < 0.05? If none does even then, the "hypothesis-generating" framing should be softened further to "descriptive catalogue."
7. Why were neuronal subclass-level annotations (below supercluster_term) not used to include at least the broad neuronal classes? Does the vascular/fibroblast supercluster caveat not apply symmetrically?
8. Microglia and mature oligodendrocytes sit at or below the block-shuffle null (P = 0.904, 0.883) despite well-documented regional heterogeneity. Do you interpret this as a CKI sensitivity limit at pseudobulk scale, or as evidence that literature regional-heterogeneity estimates are confounded by donor/library structure? Please address directly.
9. The Strong-tier ω < 15 cutoff and the ω = 7.70/9.73 calibration baselines are scheme-specific; what is the leave-pair-out–matched analogue of the Strong rule, and how many of the 39 survive under S2 (SI Note 7 reports 32/50 = 64% retention at residual < 0.3 — for which tier set)?
10. The same-origin pseudo-region lower-tail rate (37.6%) demonstrates power for within-region similarity; what is the corresponding power estimate for a *cross-region* similarity of the magnitude claimed for the Strong candidates (e.g., residual ≤ 0.2)?

## Score

**4/10** (strict). The methodological scaffolding (design-matched block shuffle, pseudo-region negative control, donor-stratified sensitivity, fixed-panel ablation, equal-n confound control) is exemplary in its candor — but the brain application, as framed, delivers no FDR-supported discovery, a headline gradient that is ~70% class-size artefact and in tension with the manuscript's own scope statement, an internally inconsistent candidate enumeration, and a candidate list that is anti-enriched relative to its own null. The transparency converts what could have been a reject into a revision case: the problems are fixable, and the fixes are largely computational.

## Recommendation

**Major Revision**

Priority conditions: (1) reconcile or re-frame the cross-class gradient against the v49.9 scope claim and headline the size-balanced estimate; (2) donor × region coverage reporting plus donor-respecting inference for the candidate screen; (3) two-stage high-B permutation for top candidates or removal of the FDR framing; (4) complete the Strong-candidate enumeration (37 → 39); (5) a literature-anchored positive control at class level and an explicit treatment of why microglia/oligodendrocytes fall below the null; (6) abstract-level disclosure that neurons (≈73% of the atlas nuclei) were excluded.
