## Minor issues

**M1. The "polymorphism class" analogue conflates sampling noise with standing variation — one caveat sentence is missing.**
In the MK framework, the polymorphism class measures *standing variation among individuals* within the population. The split-half calibration baseline partitions the *same* population at random (each half contains the same donor/individual mix), so it captures sampling noise, technical variation, and stochastic bursting — but not inter-individual standing variation. The manuscript itself already measures the quantity that is the closer MK analogue of polymorphism: donor-versus-donor comparisons (the T2 tier of the brain drift ladder; the donor-drift class in the Kang IFN-β demonstration), both of which sit systematically above the split-half baseline (e.g., brain T2 calibration, donor-drift ω exceeding split-half ω). Because the split-half denominator under-counts standing variation relative to a donor-level baseline, ω_cal is inflated relative to a polymorphism-matched analogue — which is precisely the classic direction-of-bias caveat of the neutrality index when weakly deleterious polymorphism is under-counted. The current text discloses the "denominator class constrained" caveat (MS Discussion; SI 1.4) but not this one. I suggest adding one sentence to the MK paragraph (and mirroring it in SI 1.4) noting that the split-half baseline is a same-individual technical-replicate null rather than standing polymorphism, that donor-level baselines (already computed: brain T2, Kang donor-drift) are the closer MK polymorphism analogue, and stating the resulting direction of bias on ω_cal. This strengthens rather than weakens the analogy, and the required numbers are already in the paper.

**M2. "Parallels the direction of the neutrality index" is technically inverted relative to the conventional NI definition.**
The neutrality index of Rand & Kann is conventionally NI = (Pn/Ps)/(Dn/Ds), with polymorphism in the numerator and divergence in the denominator. ω_cal = ω_obs/ω_baseline places the between-population quantity in the numerator and the within-population baseline in the denominator — i.e., it parallels the direction of 1/NI (equivalently, the direction of the MK α statistic, α = 1 − (Ds·Pn)/(Dn·Ps), where values above the null indicate excess divergence). The colon clarification that follows ("between-population divergence in the numerator, within-population baseline in the denominator") makes the intended meaning unambiguous, so no reader will be misled, but the phrase "parallels the direction of the neutrality index" is, strictly, the reciprocal of the conventional index. Suggested fix (one clause): "parallels the direction of the MK contrast (the reciprocal of the neutrality index)" or "the α-direction of the MK test". Same change in SI 1.4.

**M3 (cosmetic). Fig. 1a legend: add the "constrained" qualifier to the k_n counterpart statement.**
The legend currently reads "CKI uses housekeeping gene divergence for k_n — the counterpart of the synonymous baseline". Everywhere else the manuscript is now careful to say the HK anchor is a *constrained*, not neutral, reference class (MS Introduction/Discussion; SI 1.2/1.4). Adding one word — "the constrained counterpart of the synonymous baseline" — brings the figure legend into line and pre-empts the most common misreading at the figure level. (The legend's hedging of the Ka/Ks side itself — "(a heuristic analogy only, not a formal population-genetic claim)" — is correct and should stay.)

**M4 (optional wording). Introduction, first paragraph of the concept chain.**
"How much of the difference between two populations reflects functional adaptation rather than neutral drift?" — "adaptation" is a loaded term in the evolutionary frame the paper itself invokes; "functional change" or "functional state divergence" would avoid even the appearance of an adaptation claim at first contact. Given the extensive disclaimers that follow, this is a suggestion only.
