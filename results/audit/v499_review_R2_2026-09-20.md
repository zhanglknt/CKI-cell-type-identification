# Review — Reviewer 2 (Evolutionary Genomics / Selection Metrics)

**Manuscript:** "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics" (v49.9)
**Venue:** Nature Communications (Methodology track)
**Review date:** 2026-09-20
**Reviewer expertise:** Ka/Ks and selection-metric methodology in evolutionary genomics

---

## Summary

The authors propose CKI, a "Ka/Ks-inspired" transcriptomic index: k_n is the Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence between two pseudobulk expression vectors restricted to housekeeping (HK) genes; k_f is the same divergence restricted to per-pair-selected "identity" genes; ω = k_f/k_n is offered as a baseline-normalized divergence measure, applied to Tabula Muris/Sapiens, TCGA bulk tumors, and the Siletti brain atlas. The manuscript is unusually transparent about its failure modes — the fixed-panel ablation, anchor-visibility boundary, class-size confounding, and the non-FDR-surviving brain candidate screen are disclosed with a candor I wish more methods papers showed. However, my charge is the evolutionary framing, and there the manuscript has serious problems: (i) the denominator of CKI is the *most constrained* gene class, whereas Ks is the *neutral* standard — the analogy is structurally inverted, not merely "formally non-equivalent" as the authors concede; (ii) the v49.9 Scope concession (k_n comparable only within a cell type) directly contradicts Table 1, which ranks cell types against each other by mean ω on a statistic the authors' own controls show is denominator-driven; and (iii) the relevant population-genetic literature (McDonald–Kreitman-type contrasts; neutral models of transcriptome evolution) is absent. These are addressable, but they go to the conceptual core of the paper.

---

## Major concerns

### 1. The Ka/Ks analogy is structurally inverted: the CKI denominator is a constrained class, not a neutral standard

The manuscript's own premises defeat the analogy it is branded on. The Introduction states that Ka/Ks works by "using synonymous sites as an internal baseline so that the ratio reveals selection" (MS, Introduction), and the Discussion states the CKI assumption that "housekeeping (HK) genes are under stabilizing selection that constrains their expression variance across conditions, making them a practical constrained baseline" (MS, Discussion). These two sentences cannot both ground the same analogy. In dN/dS, Ks estimates the *neutral* substitution rate — the rate of change when selection is absent — and the shared locus mutation rate cancels in the ratio. In CKI, k_n is the divergence of the gene class under the *strongest stabilizing constraint*. CKI therefore computes (divergence of a high-variance, difference-selected gene set) / (divergence of the most constrained gene set). In evolutionary terms this is not Ka/Ks; it is closer to a McDonald–Kreitman-type contrast between a putatively selected class and a constrained reference class — and the MK framework's known pathology applies directly: when the reference class is itself under constraint, the ratio cannot distinguish positive selection on the numerator class from *relaxed constraint*, and it cannot distinguish constraint on the numerator class from *elevated divergence of the reference*. The manuscript's own results demonstrate both failure modes (TCGA: NN/TT "reversal" driven entirely by elevated tumor k_n; IFN-β: ω collapse in CD14+ monocytes driven by k_n inflation), yet the title, Abstract, and Fig. 1a continue to market the Ka/Ks framing. The disclosure in MS ("While CKI does not share Ka/Ks's formal mathematical properties (notably the shared mutation rate that cancels in the ratio; see Discussion)") is welcome but understates the problem: the issue is not only the missing rate cancellation, it is that the denominator measures the *opposite* evolutionary regime (constraint, not neutrality). At minimum the framing must be recast as MK-like, and the title reconsidered.

### 2. ω has no neutral point, no cancellable units, and no unambiguous direction — yet "conservation" is never defined

Three distinct interpretive defects compound:

- **No neutral anchor value.** In Ka/Ks, ω = 1 is the neutral expectation. Here, identical populations yield ω ≈ 7.70 (95% CI [7.37, 8.02]), and the neutral point itself is dataset-dependent (brain-internal 9.73 [9.03, 10.53]). Nothing biological attaches to ω > 1, yet Fig. 1a places "ω = Ka/Ks > 1 indicates positive selection" immediately adjacent to the CKI ratio, and the Abstract never states that the CKI neutral point is ~7.7, not 1. A reader skimming the paper will import the dN/dS number line wholesale.
- **No cancellable units.** Ka and Ks are per-site substitution rates with identical units on the same locus. k_n and k_f are bounded JS divergences ([0,1]) computed on gene sets of different sizes (~1,130 vs 200) selected by different rules; Supplementary Note 15 concedes the dimensionality check used random Dirichlet vectors and "does not simulate the variance-based gene selection mechanism that generates the ω inflation." The ratio cancels nothing; the empirical baseline absorbs the mismatch, which is calibration, not normalization.
- **Direction is ambiguous.** MS states "values far below indicate strong functional constraint" (Introduction), and Table 1 labels low ω as "most conserved." But the manuscript itself shows low ω also arises from an *inflated* denominator: tumors are "less divergent" because TT k_n is elevated 1.3–3.3-fold; CD14+ monocyte ω collapses because stimulation raises k_n 5.7-fold. "Strong functional constraint" and "housekeeping dysregulation" are opposite biological readings of the same low value. Nowhere is "conservation of a cell state" operationally defined. The incoherence surfaces inside the cross-organ analysis itself: "NK cells rank most divergent under ω yet second-most conserved under k_f among well-sampled types" (Supplementary Note 9). Under Table 1's label, NK cells are simultaneously the least- and second-most-conserved well-sampled type. A metric whose direction requires a per-analysis component audit cannot headline a "conservation ranking" without that audit being part of the headline.

### 3. The v49.9 Scope concession is fatal to Table 1 as written

This is the sharpest internal contradiction in the revision. The Discussion now states: "Because the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific—housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types—the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type, so limited cross-type discrimination of ω is expected by design" (MS, Discussion, Scope paragraph). But Table 1 ("Cross-organ conservation ranking by cell type ... ranked first by mean ω") ranks *different cell types against each other* by a quantity whose denominator, by the authors' own concession, is not comparable across cell types. Each individual ω is a within-type cross-organ comparison (in scope); the *ranking of the means across types* is a cross-type comparison of k_n-normalized values (out of scope by the authors' own rule). Worse, the authors' controls show the ranking is not merely uncalibrated but denominator-dominated: Supplementary Note 9 reports cell-type-level ρ(ω, k_f) = 0.23 with organ-clustered bootstrap 95% CI [−0.08, +0.38] (spanning zero) versus ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.31 with CI [−0.61, −0.17] (excluding zero), concluding "the interval estimates support a denominator-driven ordering"; among the well-sampled types that anchor interpretation, ω–k_f agreement is r = 0.10 (P = 0.87). So Table 1 is, to first order, a cross-cell-type ranking of 1/k_n — precisely the quantity declared non-comparable across cell types two sections later. The brain analysis proves the authors know the remedy: they computed class-specific split-half baselines (range 7.58–12.52, a 1.65-fold spread) and re-expressed the gradient under per-class calibration (6.10 → 5.99). No equivalent per-type calibration exists for the 17 Tabula Sapiens types. Until Table 1 is recomputed under per-cell-type baselines — or withdrawn in favor of within-type statements — the "cross-organ conservation ranking" should be treated as an artifact of baseline heterogeneity. "Expected by design" does not rescue an out-of-scope analysis; it indicts it.

### 4. Missing selection-metric and expression-evolution literature; loose population-genetic terminology

For a paper titled "Ka/Ks-inspired," the population-genetic grounding is two citations (Nei & Gojobori 1986; Yang 2007). Missing and directly relevant:

- **McDonald & Kreitman (1991)** and the neutrality-index literature (Rand & Kann 1996; Fay, Wyckoff & Wu 2001; Eyre-Walker 2006) — the correct framework for ratio-of-divergences across gene classes, including the known sensitivity to constraint in the reference class (Major 1).
- **Neutral models of transcriptome evolution** — Khaitovich et al. (2004) "neutral model of transcriptome evolution"; Gilad et al. (2006); Bedford & Hartl (2009); Brawand et al. (2011), whose cross-organ expression-conservation analysis is the direct antecedent of Table 1; Necsulea & Kaessmann (2014). The manuscript's central question — which cell types conserve expression programs across environments — is a studied problem in expression evolution and should be situated in it.
- Codon-model foundations (Goldman & Yang 1994; Yang & Nielsen 2000) if the dN/dS framing is retained at all.

Terminology is used loosely throughout: "functional adaptation rather than neutral drift" (Introduction) — expression noise is not genetic drift, and expression change is not adaptation; "stabilizing selection" and "functional constraint" are asserted for expression variance without citation to the expression-selection literature that actually tests these hypotheses. Either ground these terms or replace them with the neutral descriptive vocabulary the analyses support ("baseline noise," "state change").

## Minor concerns

1. **MS/SI cross-reference error:** Supplementary Note 9 refers to "the cross-organ conservation ranking of cell types (Table 2 / Fig. 5)" — the main text has Table 1, no Table 2.
2. **Filter inconsistency:** SI §1.6 states pseudobulks use "a minimum of 10 cells per group," whereas MS Methods state "at least 20 cells per entry (pseudobulk group); at least one donor/mouse contributing at least 10 cells." Which minimum applies where?
3. **LUAD k_n P-value discrepancy:** MS reports the WT > KRAS k_n contrast at Dunn–Holm P = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴; Supplementary Note 9 quotes "k_n P = 3.4 × 10⁻⁴" without specifying the test. Clarify whether these are different tests (omnibus vs pairwise) or a transcription error.
4. **Abstract over-cleans the simulation result:** "false-positive rate 0.00" omits the pooled-neutral 2/250 (0.8%) and the 2% under global overdispersion, disclosed only later. Given the paper's otherwise exemplary candor, state "0.00 under pure HK drift (0.8% pooled)" in the Abstract.
5. The well-sampled-type ω–k_f correlation (r = 0.10, P = 0.87; Supplementary Note 9) is the single most decision-relevant number for Table 1 and should appear in the main text beside the ranking, not only in the SI.
6. Table 1 reports SDs but no confidence intervals; SI §3.7 promises bootstrap CIs in Supplementary Table 2 for n ≥ 5 types — include them in Table 1 itself.
7. Fig. 1a should mark the empirical neutral point (~7.7) on the CKI side of the analogy, since Fig. 1e already concedes ω = 1 "is never observed in practice."
8. The symbol ω is the universal dN/dS symbol; the first-use disclaimer ("a heuristic index, not a formal measure of Darwinian selection") currently sits deep in the Introduction — move it to the Abstract or Fig. 1.
9. Supplementary Note 9's cross-organ control reproduces mean ω "to within 3.6e-15" — a reassuring replication detail, but the k_n-only ordering (reported for the brain) is not shown for Tabula Sapiens; add it so readers can see the denominator ranking that drives Table 1.

**MS–SI numerical consistency check (verified):** calibration baseline 7.70 [7.37, 8.02] (MS = SI §3.10/Note 3); brain grand mean 38.55, astrocytes 82.75, Bergmann/vascular 13.56, gradient 6.10 with size-balanced 1.74 [1.64, 1.84] and per-class 5.99 [4.43, 7.69] (MS = SI Notes 2/3); simulation AUCs 0.804 [0.777, 0.831] marrow / 0.908 skin (MS = SI Note 1 = CL); Kang 0/30 vs JS 36.7%, cosine 23.3% (MS = SI §3.12 = CL); brain T1 28.6% vs 35.7–45.2% (MS = SI §3.12 = CL); drift ladder 1.04→1.76→1.80 vs 1.07→3.32→2.98 (SI §3.12 = CL); TCGA NN/TT 1.10–2.46 with k_n elevation 1.3–3.3× (MS = SI §3.13 = CL); LUAD means 136.9/122.2/115.4 with n = 120/61/311 (MS = SI §3.13); TCGA pair tables 25,306 + 10,000 TN = 35,306 internally consistent with sample counts (3,567 = Σ cohorts). Table 1 counts sum correctly (5 well-sampled + 12 sparse types = 17; 43 + 16 = 59 pairs). No quantitative MS–SI contradictions beyond items 1–3 above.

## Questions to authors

1. Provide per-cell-type split-half baselines for all 17 Tabula Sapiens cell types in Table 1, exactly as done for the brain classes. Does the cross-organ ranking survive per-type ω_cal? If the baseline spread resembles the brain's 1.65-fold, the current ranking cannot stand.
2. Reconcile the Scope paragraph with Table 1: if "the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type," on what grounds do you compare mean ω across cell types at all? Please either withdraw the cross-type ranking or demonstrate empirically that HK-anchor behavior is homogeneous across the ranked types.
3. Define "conservation of a cell state" operationally. State explicitly in the Table 1 legend and Fig. 5 whether high or low ω denotes conservation, and qualify that low ω can reflect an elevated denominator (dysregulated anchor), as your own TCGA and IFN-β analyses show.
4. Why is the McDonald–Kreitman framework (and the neutrality-index literature) not discussed? It is the correct population-genetic analog for a ratio of divergences across two gene classes, and its known reference-class pathologies map one-to-one onto your anchor-visibility boundary.
5. What, biologically, does ω > 1 mean? If the answer is "nothing without dataset-specific calibration," why does Fig. 1 retain the Ka/Ks > 1 framing, and why is the ~7.7 neutral point absent from the Abstract?
6. k_f genes are selected per pair as the top-200 by |Δ| between the two compared pseudobulks. Doesn't this guarantee E[k_f] > E[k_n] under equivalence (your ω = 7.70) and make ω's elevation partially circular? Your permutation null tests label exchangeability under the same selection rule — please state in evolutionary terms what null hypothesis that corresponds to, and what alternative hypothesis a significant ω actually supports.
7. HRT Atlas HK status is defined across bulk tissues, not per cell type. Do the Tabula Sapiens cell types differ in within-type HK expression dispersion in ways that shift k_n? A per-type HK-variance audit would directly test the premise behind Major 3.

---

## Score: 4/10 (strict)

Solid engineering, exemplary statistical self-auditing, and honest reporting — but the evolutionary framing that brands the paper is structurally inverted (constrained, not neutral, denominator), the flagship cross-organ ranking contradicts the authors' own newly added Scope concession and is denominator-driven by their own controls, and the paper is disconnected from the selection-metric and expression-evolution literature it implicitly enters. These are conceptual, not cosmetic, problems; all are fixable.

## Recommendation: Major Revision

Required for acceptance: (i) recast the framing from Ka/Ks to an MK-like constrained-reference contrast, or explicitly delimit the analogy in title/Abstract/Fig. 1; (ii) recompute Table 1 under per-cell-type calibration or withdraw the cross-type ranking; (iii) define "conservation" and disambiguate ω's direction everywhere it is used as a headline; (iv) engage the McDonald–Kreitman and transcriptome-neutral-evolution literature.
