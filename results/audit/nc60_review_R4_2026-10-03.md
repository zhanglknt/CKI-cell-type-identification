## Summary

The manuscript presents CKI, a Ka/Ks-inspired ratio index ω = k_f/k_n that normalizes functional-gene divergence (identity genes) against a housekeeping baseline, and validates it across simulation, technical replicates (Kang PBMC; Siletti brain drift ladder), TCGA bulk RNA-seq, Tabula Sapiens, and the Siletti et al. human brain atlas. The brain-atlas component — my primary remit — comprises (i) a class-mean regional-divergence landscape over 10 non-neuronal superclusters (31,764 same-class cross-region pairs), (ii) a block-shuffle design-matched null with class-level tests, and (iii) a multiplicative-residual screen for anomalously similar (cell type, region pair) combinations (39 Strong candidates, none FDR-surviving). The statistical scaffolding is the strongest aspect of the paper: calibration constants, drift ladders, donor-stratified nulls, pseudo-region negative controls, small-cluster bootstrap corrections, and fixed-panel ablations are all present, internally consistent, and honestly reported. The biological yield of the brain analysis is deliberately modest and is disclosed as such.

Every number I spot-checked was verified against the current text (see "Verification log" below). The cover letter matches the manuscript's claims and numbers.

## Strengths

1. **The calibrated-null framework is a genuine methodological contribution for atlas-scale screens.** Recomputing the full Strong-candidate selection rule inside every block-shuffle permutation (expected count 148.3 vs 39 observed), reporting the permutation-resolution bound on FDR (minimum q = 0.520 is a statement about B = 1,000 resolution, disclosed explicitly), and adding the pseudo-region negative control (127,756 pseudo-pairs; near-nominal cross-origin tail rates 5.79%/6.87% vs the real 6.17%/7.90%) is exactly how atlas-scale pair screens should be calibrated. The same-origin 37.6% lower-tail rate demonstrates retained power. This is rigorous and rare.
2. **The drift ladder (T1/T2/T3) is well designed and honestly bounded.** Per-pair size-matched nulls, seven metrics, the marker-Jaccard caveat (lower FPR but weakest T3 response, 1.41 vs 1.80), the group-size FPR gradient (14.1% → 48.0%), and the gene-specific library effects in choroid plexus/Bergmann glia are all disclosed. Supplementary Table 11 reproduces every value quoted in the main text, figure legends, and cover letter.
3. **Confound handling in the brain analysis is exemplary.** Class-size confounding of k_n (ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043), equal-n downsampling, span-matched and combined span+size-matched controls (3.68 / 3.66, donor-bootstrapped), RNA-quality proxy regressions (gradient 6.33–6.45 after adjustment), and the donor-stratified null with an analytic identity-draw floor are all reported, including the results that go against the headline (size-only equal-n gradient 1.74 not donor-robust, CI [0.80, 2.34]).
4. **Writing integrity.** Bounded-scope statements are consistent across Abstract, Results, Discussion, and Limitations; the k_n-denominator dominance of the brain gradient is stated in the Results, the Figure 6 legend, and the Discussion. Terminology (ω, k_f, k_n, calibration, misreporting) is used consistently. The Discussion's practical decision rules (when to use CKI vs standard metrics vs k_f alone) are genuinely useful.
5. **Cover letter accuracy.** All quantitative claims in the CL verified: simulation FPR 0.00 vs 0.55–0.58; AUC 0.80; Kang 0/30 vs 36.7%/23.3%; brain T1 28.6% vs 35.7–45.2% (five continuous metrics); ladder 1.04 → 1.76 → 1.80 vs 1.07 → 3.32 → 2.98; "below raw JS in all ten cell classes"; 3,535 TCGA samples; KRAS retained / EGFR dissolved under purity adjustment; GTEx adjacent-normal at healthy level in lung, liver, breast. The CL's four pillars match the abstract's four pillars.

## Major comments

**M1. The abstract's and cover letter's fourth (brain) pillar overstates scope and omits the key caveats.** The abstract sentence — "In a human brain atlas, CKI quantified a regional differentiation gradient and provided a statistically calibrated framework for atlas-scale comparisons" — does not disclose that (i) the brain analysis is restricted to 10 non-neuronal supercluster classes (888,263 of ~3.3 million nuclei; neuronal subtypes, the large majority of the atlas, are excluded because `supercluster_term` does not resolve them); (ii) the gradient is dominated by the k_n denominator (endpoint contrast: k_n 3.2-fold vs k_f 2.0-fold; class-mean ω tracks k_n at ρ = −0.73 but not k_f at ρ = 0.09), so "regional differentiation gradient" reads to a neuroscience audience as a functional-specialization claim that the Results themselves explicitly decline to make ("it is k_n-dominated and does not by itself establish functional specialization"); and (iii) the candidate screen yields zero FDR-surviving pairs. The same wording is repeated as the CL's fourth pillar ("quantified a regional differentiation gradient and provides a statistically calibrated framework"). For a methods paper this restraint would be a selling point, not a weakness: I recommend the abstract/CL state the non-neuronal scope and the denominator-driven nature explicitly (one clause each), e.g. "across ten non-neuronal classes of a human brain atlas, CKI quantified a regional divergence gradient — predominantly a housekeeping-baseline effect — and provides…". Severity: **Major** (accuracy of the abstract/CL, the most-read text of the paper).

**M2. The "anti-enrichment" result (39 observed vs 148.3 expected Strong candidates; P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0) is presented in a way that will systematically mislead readers.** In the Results ("Anomalously similar pairs"), the sentence "the 39 observed lie 3.8-fold below expectation (P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0)" reads, to most readers, as a non-result (P = 1.0), when it is in fact the strongest global evidence in the paper that the observed landscape is *more* regionally structured than the design-matched null: breaking the cell–region association produces ~3.8× *more* anomalous-similarity candidates than are observed. Figure 6c's legend says "anti-enriched", but the Results paragraph never states the direction or interpretation in plain words, and never connects it to the complementary class-level finding (3 of 10 classes above the null after correction; microglia/oligodendrocytes/Bergmann glia at or below null). One to two sentences are needed, e.g.: "the depletion is itself the signal: under a null that destroys region structure, the Strong rule fires 3.8-fold more often, so regional assignment constrains cross-region similarity beyond chance (no individual pair survives correction)." Severity: **Major** (clarity of a central result; trivial to fix).

**M3. The brain section's biological narrative ends abruptly and under-integrates its own threads.** The residence/migration framework is introduced and rejected in a single sentence (OPCs second-highest at 40.62; OL-lineage fold 0.77, P = 0.92), and the one positive biological thread — the mature-oligodendrocyte thalamo-temporal orientation (9 of 10 candidates on the thalamic-relay/STH/TF axis vs 22.7% base rate; Note 14) and its relation to the dorsoventral developmental origin boundary (ref. 25, Foerster et al.) — is left at "corresponds … only at axis anatomy". Meanwhile Note 14's important structural caveat (tier membership and per-pair P-values are monotone functions of the same observed/null ω values, so tier-graded raw-P enrichment is partly guaranteed by construction) appears only in the SI. I am not asking for new analyses; I ask that (i) the one-line structural caveat from Note 14 be promoted into the main text where the tier-graded enrichment pattern could otherwise be over-read, and (ii) the Discussion explicitly frame the brain analysis as a *statistical stress-test and hypothesis-generating screen at atlas scale* (which the fourth paragraph nearly says) rather than leaving the biological framing implicit. Severity: **Major** (framing/integration; no new experiments required).

## Minor comments

1. **Cross-reference error (SI).** Supplementary Information Section 3.12 (Kang batch-1 paragraph): "the replication is shown in main-text Fig. 3d" — main-text Figure 3 has panels a–c only; the Kang replication is Fig. 3a. Severity: Minor.
2. **Package version inconsistency (SI).** SI pseudocode note (after Algorithm 1) and SI Section 3.8 both say "in the released cki package (v0.5.4)", while the main text (Methods "Package parity"; Code availability) and the Zenodo deposit are v0.5.5. Severity: Minor.
3. **Strong-candidate enumeration does not sum to 39.** The Results catalogue paragraph accounts for 16 microglia + 10 mature-oligodendrocyte + 3 astrocyte + 6 fibroblast + 2 ependymal = 37; the single OPC and single committed-OPC Strong candidates (both present in Supplementary Table 6) are not mentioned. Severity: Minor.
4. **Supplementary Fig. 9c legend percentages have an unstated denominator.** "Microglia: Strong 16 (0.96%)" uses the share of *threshold-passing candidates* per class (16/1,668 = 0.96%), not the share of all class pairs (16/5,671 = 0.28%). Verified consistent with Table 6, but the legend should state the denominator. Severity: Minor.
5. **Point-estimate/CI caliber pairing.** The span-matched control is quoted as "3.68 (donor-level bootstrap [1.67, 3.97])" and the combined control as "3.66 (donor-level bootstrap [1.92, 3.78])", but Supplementary Note 11 gives the donor-bootstrap *median* as 3.28 for both (3.68/3.66 are the observed ratio-of-means, carrying cell-resampling CIs [3.40, 4.95] / [3.60, 3.71]). Attaching a CI whose resampling median differs materially from the quoted point estimate deserves a half-sentence of clarification. Severity: Minor.
6. **SI Table of Contents lists Supplementary Tables in placement order, not numeric order** (1, 4, 5, 6, 2, 7–14, 3, 15–19). If intentional (placement order within the SI), a one-line note would help navigation; the renumbering-by-first-citation itself is correct (verified: ST1 cited before ST2/ST3 before ST4 before ST5 before ST6 in the main text). Severity: Minor.
7. **Microglia supercluster sanity check: calibrate reader expectations.** Separating microglia from CNS macrophages (two genuinely distinct cell types) at AUC = 1.00 is a low bar for a method that explicitly disclaims classification; the informative content is the k_f-carried margin (0.064 vs 0.0020) against a neutral half-split baseline near 1 (1.30) on unused data, plus the disclosure that standard metrics also reach AUC 1.00. The main text frames it as a "sanity check", which is right, but one clause stating *what* is being sanity-checked (decomposition behaviour on independent data, not separability per se) would prevent over-reading. Severity: Minor.
8. **Terminology drift for the FPR concept.** The abstract uses "false-report rate", the Results use "misreporting", and Figure 3 uses "false-positive rate/FPR" for the same statistic. Unify (suggest "false-positive rate" throughout, with one definition). Severity: Minor.
9. **Bergmann-glia endpoint fragility is disclosed in the SI but not the main text.** Supplementary Table 19 shows the lowest-ω position at the reference threshold is a near-tie (Bergmann glia 13.555 vs vascular 13.559) that flips with the min-cells threshold, and Strong counts vary 22–39 across thresholds. The main text's "(both 13.56)" handles the tie gracefully, but one parenthetical pointing to Table 19 would make the endpoint's robustness status visible without the SI. Severity: Minor.
