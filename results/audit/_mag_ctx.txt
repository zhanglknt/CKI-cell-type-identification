To ask whether the ratio resolves biologically distinct tumor states, we stratified LUAD tumors by driver mutation (EGFR-mutant n = 61, KRAS-mutant n = 120, wild-type n = 311—wild-type for EGFR and KRAS, since tumors carrying other drivers were not separately excluded; double mutants, n = 2, excluded) using per-tumor ω (the mean over all TT pairs in which a tumor participates, including pairs with tumors of other driver classes, so the group means are not formed from disjoint pair sets; Fig. 5b). KRAS-mutant tumors showed the highest tissue-level divergence (mean ω = 136.9), exceeding wild-type (115.4; Kruskal-Wallis across groups P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷; Dunn–Holm P = 3.6 × 10⁻⁷) and EGFR-mutant tumors (122.2; Dunn–Holm P = 0.008), while EGFR-mutant and wild-type tumors did not differ significantly (Dunn–Holm P = 0.39; bootstrap difference 6.8, 95% CI −2.1 to 15.2). The k_f/k_n decomposition separated the two drivers’ associations mechanistically (Fig. 5c,d): the KRAS contrast carried a functional component (KRAS versus EGFR under k_f alone, Dunn–Holm P = 0.015; KRAS−WT k_f difference 0.011, 95% CI 0.002–0.019) accompanied by a lower k_n (WT > KRAS, Dunn–Holm P = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴), both of which raise the ratio. The elevation was carried predominantly by the lower baseline (k_n contributed ~84% of the log-ω gap; the unadjusted k_f contrast was non-significant, Dunn P = 0.097). Because driver classes could differ in tumor purity, we scored every tumor with the ESTIMATE stromal and immune signatures and used the combined admixture score as a covariate (Methods): admixture differed across driver groups, highest in EGFR-mutant tumors (Kruskal-Wallis P = 0.003), and after admixture adjustment the KRAS–wild-type difference remained significant for both components (Δω +16.8, P = 4.0 × 10⁻⁶; Δk_f +0.012, P = 0.009; Δk_n −0.0004, P = 0.003), whereas EGFR-mutant tumors no longer differed from wild-type in any metric (all P > 0.4)—the apparent EGFR elevation is attributable to stromal/immune admixture rather than to a baseline shift. KRAS-mutant tumors were strongly enriched for ever-smokers (94% versus 63% in EGFR-mutant and 86% in wild-type tumors, χ² = 30.3, P = 2.7 × 10⁻⁷), yet adjusting for smoking status—alone or jointly with age, sex, and admixture—left the KRAS–wild-type differences essentially unchanged (Δω +13.9, P = 5.9 × 10⁻⁴ with smoking alone; +13.6, P = 3.3 × 10⁻⁴ in the joint model, with both components likewise retained; Section 3.13 of the Supplementary Information). That a single bulk-level statistic, read through its components, distinguishes a functionally divergent driver class (KRAS, robust to purity and smoking adjustment) from an apparent association explained by admixture (EGFR) is precisely the decomposition CKI is designed to provide—although these remain observational associations: the admixture score is an ESTIMATE-style proxy rather than a consensus-purity estimate (it is monotonically equivalent to published ESTIMATE purity, so regression-adjusted conclusions are unaffected), and pack-years data were too sparse to model (smoking status covered 427 of 492 tumors, 87%). Within-cancer-type stratification analyses beyond LUAD (LIHC Edmondson grade, BRCA PAM50) are reported as denominator-dominated vignettes in the Supplementary Information (Supplementary Note 9; Supplementary Fig. 4b), because both orderings reverse under k_f alone.
