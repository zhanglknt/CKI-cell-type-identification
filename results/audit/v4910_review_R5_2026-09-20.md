# Reviewer 5 (R5) — Neuroscience single-cell atlas expert
## Blind review of v49.10 (Nature Communications, Methodology) — Brain application focus
Date: 2026-09-20

---

## Summary

The manuscript introduces CKI, a Ka/Ks-inspired ratio ω = k_f/k_n that normalizes identity-gene divergence by a housekeeping-gene baseline, and applies it to the Siletti et al. human brain single-nucleus atlas (888,263 non-neuronal nuclei, 10 classes, 31,764 same-cell-type cross-region pairs). This revision responds to three prior-round criticisms: (i) the headline gradient now leads with the size-balanced 1.74-fold estimate in the Abstract; (ii) the candidate enumeration now includes the OPC lineage and sums to 39; (iii) the screen paragraph now discloses donor structure. I verified the enumeration fix as fully substantive (arithmetic closes; consistent across MS, SI, and Fig. 8). The other two fixes are partially substantive: the headline correction is not carried "throughout" as claimed, and the donor-confounding fix is a disclosure sentence rather than the required screen-level analysis. The manuscript remains unusually self-critical — the 0-FDR verdict, the anti-enrichment of the Strong rule (39 observed vs 148.3 expected), and the k_n-dominated decomposition are all stated plainly — and the pseudo-region negative control (Supplementary Note 12) is a genuinely well-designed calibration check. The residual problems are concentrated where the biology is: the cross-class ω ranking is largely a class-size/denominator artifact by the authors' own controls, and the pair-level catalogue still rests on a null that crosses donor boundaries while 94.5% of observed pairs share a donor.

## Major concerns

### M1. The headline fix is incomplete: 6.10 still leads in Results, Discussion, and Fig. 6, contradicting the "throughout" claim

The Abstract now reads "Brain analysis revealed a 1.74-fold size-balanced regional gradient ([1.64, 1.84]; 6.10-fold uncorrected)" and the Introduction matches ("quantifying a regional differentiation gradient (1.74-fold size-balanced; 6.10-fold uncorrected full-data estimate)"). This is an improvement. However:

- The Results paragraph still opens with the uncorrected number: "Mean ω spanned a 6.10-fold gradient across cell classes" — with 1.74 arriving only mid-paragraph as an attenuation.
- The Discussion opens: "The brain analysis showed a 6.10-fold ω gradient across 10 cell classes (from 13.56 in Bergmann glia and vascular cells to 82.75 in astrocytes...)" — no 1.74 co-report anywhere in that sentence; the only corrected value co-reported there is the class-baseline 5.99.
- The Fig. 6 legend likewise leads with 6.10 ("a 6.10-fold gradient that is an uncorrected upper bound inflated by class-size imbalance (equal-n estimate 1.74-fold, 95% CI [1.64, 1.84])").

Most problematically, the Results text claims "we co-report 6.10-fold as the full-data estimate and 1.74-fold as the size-balanced estimate throughout" — this is contradicted by the Discussion sentence above. A claim of consistency that the manuscript itself violates is worse than no claim. Given that ~70% of the magnitude is class-size imbalance (authors' own figure: "the gradient falls from 6.10-fold to 1.74 (95% CI [1.64, 1.84])—direction preserved, interval excluding 1, but ~70% of the magnitude reflects class-size imbalance"), 1.74 must be the primary claim in every section, with 6.10 clearly labeled as an uncorrected sensitivity estimate. As written, a reader of the Discussion alone would take away 6.10.

On whether the equal-n CI is itself a meaningful finding: yes, partially — the interval [1.64, 1.84] excludes 1, so the astrocyte-versus-Bergmann-glia direction survives size balancing. But it is a weak and narrow finding: (a) it is a percentile interval over 20 downsampling replicates ("1.74 ± 0.07 (mean ± SD over 20 replicates; 95% percentile interval [1.64, 1.84]", Supplementary Note 10) — it captures only subsampling variability of nuclei, not region clustering, not donor structure, and not library-count imbalance; (b) the class ω ranking does not reproduce under equal-n (ρ = 0.370, P = 0.29), so only the two endpoint classes survive, and one endpoint (Bergmann glia) is "all intra-cerebellar, so their low ω partly reflects this anatomical restriction" — an acknowledged confound that persists in the equal-n contrast; (c) the residual 1.74 is not decomposed: is it k_f or k_n? Given the authors' own finding that the full-data gradient "chiefly reflects housekeeping stability across regions, not functional-gene divergence", the equal-n residual could be equally denominator-driven. Requested: equal-n gradient under k_f-only, and a region-clustered studentized bootstrap-t CI (the machinery of Supplementary Note 2) for the 1.74 estimate.

### M2. Class-level ω ranking is not interpretable as functional divergence, and the main text understates its own evidence for this

The size–confound chain is now documented, but inconsistently reported:

- MS quotes only the weaker statistic: "Class-mean k_n correlates negatively with log10(class size) (Spearman ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043)". Supplementary Note 10 additionally reports "Pearson r = −0.850 (P = 0.0018)" — the stronger, more damning association, which belongs in the main text.
- Equal-n abolishes the k_n ordering outright ("the k_n class ranking is uncorrelated between full and downsampled data (ρ = −0.055, P = 0.88)") and leaves the ω ordering non-reproducible ("the ω ranking only weakly (ρ = 0.370, P = 0.29)").
- Class-mean ω tracks k_n (ρ = −0.73, P = 0.02) but not k_f (ρ = 0.09, P = 0.80), and under k_f-only "astrocytes rank third of ten (k_f-only gradient 4.1-fold)".

Taken together, the cross-class ω ranking is, to first order, a class-size artifact transmitted through the denominator. The authors concede this ("the gradient should not be read as a ranking of functional divergence"; "We therefore recommend treating class-level ω as composite and comparing k_f and k_n directly for mechanistic attribution") — yet the section is titled "Brain regional analysis reveals divergence gradients", and the Abstract's single brain sentence is the gradient. By the paper's own decision rule — "the honest default for ordering claims is k_f plus a design-matched null" — the brain class ordering should be reported primarily under k_f-only (with its design-matched null), with the composite ω ordering as a secondary descriptive. The current arrangement inverts the paper's own stated best practice at exactly the analysis that headlines the brain section.

A related unexamined confound: equal-n balances nuclei (4,118 per class) but not libraries. Class k_n is computed on pseudobulks aggregated across 10x libraries, and the drift ladder shows a real library-level technical component that "grows detectable with power and that the k_n denominator absorbs only partially". Astrocytes and oligodendrocytes draw on far more libraries than committed OPCs or Bergmann glia; residual library-count imbalance may survive inside the 1.74. Also, downsampling astrocytes to 4,118 nuclei across 108 regions averages ~38 nuclei per region, which will drop many (region, class) groups below the ≥ 20-nuclei filter — meaning the equal-n astrocyte mean is computed over a different region set than the full-data mean. Neither the changed region coverage nor per-class library counts at equal-n are reported.

### M3. The donor-confounding fix is a disclosure, not an analysis; the screen-level donor-stratified null is still missing

The added sentence is accurate and welcome: "the catalogue also inherits the atlas's donor structure (four donors; 94.5% of region pairs share at least one), leaving pair-level nominations subject to donor confounding". But a disclosure sentence cannot substitute for the analysis, because:

1. The donor-stratified shuffle exists in the Methods only for the class-level tests: "To ask whether the class-level significance of the pooled block-shuffle null survives when library-to-region assignments cannot cross donor boundaries, we re-ran the permutation test with a donor-stratified shuffle". The 31,764-pair multiplicative residual screen — the analysis that produces the 39 Strong candidates, the 148.3 expected-null count, and every per-pair q-value — still uses the free shuffle, which permutes libraries across regions irrespective of donor.
2. The bias direction is systematic, not random: the drift ladder shows donor differences raise divergence (T2 firing 90.9%, calibration 1.76), so observed donor-sharing pairs have lower ω than the donor-mixed null pairs on average. The free null therefore overestimates expected ω precisely for the pairs the screen nominates, biasing the lower tail toward false "anomalously similar" candidates. The marginal lower-tail excess (6.17% vs 5% nominal) is small, and the pseudo-region control (cross-origin pseudo-pairs: 5.79% lower vs 6.87% upper) shows the null is not grossly miscalibrated in aggregate — but marginal calibration does not certify individual nominations, and the pseudo-region split preserves within-region donor structure by construction, so it cannot rule out donor-driven pair-level nominations either.
3. The authors already possess the machinery (the class-level donor-stratified null was run at B = 1,000 with identical pipeline). Running the same donor-constrained shuffle for the residual screen — recomputing the expected Strong count (vs 148.3) and the per-pair q-value distribution — is the direct test of whether the catalogue survives. It is not run.

Additional missing artifact: no donor × region coverage table exists anywhere in the Supplementary Information (the TOC lists Tables 1–19; none is a coverage table; Note 11 is a region glossary). Given "median top-donor share within a region: 0.61" and four donors, the reader cannot assess which regions or candidate endpoints are effectively single-donor. Required: (a) a donor × region coverage table per class (donors per region, top-donor share, libraries per donor); (b) the donor-stratified screen null; (c) donor composition of the 39 Strong candidates (how many are same-donor pairs; e.g., are the A14–Pul / A14–LG microglial nominations drawn from one donor?).

On the question of whether any same-cell-type cross-region comparison can be clean with this design: strictly, no. With 94.5% of region pairs sharing ≥ 1 donor and a median top-donor share of 0.61, every pooled pair-level comparison is donor-entangled; same-donor restriction removes between-donor variance but leaves region effects unidentifiable from donor idiosyncrasies at n = 4 donors. This does not invalidate the class-level gradient tests (the within-donor analysis preserving a 4.50-fold gradient is reassuring there), but it means the pair-level catalogue can at best be repositioned as "donor-confounded nominations prioritized for designs that can separate donor from region" — a stronger downgrade than the current single clause.

### M4. Enumeration fix: verified and substantive

The prior omission is repaired. The Results text now reads: "Candidates concentrate in microglia (16 Strong) and oligodendrocytes (10), with fibroblasts (6), astrocytes (3), ependymal cells (2), and the oligodendrocyte precursor lineage (one committed OPC, one OPC) accounting for the remainder (39 in total); vascular cells, choroid plexus, and Bergmann glia contributed none." Arithmetic: 16 + 10 + 6 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 39. ✓

Cross-checks all agree:
- "Microglia contributed the largest share (16 of 39, 13 with raw P < 0.05)" — consistent, and the class-level paradox (microglia mean below null, P = 0.904, yet most Strong candidates) is explicitly reconciled via the rule's bias toward low-ω classes ("52.0 of the 148.3 null candidates are microglial; observed 16, fold 0.31, P = 0.990").
- Oligodendrocyte lineage 12 of 39 (= 10 mature + 1 committed OPC + 1 OPC): consistent with "the oligodendrocyte lineage is not enriched (fold 0.77, P = 0.92)" in the Results and with the Supplementary Fig. 8 inset legend: "OL-lineage representation among Strong candidates — 12 of 39 Strong, fold = 0.77, hypergeometric P = 0.92 (no enrichment)". ✓
- Class sizes in the Methods (astrocytes 155,025; oligodendrocytes 490,246; OPCs 105,723; committed OPCs 4,118; microglia 91,826; vascular 9,586; fibroblasts 8,897; ependymal 5,779; choroid plexus 7,643; Bergmann glia 7,965) sum to 886,808, matching the stated filtered total. ✓
- Tier counts: 39 + 1,171 + 5,381 + 25,173 unclassified = 31,764. ✓

This fix is substantive and internally consistent.

### M5. A numerical/listing inconsistency in the threshold sweep

The Results state: "thresholds of 10, 20 (reported), 50, and 100 nuclei retain 10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12" — four retention counts are given but only three gradient values. One gradient (presumably at the 50-nuclei threshold) is missing. Supplementary Table 19 is cited but its per-threshold values are not recoverable from the provided text. This must be reconciled; as written the sentence cannot be parsed.

### M6. Biological interpretation: hedging is mostly adequate, with two residual over-reaches

The 0-FDR verdict is stated first ("no candidate survives FDR correction (minimum q = 0.520)"), the catalogue is called "a prioritized hypothesis list, not discoveries", the microglial concentration is explicitly not claimed as composition ("we make no class-composition claim"), and the thalamo-temporal axis tests are labeled post hoc with the mathematical coupling between tier rule and per-pair P-values disclosed in Supplementary Note 13. The astrocyte result is properly decomposed ("under a k_f-only ordering astrocytes rank third of ten (k_f-only gradient 4.1-fold)... the gradient chiefly reflects housekeeping stability across regions, not functional-gene divergence"). This is better hedged than most exploratory catalogues.

Two residual issues:
1. The microglial LG/Pul/TF and oligodendrocyte thalamo-temporal narratives each occupy full paragraphs with literature anchoring (Foerster et al. dorsoventral origins; compartmentalized astrogenesis) despite zero FDR-significant candidates. The anchoring is consistently labeled ("literature anchoring rather than independent validation"; "the anatomical concentration itself is the observation to be explained"), but the sheer textual weight creates an availability bias toward these specific region pairs. Given M3 (donor confounding untested at the pair level) and the rule's demonstrated bias toward low-ω classes, these paragraphs should be compressed and explicitly flagged as donor-confounded until the donor-stratified screen null is run.
2. The set-level thalamo-temporal permutation tests "assume independent draws and are reported for reference only" (endpoints shared: MG in three pairs, TF in four) — properly caveated in Note 13, but the main-text Discussion cites the concentration as "concentrates on the thalamo-temporal axis rather than being scattered across the 108 regions" without repeating the shared-endpoint caveat at the point of use.

## Minor concerns

1. Abstract wording: "Brain analysis revealed a 1.74-fold size-balanced regional gradient" — "regional gradient" invites misreading as a spatial gradient; it is a cross-class gradient of mean cross-region ω. One clause ("across non-neuronal cell classes") would fix this. The composite, k_n-dominated nature of the gradient is also absent from the Abstract's only brain sentence, despite being the paper's own central finding about it.
2. The fixed-panel ablation paragraph states "vascular cells reached significance (P = 0.035)" without restating in the same sentence that the primary null gives P = 0.115 (non-significant); the SI discloses this ("vascular cells reached significance (P = 0.035, versus 0.115 reported, non-significant under the primary null)") but the MS phrasing "largely robust" lets an inference flip read as robustness. Mirror the SI caveat in the main text.
3. The within-donor gradient (4.50-fold) is itself an uncorrected class-size comparison (same caveat as the 6.10), and its Bergmann-glia endpoint rests on 10 within-donor pairs (Methods: "Bergmann glia: 10 pairs"). State both fragilities at the point of claim.
4. "P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0" — state explicitly that all 1,000 permutations exceeded 39, so this is a saturated empirical P, not an artifact.
5. MS reports the Spearman ρ = −0.648 (P = 0.043) for the k_n–size association only; the SI's Pearson r = −0.850 (P = 0.0018) should be co-reported in the main text (see M2).
6. The equal-n target (4,118 = committed OPC class size) should be accompanied by per-class surviving region counts at equal n, since proportional downsampling changes which (region, class) groups pass the ≥ 20-nuclei filter.
7. MS/SI number cross-checks otherwise pass: equal-n statistics (6.10 → 1.74 ± 0.07, [1.64, 1.84]); expected null 148.3 vs 39 (3.8-fold depletion); tier raw-P rates (31/39 = 79.5%, 417/1,171 = 35.6%, 909/5,381 = 16.9%, 603/25,173 = 2.4% vs 6.2% overall); lower/upper tail counts (1,960 = 6.2%; 2,510 = 7.9%); pseudo-region rates (5.79%/6.87% vs 6.17%/7.90%; same-origin 37.6% = 263/700); brain-internal baseline 9.73 [9.03, 10.53]; class-specific baselines and studentized intervals ([5.76, 28.59] Bergmann glia; 5.99 [4.43, 7.69] gradient); donor-stratified class-level null leaving "the same 3 of 10 classes surviving correction" with disclosed Monte-Carlo discrepancies (ependymal 0.058 vs 0.075; committed OPC 0.004 vs 0.005) — all consistent between MS and SI.

## Questions to authors

1. Donor-stratified screen: re-run the multiplicative residual screen under the donor-stratified block shuffle (the class-level machinery already exists). What is the expected Strong count (vs 148.3 under the free shuffle), and what is the minimum per-pair q? How many of the 39 Strong candidates are same-donor pairs, and what is the donor composition of the microglial A14–Pul / A14–LG / Cla–LG and oligodendrocyte LP–TF / STH–TF nominations?
2. Donor × region coverage: provide a per-class table of donors per region, top-donor share, and libraries per donor, so readers can identify effectively single-donor regions and candidate endpoints.
3. Equal-n diagnostics: report the equal-n gradient under k_f-only; provide a region-clustered (studentized bootstrap-t) CI for the 1.74 estimate; report per-class region counts and library counts surviving equal-n downsampling.
4. Threshold sweep: supply the missing gradient value for the 50-nuclei threshold ("10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12") and reconcile with Supplementary Table 19.
5. Headline consistency: justify retaining 6.10 as the leading figure in the Results, Discussion, and Fig. 6 given your own statement that the ordering "should not be read as a ranking of functional divergence" and that "~70% of the magnitude reflects class-size imbalance".
6. Given that class-mean ω tracks k_n (ρ = −0.73) and the k_f-only class ordering differs (astrocytes third), would you report the brain class ordering under k_f plus the design-matched null as the primary result, per your own recommended default?

## Recommendation

**Major Revision**

Rationale: the enumeration fix (prior issue ii) is fully substantive and verified; the headline fix (i) and donor fix (iii) are partially substantive — the 1.74-first ordering is not carried into Results/Discussion/Fig. 6 despite an explicit "throughout" claim, and the donor disclosure is not backed by the screen-level donor-stratified null or a donor × region coverage table, both of which are feasible with the authors' existing machinery and are decisive for the manuscript's main hypothesis-generating deliverable. The equal-n control's own uncertainty ([1.64, 1.84]) is real but under-propagated (no region clustering, no donor structure, no k_f decomposition, changed region coverage at equal n). None of these gaps requires new data; all are computable from the existing pipeline, which is why they should be required rather than requested.
