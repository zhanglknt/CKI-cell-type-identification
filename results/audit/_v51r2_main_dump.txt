===== Introduction =====
[01][ 42w][ ] Comparing two cell populations is among the most common tasks in single-cell analysis. Researchers typically choose a standard metric: Euclidean distance, cosine similarity, Pearson or Spearman correlation, or Jensen-Shannon divergence. These metrics are convenient, but they treat all gene expression differences equally.
[02][ 93w][ ] Not all expression changes have the same biological meaning: a twofold change in GAPDH may reflect technical noise, whereas a twofold change in a transcription factor may reflect a functional state shift, and standard metrics cannot tell these apart. The problem is acute in large single-cell atlases 1, where donor- and batch-level variation often dominates over cell-type identity. Methods such as Harmony 2, scVI 3, and SATURN 4 remove such nuisance variation 5, but a key question remains: how much of the difference between two populations reflects functional change rather than neutral drift?
[03][ 70w][ ] This question mirrors one addressed in molecular evolution. The Ka/Ks ratio (dN/dS) distinguishes nonsynonymous changes (Ka, which alter the protein) from synonymous changes (Ks, largely silent) 6,7, using synonymous sites as an internal baseline so that the ratio reveals selection. While CKI does not share Ka/Ks’s formal mathematical properties (notably the shared mutation rate that cancels in the ratio; see Discussion), it adopts an analogous heuristic logic for transcriptomic comparisons.
[04][121w][ ] CKI defines two rates: a baseline divergence rate k_n, estimated from housekeeping (HK) gene expression, and a functional divergence rate k_f, estimated from cell-type identity genes; the ratio ω = k_f/k_n quantifies baseline-normalized functional divergence (Fig. 1). The scope of ω is bounded by one central assumption, quantified empirically throughout: the HK anchor is valid only where housekeeping expression remains constrained. Functional signal on HK genes is invisible to ω by construction, and in disease or strong-perturbation contexts the anchor itself shifts (TCGA and IFN-β below; Supplementary Note 1), inflating the denominator and deflating the ratio; in such regimes ω must be read alongside its components, and k_f with a design-matched null is the honest default for ordering claims (Results; Discussion).
[05][182w][ ] Here, we show that CKI provides a baseline-normalized index of cell-state divergence across five scales. First, random splits of the same mouse cell population (Tabula Muris 8) yield ω above 1 (empirical baseline 7.70, 95% CI [7.37, 8.02]). Second, in Tabula Sapiens human data 9, ω is negatively correlated with all four standard distance metrics—partly a denominator effect (Results). Third, on real technical replicates (30 cross-lane PBMC pairs; 2,161 brain library pairs), ω shows the lowest drift misreporting among continuous metrics while retaining sensitivity to genuine biology. Fourth, on TCGA data 10,11 (five cancer types, 3,567 samples), CKI uncovers a consistent pan-cancer reversal—tumors less divergent than adjacent non-tumor tissue, attributable to an elevated housekeeping baseline—and resolves lung-adenocarcinoma driver classes: KRAS-mutant tumors retain both functional (k_f) and baseline (k_n) divergence after purity and smoking adjustment, while the apparent EGFR association dissolves. Fifth, in a human brain atlas 12, we quantify a regional differentiation gradient (1.74-fold size-balanced) and screen for anomalously similar cell-type/region pairs; no candidate survives FDR correction, so the screen is a bounded, hypothesis-generating catalogue. Ground-truth simulations complement all five analyses throughout.
===== Results =====
[06][  3w][H] Decomposing transcriptomic variation
[07][ 36w][ ] CKI takes two cell populations, each a pseudobulk expression vector (mean expression across cells), and applies the same metric (Jensen–Shannon divergence) in three steps on the same matrix, so the ratio is internally calibrated (Fig. 1).
[08][ 48w][ ] Step 1: baseline divergence rate k_n: pseudobulk vectors restricted to housekeeping (HK) gene indices, +1 pseudo-count, L1 normalization; k_n is the JS divergence between the two HK-gene probability distributions. Because HK genes should not differ systematically between biologically equivalent populations 13, k_n captures baseline technical and physiological noise.
[09][ 57w][ ] Step 2: functional divergence rate k_f on cell-type identity genes: top-2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs; Seurat, HK excluded) for the Tabula Muris full pairwise matrix (Supplementary Fig. 1), and the pair’s top-200 differentially expressed genes (absolute mean difference, HK excluded) for all other analyses, with k_n computed per pair on the shared HK set (fixed-panel ablation, Results).
[10][ 51w][ ] Step 3: ω = k_f/k_n. Statistical inference uses permutation testing (B = 1,000 for all datasets): group labels are permuted (cell labels for mouse and human, sample labels for TCGA) or, for the brain, library-to-region assignments block-shuffled; empirical P-values, standardized effect sizes, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR are computed within each dataset (Methods).
[11][ 32w][ ] A parameter sweep on Tabula Muris (703 pairs, 6 organs) showed that adding pathway enrichment scores to k_f does not improve discrimination (identity-only AUC = 0.786; Supplementary Fig. 1, Supplementary Table 1).
[12][  4w][H] Calibration confirms baseline behavior
[13][ 51w][ ] We calibrated CKI on the Tabula Muris FACS dataset 8 (SmartSeq2, 15,057 cells, 22,308 genes, 6 organs), with housekeeping genes from HRT Atlas v1.0 13 (mouse ortholog column): top-2,000 HVGs for the full pairwise matrix (703 pairs, Supplementary Fig. 1), and the hybrid per-pair scheme for the pilot calibration (Fig. 2).
[14][ 68w][ ] Control comparisons randomly split the same population into halves across six FACS control populations (Fig. 2a): 50 split-half replicates per population (300 ω values) stabilized the baseline at mean ω = 7.70 (95% CI [7.37, 8.02]; legacy six-split estimate 6.67 consistent; leave-one-population-out range 6.75–8.08; Section 3.10). No control comparison reached significance (all P > 0.05, one-sided permutation test): CKI recognizes biologically equivalent populations as having no functional divergence.
[15][ 78w][ ] Beyond controls, ω increased monotonically with biological distance (Fig. 2b, c): same cell type across organs (mean ω = 21.31, n = 4) sat below different cell types within an organ (mean ω = 43.19, n = 3), with cross-organ cross-type comparisons highest (2–4 pairs per category, calibration-scale estimates). Component analysis confirmed k_f as the driver: from controls to inter-cell-type comparisons, k_f rose ~400-fold (0.0015 → 0.63–0.68) while k_n rose only 72–118-fold—CKI measures functional divergence, not total difference.
[16][ 65w][ ] Because the empirical baseline deviates from the theoretical ideal (ω = 1), we introduce ω_cal = ω_obs / 7.70. Split-half calibration inside each dataset shows the factor to be dataset-relative (brain atlas 9.73 [9.03, 10.53]; Tabula Sapiens 7.67 [7.39, 8.00], inside the mouse CI); class-specific baselines leave the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient essentially unchanged, and a ratio-estimator audit confirms the calibration absorbs ratio bias (Supplementary Notes 2–4).
[17][  7w][H] Correlation structure between CKI and standard metrics
[18][ 87w][ ] We extended CKI to the Tabula Sapiens human atlas 9 (108,136 cells; 99 filtered cell-type entries, 4,851 pairs across six organs), using the same hybrid scheme with HRT Atlas v1.0 HK genes 13 (human column) (Fig. 2d). Human ω ranged from 1.35 to 87.69 (mean 21.61, median 19.65); cross-dataset ω comparisons are rank-based only (Discussion). The biological hierarchy was preserved: same cell type across organs (mean ω = 15.83, n = 59) sat below different cell types within an organ (mean ω = 24.87, n = 1,038).
[19][100w][ ] We computed CKI ω and four standard metrics (raw JS divergence, Spearman distance, cosine distance, marker Jaccard distance) on all 4,851 human cell-type pairs. ω correlated negatively with all four (Spearman r = −0.36 to −0.46, all P < 10⁻¹⁴⁵), the four forming a tight positive cluster (pairwise r = 0.57–0.93). The negative sign is partly a ratio artifact: k_f correlated positively with the standard metrics (r = +0.43 to +0.72) and k_n more strongly (r = +0.69 to +0.81), and conditional on k_n all four turned positive (partial r = +0.11 to +0.54, all P < 1 × 10⁻¹⁴).
[20][ 61w][ ] CKI was the only metric scoring same-organ different-cell-type pairs above different-organ different-cell-type pairs (Mann-Whitney P = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁸); all four standard metrics showed the opposite pattern. Decomposition tempers the functional reading: same-organ pairs have indistinguishable k_f (P = 0.60) but lower k_n (P = 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶)—a more stable housekeeping baseline within organs, not greater functional specialization (Supplementary Note 9).
[21][  5w][H] Ground-truth simulation: specificity versus sensitivity
[22][149w][ ] No real dataset provides pairs with known functional divergence, so we built a semi-synthetic ground truth by injecting perturbations into a real background (Tabula Muris FACS marrow B cells; true pre-injection divergence zero; Methods). Under pure neutral housekeeping drift, raw JS and cosine exceeded their null thresholds in 55% and 58% of replicates, ω in none (2% under global overdispersion); an injected functional module was detected only when strong (0 of 150 replicates at δ = 1, 13% at δ = 2), per-pair top-200 selection saturating with noise under the null (median baseline k_f = 0.025). ω is structurally blind to modules placed on HK genes (detection 0.000 at every δ; k_n fired at 0.61–1.00), yet under expression-matched low-variance non-HK drift ω stayed at its calibrated false-positive rate (0.000–0.067) while raw JS and cosine inflated to 0.81–1.00 (Supplementary Notes 1, 5)—the ratio cancels global compositional drift wherever it acts.
[23][ 80w][ ] ω best discriminated functional (δ ≥ 0.25) from neutral perturbations (Fig. 2e; AUC = 0.80 versus 0.72 k_f, 0.64 raw JS, 0.58 cosine, 0.21 k_n; 95% CI [0.777, 0.831]): the standard metrics’ apparent power is purchased with false positives on neutral drift. The ratio also conferred robustness to technical asymmetry (fourfold cell-count imbalance: ω −32%, k_f +67%, cosine +108%), and is thus a specificity-first screen, rejecting neutral drift at the cost of bounded power for weak-to-moderate signals (Supplementary Note 1).
[24][ 35w][ ] The design fully reproduced in a second background (skin keratinocyte stem cells; AUC(ω) = 0.908 versus AUC(k_f) = 0.859; Fig. 2e), with background-dependent power bounds and higher k_f power under fourfold imbalance (Supplementary Note 1).
[25][  6w][H] Real-data neutral-drift calibration on technical replicates
[26][ 67w][ ] We tested the same specificity on real technical replicates, calibrated against per-pair size-matched cell-shuffle nulls (B = 200; Methods). In the first batch of the IFN-β PBMC dataset 14, the 30 same-donor, same-condition cross-lane pairs serve as clean technical replicates: ω was fully calibrated (0 of 30 above its null 95th percentile; k_f likewise 0 of 30), whereas raw JS misreported 36.7% and cosine 23.3% (Fig. 3d).
[27][ 85w][ ] The design scales to the brain atlas (606 10x libraries) as a three-tier drift ladder of library-level pairs 12: T1, same donor, region, and cell type (2,161 pairs); T2, different donors within a region (1,089); T3, different regions within a donor (1,656). On T1, ω misreported least among the continuous divergence metrics (FPR 28.6% versus 37.6–45.2% for the others; Fig. 3b, c), and across the ladder its calibration gradient is the shallowest (T3 ratio 1.80 versus 2.98 raw JS; full per-tier values in Section 3.12).
[28][ 80w][ ] Two qualifications temper the brain result. The absolute ω FPR is not zero and grows with group size (14.1% below 30 nuclei per library to 48.0% above 500; raw JS 28.6% to 72.5%), a library-level technical component the anchor absorbs only partially—the 0-of-30 Kang outcome sits at the favorable end of this size dependence. A minority of classes show gene-specific library effects the anchor cannot absorb (Section 3.12). Neutral-drift immunity thus transfers to real data only as a relative-calibration advantage.
[29][ 59w][ ] An independent validation on unused data reinforces this calibration: on the CELLxGENE Microglia supercluster 12 (91,838 nuclei), sample-matched microglia-versus-CNS-macrophage pairs (n = 35) separate cleanly from neutral half-splits (n = 40; ω 21.83 ± 7.20 versus 1.30 ± 0.36; Mann-Whitney P = 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴; AUC = 1.00), the margin carried by k_f (Supplementary Note 16; Supplementary Fig. 14).
[30][  5w][H] Real perturbation demonstration: IFN-β-stimulated PBMCs
[31][106w][ ] On a real perturbation with unknown effect on the housekeeping anchor—PBMCs from eight donors, control versus 6-hour IFN-β stimulation 14 (24,413 cells, six cell types; Methods)—condition is fully confounded with lane, so we read this as a relative architecture demonstration. The perturbation was visible at the ω level in all six cell types, but k_f alone separated perturbation from donor drift as well or better (rank AUC: ω 0.55–0.92, k_f 0.74–1.00); in CD14+ monocytes ω fell to 0.55 while k_f retained 0.98, because stimulation raises housekeeping expression itself (median k_n 1.2–5.7-fold) and the inflating denominator partially cancels the functional signal (Supplementary Fig. 3; Supplementary Note 6).
[32][  4w][H] Benchmarking against perturbation-response metrics
[33][112w][ ] We benchmarked CKI against MELD (v1.0.2) and scDist (Python approximation of the R-only package) on the Kang IFN-β dataset 14. CKI and MELD agreed on effect direction in 6 of 6 cell types, but MELD’s within-type separation was near-saturated (AUC 0.997–0.9998). In an additive mean-shift simulation, MELD and the scDist approximation detected every configuration (sensitivity 1.00), whereas CKI ω rose from AUC 0.52 to 0.79 as the shift grew on 100 genes but collapsed to 0.05–0.13 on 500 genes, where the anchor responds (k_n AUC = 1.000) and the ratio is annihilated by its denominator; users seeking maximal detection power for broad perturbations should use MELD, scDist, or CKI’s own k_f component.
[34][  6w][H] Fixed-panel ablation: robust rankings, scheme-specific ω
[35][115w][ ] The reference implementation first reproduced the reported landscape exactly (maximum per-pair |Δω| = 6.4 × 10⁻¹³ over 31,764 pairs). Non-circular panels left rankings essentially untouched (pair-level ρ = 0.937 leave-pair-out, 0.918–0.931 other panels; class means ρ = 0.90–0.99), and the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient was preserved and amplified (6.10-fold reported; 6.53-fold leave-pair-out), with class-level significance largely robust under scheme-matched block-shuffle nulls (Supplementary Note 7). Circular selection inflated k_f by a median 1.61-fold versus leave-pair-out (grand mean ω 38.55 reported; 26.5 leave-pair-out; 6.5 fixed), so absolute ω values are upper-bound, scheme-specific estimates: rank-based conclusions are robust, but the tier cutoffs (ω < 15/25/35) are calibrated to the reported scheme’s inflated scale and do not transfer (Supplementary Note 7).
[36][  8w][H] A pan-cancer map of tissue-level divergence in tumors
[37][136w][ ] We applied CKI to TCGA bulk RNA-seq across five cancer types (LUAD, LUSC, LIHC, KIRC, BRCA) 10,11, totalling 3,567 samples. At bulk resolution, averaging over tumor, stromal, and immune compartments, ω quantifies divergence between tissue states; we compare tumor–tumor (TT), normal–normal (NN), and tumor–normal (TN) pairs within each cancer type (Fig. 4; Supplementary Fig. 4). A pan-cancer reversal emerged: NN pairs were more divergent than TT pairs in all five (mean NN/TT ω: LUAD 2.46, KIRC 1.88, LUSC 1.71, BRCA 1.57, LIHC 1.10; cluster-bootstrap CI excluding 1 in four of five, LIHC excepted). Decomposition identified the mechanism: TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 1.3–3.3-fold in every cancer type while TT k_f was equal to or higher—the reversal reflects a more stable housekeeping baseline among adjacent non-tumor specimens, not smaller functional-gene divergence of tumor cells (Fig. 4a).
[38][156w][ ] Stratifying LUAD tumors by driver mutation (61 EGFR-mutant, 120 KRAS-mutant, 311 wild-type) on per-tumor ω (Fig. 4b), KRAS-mutant tumors showed the highest tissue-level divergence (mean ω = 136.9 versus 115.4 wild-type and 122.2 EGFR-mutant; Kruskal-Wallis P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷; Dunn–Holm P ≤ 0.008 for both KRAS contrasts, P = 0.39 for EGFR–wild-type; label-permutation confirmed; Section 3.13). The KRAS elevation was carried predominantly by a lower housekeeping baseline (~84% of the log-ω gap) with an accompanying functional component (KRAS versus EGFR under k_f alone, P = 0.015). Adjusting for ESTIMATE stromal/immune admixture left the KRAS–wild-type difference significant (adjusted log-ω ratio 1.19, 95% CI [1.12, 1.26]) but abolished the apparent EGFR elevation (all adjusted P > 0.4); adjusting for smoking, alone or jointly with admixture, age, and sex, left the contrast essentially unchanged (Section 3.13). TP53 co-mutation and histological subtype were not adjusted for, so residual confounding remains; stratifications beyond LUAD are denominator-dominated vignettes (Supplementary Note 9).
[39][154w][ ] Four controls bound the interpretation (Supplementary Note 8). Composition adjustment left the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged (−1.3% pooled, 95% CI −4.8% to +2.0%), and high-purity-half comparisons increased the TT k_n elevation in all five cancer types, so admixture can only weaken, not create, it. All TCGA statistics were recomputed under a linear probability mapping (the authoritative caliber), with key results preserved (LIHC effect size mapping-sensitive: 1.10 versus 1.31 softmax; Section 1.7). LUAD KRAS identity panels showed no MSigDB Hallmark enrichment 15 after correction (all q ≥ 0.24), composition-adjusted regressions retained the TT ≥ NN k_f ordering in all five, and excluding the 32 cell-line-derived LIHC samples leaves the LIHC null unchanged (Section 1.7). TCGA “normal” samples are adjacent non-tumor, and per-tumor ω was not associated with survival (LIHC Cox HR per SD 1.07, 95% CI 0.88–1.31): we report all TCGA findings as tissue-level divergence at bulk resolution, pending single-cell or deconvolution-based validation.
[40][  7w][H] CKI ranks cell types by cross-organ conservation
[41][106w][ ] Of the 4,851 Tabula Sapiens pairs, 59 are same-cell-type cross-organ comparisons, asking which types maintain identity regardless of residence (Fig. 5; Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 5). Among well-sampled types (n ≥ 5 pairs; Table 1), CD8+ T cells (mean ω = 9.93 ± 4.62, n = 6) and Plasma cells (mean ω = 10.20 ± 2.03, n = 6) were the most conserved, followed by Neutrophils and NK cells, with Macrophages (n = 15) intermediate; Endothelial cells and Erythrocytes were the most divergent, consistent with organ-specific endothelial programs 16, though both rest on n = 3 pairs—sparsely sampled types (n = 1–3) carry no reliable signal.
[42][ 91w][ ] The cross-organ ranking agreed little with standard-metric rankings (Spearman r = −0.40 to 0.02, n = 59 pairs; Supplementary Table 2), because CKI explicitly normalizes. A k_f-only control qualifies this (Supplementary Note 9): per-cell-type mean ω and mean k_f are only weakly concordant (r = 0.23, 95% CI [−0.08, 0.38], n = 17), the extremes reproduce but the middle does not. The ranking is thus a composite of functional divergence and baseline differences, and because the k_n anchor is not calibrated across cell types, cross-type gaps should be read descriptively (Discussion).
[43][  6w][H] Brain regional analysis reveals divergence gradients
[44][ 66w][ ] We applied CKI to the Siletti et al. human brain atlas 12 (~3.3 million nuclei, 108 regions), focusing on 888,263 non-neuronal nuclei in 10 major classes (neurons excluded: supercluster_term does not resolve neuronal subtypes). ω was computed for all same-cell-type cross-region pairs (31,764; Fig. 6; Supplementary Fig. 6; Supplementary Table 3); vascular cells and fibroblasts are heterogeneous superclusters, so their class-level ω values are supercluster averages.
[45][ 68w][ ] Class-mean ω spanned a regional gradient from Bergmann glia and vascular cells (both 13.56) to astrocytes (82.75 ± 44.98, n = 5,778 pairs)—a full-data endpoint gradient of 6.10-fold. Because class-mean k_n is confounded by class size (below), this headline is an uncorrected upper bound: an equal-n downsample control attenuates it to 1.74 (95% CI [1.64, 1.84]) and a span-matched control (21 intra-cerebellar pairs) yields 3.68 (Supplementary Note 10).
[46][ 84w][ ] A cell-type-level block-shuffle null permuting library-to-region assignments (B = 1,000; Methods) tested whether the gradient exceeds chance: regional structure significantly raised mean ω for 4 of 10 classes (astrocytes at the permutation floor; OPCs, committed OPCs, fibroblasts P ≤ 0.030), 3 of 10 surviving Benjamini-Hochberg correction; a donor-stratified null leaves the same 3 of 10, and ependymal cells—the one class moving against the conservative direction—still do not survive (stratified q = 0.052). Microglia, oligodendrocytes, and Bergmann glia sit at or below the null expectation.
[47][ 78w][ ] Four analyses probe the gradient’s robustness. First, restricting to same-donor region pairs preserved the extremes and a 4.50-fold gradient, so donor identity cannot explain it away (Supplementary Note 12). Second, the astrocyte-versus-Bergmann-glia contrast is predominantly a k_n effect (k_f differs 2.0-fold; mean k_n 3.2-fold): class-mean ω tracks k_n (Spearman ρ = −0.73) but not k_f (ρ = 0.09), and under a k_f-only ordering astrocytes rank third of ten—the gradient chiefly reflects housekeeping stability across regions, not functional-gene divergence.
[48][ 81w][ ] Third, class-mean k_n correlates negatively with class size (Spearman ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043), while class-mean ω is uncorrelated with all tested confounders (P ≥ 0.091; Supplementary Note 10). The equal-n residual is carried predominantly by the functional term (astrocyte/Bergmann-glia k_f ratio 2.09 equal-n, 2.03 full-data), whereas the k_n ratio reverses (equal-n 1.29, astrocyte higher; full-data 0.31)—the full-data Bergmann-glia k_n elevation is largely a class-size artefact. Fourth, the gradient is robust to the group-size threshold in direction but not magnitude.
[49][ 54w][ ] The gradient aligns with known cell biology: vascular cells and fibroblasts are tissue-resident and locally replenished, consistent with low ω (though low class-level ω is not spatial uniformity 17,18); microglia share core surveillance machinery 19; astrocytes express region-specific ion channels, transporters, and secreted factors—regional specialization compatible with, but not established by, the k_n-dominated ordering.
[50][ 51w][ ] The gradient suggests a residence/migration framework (recent migrants should show low inter-regional ω; residents accumulate regional signatures), but the data provide no support: OPCs—the most actively migrating non-neuronal population—show the second-highest mean ω (40.62), and the screen shows no oligodendrocyte-lineage concentration (fold 0.77, P = 0.92); the framework remains hypothesis generation.
[51][  6w][H] Anomalously similar pairs: a hypothesis-generating screen
[52][ 92w][ ] Low ω for a cell type across two regions indicates similarity beyond baseline expectation, with non-exclusive candidate mechanisms (developmental origin heterogeneity, colonization-route boundaries 20–22, postnatal migration 23,24). Globally, under a design-matched null that recomputes the full selection rule on every block-shuffle permutation, the expected number of Strong candidates is 148.3 across the 31,764-pair pool—the 39 observed lie 3.8-fold below expectation (P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0), and no candidate survives FDR correction (minimum q = 0.520). The catalogue is thus a prioritized hypothesis list, inheriting the atlas’s four-donor structure (Supplementary Note 11).
[53][ 70w][ ] The screen uses a multiplicative model, expected_ω = μ_ct × μ_pair / μ_grand (Methods): a residual (observed/expected) well below 1 marks a cell type far less differentiated between two regions than expected from its plasticity and the pair’s divergence. Three tiers: Strong (residual < 0.3, ω < 15, lowest ω in the pair), Moderate (residual < 0.5, ω < 25), Weak (residual < 0.75, ω < 35; Supplementary Fig. 8).
[54][ 73w][ ] Criteria identified 39 (0.12%) Strong, 1,171 (3.69%) Moderate, and 5,381 (16.94%) Weak candidates among 31,764 comparisons (Supplementary Table 4). Under the block-shuffle null (B = 1,000; Supplementary Fig. 9), 31 Strong candidates showed raw P < 0.05; the only sub-nominal stratified family is intra-cerebellar Bergmann-glia (m = 21; minimum q = 0.042). The microglial concentration (16 Strong) does not survive the design-matched null (fold 0.31, P = 0.990): we make no class-composition claim.
[55][ 46w][ ] A negative control confirms the null is calibrated by library-level, not regional, structure: libraries of every region were split into pseudo-regions and the identical test re-run on 127,756 pseudo-pairs (Supplementary Note 12; Supplementary Fig. 10)—cross-region pseudo-pairs gave near-nominal tail rates, same-region halves a 37.6% lower-tail rate.
[56][ 80w][ ] The catalogue converges on anatomically coherent themes. Microglial candidates (16 of 39) concentrate on visual-relay and orbitofrontal dissections (five pairs involve the lateral geniculate nucleus; Supplementary Note 11); mature-oligodendrocyte candidates (10, all raw P < 0.05) show a thalamo-temporal orientation crossing rather than following the dorsoventral origin boundary 25 (mechanistic basis unassigned; Supplementary Note 13). Astrocytes, fibroblasts, and ependymal cells contributed sparse candidates 26,27; Bergmann glia, vascular cells, and choroid plexus none 26,28. All are hypothesis-generating targets for follow-up 19.
[57][ 36w][ ] The block-shuffle re-analysis thus tempers the brain catalogue: an earlier per-pair label-shuffle implementation was anti-conservative (36.3% of P-values at the floor) by ignoring the 10x-library block structure, and we position the entire candidate list as hypothesis-generating.
===== Discussion =====
[58][158w][ ] CKI reframes transcriptomic comparison—from measuring absolute distance to quantifying functional divergence relative to an internal baseline—but is better described as a recombination of established ideas than a conceptual shift: reference genes have long anchored normalization and quality control 29, the constrained-variance property of housekeeping genes is characterized genomically 13,30, and gene-specific noise floors are central to the transcriptional-noise literature 31–33. CKI combines these ingredients into a per-comparison, design-testable index with explicit split-half calibration and a design-matched permutation null. The key assumption is that HK genes are under stabilizing selection constraining their expression variance; unlike Ka/Ks—where a shared mutation rate cancels mathematically—CKI uses empirically defined HK genes as baseline, lacking comparable mechanistic cancellation, so CKI is a heuristic index rather than a formal measure of selection. The empirical calibration indicates k_f systematically exceeds k_n even for identical populations (Results), so ω_cal = ω / 7.70 is the operational scale, interpreted against the empirical equivalent-population distribution rather than fixed cut-offs.
[59][101w][ ] CKI is a divergence index, not a classifier—by design. Classifying cell types from transcriptomic data is largely solved; CKI answers a complementary question: regardless of labels, how much functional divergence separates two populations relative to their shared baseline? The negative correlation with standard metrics is partly a denominator effect (decomposition, Results), so ω is a composite of numerator and denominator information rather than an independent dimension. The ratio earns its increment over k_f under controlled ground truth and in drift rejection; on real-data orderings the increment is not established, and k_f with a design-matched null remains the default for ordering claims.
[60][ 63w][ ] The Ka/Ks analogy is heuristically productive but technically bounded: HK genes are empirically defined rather than mechanistically neutral, and the HK anchor is itself the most strongly constrained expression class, so ω should be read as functional divergence in excess of a constrained baseline, never as evidence of positive selection. The full correspondence—including its McDonald–Kreitman-style fourth term 34—and caveats are in Section 1.4.
[61][ 56w][ ] CKI complements rather than replaces existing methods: SAMap 35 and SATURN 4 address cross-species alignment and CACIMAR 36 conservation scoring—different questions from within-species functional divergence, so we did not benchmark quantitatively. More broadly, CKI provides a principled null model for any transcriptomic comparison: before concluding two populations differ meaningfully, ask whether the difference exceeds baseline expectation.
[62][ 45w][ ] CKI is also not redundant with Augur 37, which prioritizes cell types by condition predictability: rankings are moderately concordant (Augur separability versus class-mean ω ρ = 0.442; versus k_f ρ = 0.564) with overlapping extremes, at n = 10 classes descriptive only (Supplementary Note 14).
[63][154w][ ] The pan-cancer reversal—tumor specimens less divergent than adjacent non-tumor tissue (mean NN/TT 1.10–2.46, cluster-bootstrap CIs excluding 1 in four of five cancers)—is a robust tissue-level observation whose mechanistic reading remains cautious: at bulk resolution the apparent convergence could reflect cell-composition shifts, peritumoral inflammation, or RNA-quality differences rather than genuine transcriptional convergence, and the reversal is predominantly a k_n effect, so the data do not indicate smaller functional divergence of tumor cells. A marker-panel composition check (Supplementary Note 8) attenuates the pooled k_n coefficient by only −1.3%—a lower bound, since marker panels are noisy proxies—so the pooled k_n excess is not fully explained by marker-measurable composition (plausibly reflecting tumor-specific housekeeping dysregulation 38), but composition cannot be excluded either; single-cell or deconvolution-based validation remains necessary. Tumor and adjacent non-tumor aliquots differ systematically in source site and batch, and adjacent non-tumor is not healthy tissue; separating tumor-specific housekeeping elevation from field effects requires an external healthy reference.
[64][127w][ ] The cross-organ and cross-brain-region analyses show CKI measuring functional differentiation at multiple spatial scales, with one qualification: the brain regional gradient is a composite signal dominated by the k_n denominator (3.21-fold versus 2.03-fold from k_f), not a pure functional-divergence ranking. The candidate screen illustrates a second principle: 31 of 39 Strong candidates showed raw P < 0.05 but none survived correction (minimum q = 0.520; tier-dependence caveats in Supplementary Note 13)—statistical claims at atlas scale require nulls respecting the experimental design, and the absence of FDR-significant signals is itself informative, delimiting what adult transcriptomes alone can support. Preliminary cross-species validation found no significant correlation of per-cell-type mean ω rankings (Spearman r = −0.17, P = 0.55; Supplementary Fig. 11), so cross-species transferability of ω remains unestablished 39.
[65][134w][ ] Although no individual candidate survives FDR correction, several signals are consistent with prior reports—literature anchoring rather than independent validation. Maturation comparisons must use the k_f-only ordering, because the composite is k_n-dominated: under k_f alone the lineage ranks committed OPCs highest (k_f class mean 0.197, highest of ten, with the widest sampling uncertainty) above oligodendrocytes (0.054), with OPCs lowest (0.048)—partially consistent with reports that early oligodendrocyte stages are regionally uniform whereas mature subsets become region-enriched 40,41—whereas the composite ω ordering gives the opposite picture; neither supports a monotone-maturation reading. The thalamo-temporal orientation of the mature-oligodendrocyte candidates concentrates on an axis rather than scattering across the 108 regions (two null references in Supplementary Note 13); correspondence with the developmental organization of forebrain oligodendrocytes 25 remains at the level of axis anatomy, pending lineage-tracing or spatial-transcriptomic follow-up.
[66][172w][ ] CKI and standard metrics answer different questions, and choosing between them reduces to a few practical rules. Use CKI when the question is relative—is the functional divergence between two populations larger than what their own housekeeping baseline varies by, within the same dataset and gene-selection scheme?—offering a normalized scale and a specificity-first screen: a significant ω rejects label exchangeability under a design-matched null, and functional signal on the anchor is invisible to ω, as the IFN-β demonstration makes explicit (14; Results). Before trusting fine-grained ω orderings, diagnose the denominator: if k_n lies in the extreme tail of its own null or correlates with technical covariates, report k_f with the design-matched null directly—in the real data analyzed here, ω orderings were predominantly denominator-driven (Supplementary Note 9), so k_f plus a design-matched null is the honest default for ordering claims. Use standard metrics when absolute cross-dataset distances are required, for classification, for weak-to-moderate effects at atlas scale, or when the baseline itself is suspect—then interpret k_n and k_f separately rather than trusting ω alone.
[67][ 97w][ ] For prospective applications we recommend: (i) at least 100–200 cells per group, below which pseudobulk noise inflates k_n and k_f unpredictably; (ii) the pre-specified HRT Atlas v1.0 housekeeping panel (cki/data/hrt_atlas.csv) rather than data-driven auto-detection; (iii) comparisons only within the same dataset and gene-selection scheme—absolute ω does not transfer; (iv) calibration within the dataset of interest by split-half sampling, interpreting ω against that internal baseline rather than the mouse-derived 7.70; (v) a permutation null matching the experimental design (block-shuffle where libraries or donors define blocks; cki.blocknull.block_shuffle_test), expecting bounded sensitivity—ω rejects neutral drift rather than detecting weak functional shifts.
[68][369w][ ] Limitations. Several boundaries delimit where ω can fire and how it should be read. (i) Scope: CKI detects state changes within a given cell type, not cell-type identity; because the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific, the k_n baseline is directly comparable only within the same cell type, so limited cross-type discrimination of ω is expected by design and delineates, rather than limits, its scope; CKI currently operates at the pseudobulk level. (ii) Gene-set definition and calibration: the per-pair k_f scheme selects genes by the observed difference itself, so k_f magnitudes are inflated upper bounds (median 1.61-fold) and absolute tier thresholds are scheme-specific; housekeeping expression may be dysregulated in cancer 38; and the empirical calibration factor (7.70, 95% CI [7.37, 8.02]) derives from mouse split-half controls, so absolute ω does not transfer across datasets or gene-selection schemes. Key parameters (top-200 DE genes, 2,000 HVGs, the +1 pseudo-count) are practical defaults, not optimized values. (iii) Data and design: the TCGA analysis is limited to bulk resolution, its per-cancer permutation test holds the identity panel fixed (anti-conservative), and five cancer types make the results exploratory; the brain atlas uses post-mortem tissue, and donor-level PMI and RNA-integrity covariates were unavailable, so regionally differential RNA degradation could masquerade as regional structure in k_n. (iv) Anchor visibility: ω is structurally insensitive to perturbations that move the housekeeping anchor itself—in simulation, once 500 genes shifted, the ratio collapsed (AUC 0.05–0.13)—and donor-paired permutation power vanishes beyond roughly 500 cells per group, bounding the operating window at ~50–200 cells; the scDist results were obtained with a Python approximation of the R package and should be re-verified against the original implementation. (v) Multiple testing and cluster-aware inference: the class-level upper-tail tests and the pair-level lower-tail screen are separate families reported without joint cross-family correction; the block-shuffle null assumes libraries are otherwise exchangeable, so systematic library-level confounding would distort the null in ways the permutation cannot capture (bounded by the pseudo-region negative control; Supplementary Note 12); and B = 1,000 bounds the resolvable P-value at 9.99 × 10⁻⁴, so the per-pair FDR outcome (minimum q = 0.520) is a statement about permutation resolution rather than evidence against any candidate. Full treatments and supporting sensitivity analyses are given in Supplementary Notes 1–16.
[69][109w][ ] CKI adapts the baseline-normalization logic of Ka/Ks to single-cell transcriptomics, decomposing population divergence into a housekeeping baseline rate (k_n) and an identity-gene functional rate (k_f). Across simulation, calibration, cross-organ, pan-cancer, and brain-regional analyses, ω rankings carried reproducible biological signal while absolute ω values remain scheme- and dataset-specific; statistical claims at atlas scale require nulls respecting the experimental design; and the absence of FDR-significant candidates is itself an informative bound on what adult transcriptomes alone can support. Released as an open-source Python package with a fully reproducible pipeline, CKI offers an interpretable complement to standard distance metrics, with applications to developmental biology, drug-response profiling, aging research, and evolutionary cell biology.
