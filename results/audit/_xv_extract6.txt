A marker-panel composition check quantifies the composition confound directly (Supplementary Note 8): tumor-tumor pairs do show larger composition differences than normal-normal pairs (median |Delta z| 1.33–1.46-fold higher across the immune, stromal, and epithelial panels, and 1.24-fold with the myeloid panel included; all P < 10⁻⁷⁷), and within tumor-tumor pairs k_n correlates with the overall four-panel composition difference (Spearman ρ = 0.387 pooled; 0.23–0.52 per cancer type); however, in regressions of log k_n on pair type with the four composition deltas as covariates—including a myeloid panel (CD68, CD163, LST1, FCGR3A, C1QA)—and sample-level cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (B = 200), the tumor-pair coefficient attenuates by −0.5% pooled (95% CI [−3.2%, +2.6%]), with marked per-cancer heterogeneity (BRCA −14.0%, LUSC −9.7%, LUAD −2.3%, KIRC +19.6%, LIHC +33.5%). Because marker panels are noisy proxies for true composition, these attenuation estimates are lower bounds on the true composition contribution; the pooled tumor-pair k_n excess is therefore not fully explained by a noisy marker-panel proxy, but neither can a marker-panel regression exclude composition as the driver—plausibly reflecting tumor-specific housekeeping dysregulation 39 or RNA-quality differences instead, and single-cell or deconvolution-based validation remains necessary. Two further limitations bound the bulk analysis: within each cancer type, tumor and adjacent non-tumor aliquots differ systematically in tissue-source site and processing batch (a known TCGA property), so part of the NN/TT contrast could track these technical strata—we did not test a batch-matched subset; and adjacent non-tumor tissue from cancer patients is not healthy tissue, so separating tumor-specific housekeeping elevation from field effects will ultimately require an external healthy reference (e.g., GTEx), which we leave to future work. Within LUAD, the KRAS enrichment among ever-smokers mirrors known lung-adenocarcinoma epidemiology, yet smoking status itself does not carry the divergence association, so the KRAS-linked functional divergence is unlikely to be a smoking-field effect; and although the purity adjustment relies on an ESTIMATE-style signature score rather than a consensus-purity estimate, the score is monotonically equivalent to published ESTIMATE purity, leaving the adjusted conclusions unchanged. The PAM50 gradient cannot be read as a functional-divergence ordering: under k_f-only analysis the subtype ordering largely reverses (Luminal A lowest mean k_f, Basal-like highest; Supplementary Note 9), so the ω gradient is predominantly a baseline effect, and the proliferation-confound explanation (aggressive subtypes proliferate fastest) remains. The raw ω scale also varies substantially across datasets and computation schemes (e.g., BRCA Luminal A ω ≈ 142.0 versus mean brain regional ω = 38.55). In TCGA, per-pair k_n is comparable to the single-cell datasets (mean 0.005, range 1.6 × 10⁻⁴–5.6 × 10⁻², versus a mean per-pair k_n of 0.0035 in the brain single-nucleus dataset), so the higher raw ω in TCGA is driven primarily by a larger k_f: bulk tumor-versus-normal expression differences span thousands of genes. Cross-dataset ω comparisons should therefore be interpreted as rank-based rather than absolute.
