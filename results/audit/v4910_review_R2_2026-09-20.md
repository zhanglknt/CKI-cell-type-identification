# Review — Reviewer 2 (Evolutionary Genomics)

**Manuscript:** "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics" (v49.10)
**Journal:** Nature Communications (Methodology)
**Reviewer role:** Ka/Ks, selection metrics, evolutionary framing
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Prior round:** Reviewer flagged (i) the Ka/Ks analogy is structurally inverted (Ks is a neutral standard; HK genes are constrained) and (ii) McDonald–Kreitman was uncited. v49.10 adds a Discussion sentence on the inversion and inserts McDonald & Kreitman (1991) as ref [34], renumbering 34–56 → 35–57.

---

## Summary

CKI decomposes transcriptomic divergence between two pseudobulk populations into k_n (JS divergence on a housekeeping-gene reference) and k_f (JS divergence on per-pair selected identity genes), and reports ω = k_f/k_n. The manuscript is unusually self-critical: it documents that its real-data orderings are predominantly denominator-driven, that the brain gradient is ~70% class-size artifact, that no candidate survives FDR correction, and that the simulation specificity advantage is conditional on an HK-anchored neutral model (adversarial controls in Supp. Notes 1/5). The v49.10 fix adds a genuine acknowledgment that the Ka/Ks analogy is structurally inverted, and the reference renumbering is correctly executed (spot-checked: [34] = McDonald & Kreitman, Nature 351:652–654, 1991; SAMap [35], Augur [37], Waxman & Wurmbach [38], Marques [40], Spitzer [41], Efron & Tibshirani [57] all match their new positions). MS/SI numerical cross-checks largely pass (simulation AUCs, drift-ladder FPRs, Kang 0/30 vs 36.7%/23.3%, LUAD driver means 136.9/122.2/115.4, calibrated values 82.75/9.26 = 8.94, 13.56/9.08 = 1.49, etc.).

However, the specific fix for the analogy problem is only half-substantive: the McDonald–Kreitman citation is a misattributed name-drop, the ω > 1 threshold introduced by the new sentence conflicts with the manuscript's own calibration framework, the Supplementary Information was not updated and still asserts the opposite ("structurally similar"), and the headline elements (title, Fig 1a, Table 1 cross-type ranking) continue to carry the inverted framing to every reader. The Table 1 caveat does not resolve the contradiction with the scope claim, and the scope claim's stated premise is contradicted by the manuscript's own implementation. Major revision is required.

## Major concerns

### 1. The McDonald–Kreitman citation is a name-drop that misattributes "constrained-reference logic" to the MK test, and therefore does not resolve the inversion problem.

The new Discussion sentence reads:

> "The inversion is structural rather than incidental: in Ka/Ks the denominator (Ks) is a putatively neutral standard, whereas the HK anchor is itself the most strongly constrained expression class, so the construction parallels not a neutral-standard ratio but the constrained-reference logic of McDonald–Kreitman-style contrasts 34; ω above 1 should accordingly be read as functional divergence in excess of a constrained baseline, never as evidence of positive selection."

The first clause is a real improvement — the inversion is now named, and the "never as evidence of positive selection" disclaimer is correct and necessary. But the pivot to McDonald–Kreitman is wrong on its own terms. In the classical MK test, the neutral standard is **still the synonymous class** (P_s and D_s); what MK adds over Ka/Ks is the **polymorphism-vs-divergence contrast**, which is what allows the separation of neutral from adaptive fixation. The MK test's denominator is not a "constrained reference" — it is the same putatively neutral standard used by Ka/Ks, which is precisely the feature the preceding clause of the authors' own sentence identifies as the disanalogy. CKI has no analogue whatsoever of the polymorphism/divergence distinction: nothing in CKI corresponds to within-species segregating variation vs. fixed between-species differences. The sentence therefore replaces one imperfect analogy with a second, unworked-out one, cites a 1991 paper in support, and moves on. Naming the contrast without mapping any component of CKI onto it is a cosmetic fix. Either the authors must state explicitly what in CKI plays the role of polymorphism and what plays the role of divergence (there is nothing), or they should drop the MK parallel and state the honest position: CKI is a ratio of divergence on a weakly constrained class to divergence on a strongly constrained class, a construction with no direct population-genetic analogue, and the Ka/Ks comparison is rhetorical branding rather than structural inspiration.

### 2. The fix was applied to the main text only; the Supplementary Information still asserts the opposite, and the new "ω above 1" anchor contradicts the manuscript's own calibration framework.

(a) SI §1.2: "k_n thus provides an internal baseline, heuristically analogous to Ks (synonymous substitution rate) in molecular evolution." SI §1.4: "The Ka/Ks analogy is structurally similar but mathematically non-equivalent." The main text now says "The inversion is structural rather than incidental." These cannot both stand. McDonald–Kreitman appears nowhere in the SI. A one-file patch is the signature of a cosmetic revision.

(b) The new sentence anchors interpretation at "ω above 1", but the Introduction anchors it at the empirical calibration baseline: "values close to the equivalent-population calibration baseline (established empirically within each dataset; see Results) indicate baseline-consistent differences, values far above indicate functional divergence exceeding baseline variation, and values far below indicate strong functional constraint." The calibration baseline is 7.70 (mouse), 9.73 (brain-internal), 7.67 (Tabula Sapiens), or class-specific 7.58–12.52. Since split-half **equivalent** populations already yield ω ≈ 7.70, "ω above 1" is true of essentially every comparison in the paper, including biologically null ones, and carries no information. The Introduction and SI §1.4 define direction relative to the calibrated baseline; the new Discussion sentence reverts to the Ka/Ks-idiom threshold of 1. Fig 1e compounds this: "ω = 1 (the theoretical baseline for equivalent populations, marked by dashed line)... ω = 1 is never observed in practice." A "theoretical baseline" that is never observed and is empirically wrong by 7.7-fold is not a baseline; it is a residue of the discarded analogy. The direction definition must be unified on the calibrated baseline, and the ω = 1 marker removed or explicitly labeled as an analogy artifact.

(c) The disclaimer is asymmetric: ω > 1 is disclaimed as "never... evidence of positive selection", but no symmetric caution states that ω far below baseline is not evidence of purifying selection — yet the Introduction explicitly offers "values far below indicate strong functional constraint", which is the same selection-regime reading the authors just disavowed for the upper tail.

### 3. The title and Fig 1a still deliver the inverted analogy, and the positive-selection language, to every reader.

Title: "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics."

Fig 1a legend: "Ka/Ks uses synonymous substitution rate (Ks) as a neutral baseline; ω = Ka/Ks > 1 indicates positive selection (a heuristic analogy only, not a formal population-genetic claim). CKI uses housekeeping gene divergence for k_n and identity gene divergence for k_f; ω = k_f/k_n quantifies transcriptomic divergence relative to the baseline."

The parenthetical hedge does not survive the layout: panel (a) is titled as a "Conceptual analogy", places "ω > 1 indicates positive selection" immediately above "ω = k_f/k_n", and reuses the same symbol ω for both quantities — inviting exactly the reading the Discussion now forbids ("never as evidence of positive selection"). Given the authors' own concession that the analogy is structurally inverted, the paper's title and opening figure are built on the disavowed framing. The parenthetical is a fig-leaf: a reader who sees only the title and Fig 1a — the majority — will take away "transcriptomic dN/dS". The title's "Ka/Ks-inspired" should be reconsidered (e.g., "a constrained-baseline-normalized index of functional divergence"), and Fig 1a should either drop the positive-selection sentence entirely or be redesigned to show the inversion explicitly (Ks = neutral standard vs. HK = most constrained class).

### 4. The Table 1 cross-organ ranking still contradicts both the scope claim and the authors' own decision rule; the new caveat is descriptive, not structural.

Scope claim (Discussion): "Because the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific—housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types—the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type, so limited cross-type discrimination of ω is expected by design and delineates, rather than limits, the index's scope."

New Table 1 caveat (Results): "because the k_n anchor is not calibrated across cell types (Discussion), cross-type gaps in mean ω should be read as descriptive rather than calibrated differences."

Authors' own decision rule (Discussion): "the honest default for ordering claims is k_f plus a design-matched null, with ω reserved for its specificity-first property—rejecting HK-anchored neutral drift—rather than for ranking; the real-data ω orderings were predominantly denominator-driven."

Yet the Results section is headed "CKI ranks cell types by cross-organ conservation", and Table 1/Fig 5 present a 17-type ranking by mean ω as a main result. The caveat does not resolve the contradiction; it re-labels it. The manuscript's own control (Supp. Note 9) shows the ranking does not reproduce under k_f: "Spearman r = 0.23 (P = 0.37, n = 17)... r = 0.10 (P = 0.87) among the 5 well-sampled types", while "ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.31 has 95% CI [−0.61, −0.17], excluding zero — the interval estimates support a denominator-driven ordering", with the specific pathology that "NK cells rank most divergent under ω yet second-most conserved under k_f among well-sampled types." A ranking that (i) the authors' own framework classifies as denominator-driven, (ii) fails its own numerator control at the cell-type level, and (iii) is declared out-of-scope by the scope statement, should not be a main-text table headed "ranks cell types by cross-organ conservation". Either present the ranking under k_f with ω as a supplementary composite, or demote Table 1 to a descriptive exhibit with the denominator caveat in the caption. The word "conservation" additionally imports an evolutionary reading (sequence/expression conservation) that the authors elsewhere explicitly disavow for this ordering.

### 5. The scope claim's stated premise is contradicted by the manuscript's own implementation and evidence.

The scope claim justifies non-comparability by "housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types." But the Methods state: "Housekeeping (HK) genes are loaded from the HRT Atlas v1.0 reference 13 (1,130 human-mouse conserved HK genes; mouse ortholog column for mouse datasets, human column for Tabula Sapiens, TCGA, and the brain atlas)" — one global HK set for every cell type in every analysis. SI Algorithm 2 confirms: "k_n uses the global HK set (same for all pairs)". And the manuscript's own calibration evidence shows the anchor baseline is nearly identical across the two most extreme cell classes: "essentially unchanged (the two classes' baselines nearly coincide, 9.26 vs 9.08)" — astrocytes (the most divergent class, ω = 82.75) vs. Bergmann glia (the most constrained, 13.56). If the anchor were meaningfully cell-type-specific, these two maximally different glial classes should not have coinciding baselines. What actually varies is the k_n **value** (class-specific baselines span 7.58–12.52, a 1.65-fold spread), and Supp. Note 10 attributes that variation largely to class size, not cell-type biology: "k_n correlates significantly with class size — Spearman ρ = −0.648 (P = 0.043)... smaller classes have noisier HK pseudobulks and hence larger k_n." The scope claim therefore conflates (a) HK gene-set identity — which is constant by construction in every reported analysis — with (b) per-pair k_n estimator noise and class-size-dependent sampling — which is the real, and honestly documented, limitation. The sentence as written asserts a mechanism ("housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types") that the paper's own pipeline precludes and its own evidence undermines. The limitation should be restated in terms of the per-pair estimator, scheme dependence, and class-size effects.

### 6. Internal numerical inconsistency: the Discussion quotes legacy softmax-caliber composition-attenuation values that contradict the "authoritative" linear-caliber values in the Results and SI.

Results (TCGA controls): "all reported TCGA statistics were recomputed under a linear probability mapping... and this linear re-computation is the authoritative caliber throughout" — quoting pooled k_n attenuation "−1.2%, bootstrap 95% CI −4.1% to +2.6%; per cancer type −16% to +37%". SI §Note 8 (linear update) confirms: "pooled four-panel attenuation −0.8% (cluster-bootstrap median −1.2%, 95% CI [−4.1%, +2.6%]); per-cancer attenuation LIHC +37.1%, KIRC +18.3%, BRCA −16.2%, LUAD −1.6%, LUSC −11.1%."

But the Discussion states: "the tumor-pair coefficient attenuates by −0.5% pooled (95% CI [−3.2%, +2.6%]), with marked per-cancer heterogeneity (BRCA −14.0%, LUSC −9.7%, LUAD −2.3%, KIRC +19.6%, LIHC +33.5%)" — the legacy softmax values, unlabeled, in the same manuscript that declares the linear caliber authoritative "throughout". A reader cannot tell which numbers are current. All legacy-caliber numbers must be purged or explicitly labeled.

## Minor concerns

1. **"Monotonically" is contradicted by the authors' own X-category range.** "Beyond controls, ω values increased monotonically with biological distance (Fig. 2b, c)" — but the same paragraph reports D (different cell type, same organ) at "mean ω = 43.19... range 30.1–61.7" and X (different cell type, different organ) at "23.0–31.6". X is the most distant category and has the *lower* values (n = 2, admittedly). The same reversal appears in the human atlas (different-organ different-cell-type 20.80 < same-organ different-cell-type 24.87) and is there correctly analyzed as a k_n effect. "Monotonically" should be deleted.

2. **NK cells are placed at both ends of the ranking within one page.** Results: "CD8+ T cells... and Plasma cells... were the most conserved, followed by Neutrophils and NK cells" — yet the immediately following paragraph and Supp. Note 9 state "NK cells rank most divergent under ω, second-most conserved under k_f." If NK is most divergent under ω among well-sampled types, it cannot be third-most conserved in the ω-based listing. Please reconcile.

3. **Incomplete threshold sweep sentence.** "thresholds of 10, 20 (reported), 50, and 100 nuclei retain 10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12" — four thresholds, three gradient values; the 100-nuclei gradient is missing.

4. **Fig 4c legend vs. Results on the KRAS k_n adjustment.** Legend: "KRAS versus WT retained after purity and smoking adjustment, adjusted P = 0.009 and 0.029"; Results: "Δk_n −0.0004, P = 0.003" (admixture-only). Two different adjustments are conflated without labeling; SI gives "omega +13.6, P = 3.3 × 10⁻⁴" for the joint model. Please make the adjustment model explicit at each citation.

5. **Supplementary Tables 1–19 are file pointers, not tables.** E.g., Supp. Table 2: "Data file: results/phase35_cross_organ_conservation.csv." Consequently, several main-text numbers (CD8+ T 9.93 ± 4.62; Plasma 10.20 ± 2.03; Macrophages 15.49 ± 8.46; the NN/TT ratio CIs) cannot be verified from the SI document itself. For a Methods paper, the key tables should be rendered.

6. **Fig 1e's "theoretical baseline ω = 1"** should be removed or relabeled (see Major 2b); it currently reinforces the Ka/Ks reading one figure panel after the hedge.

7. **Positive note for the record:** the numerical agreement between MS and SI is otherwise high. I verified: simulation type-I/power grid and AUCs (0.804/0.716/0.640/0.584/0.213; skin 0.908/0.859), Kang 0/30 [0.000, 0.114] with calibration 0.963 vs. raw JS 36.7% and cosine 23.3%, drift-ladder T1 FPRs (ω 28.6% vs. 45.2/44.1/40.6/37.6/35.7%; Jaccard 19.9%), T2/T3 calibrations (1.76/1.80 vs. 3.32/2.98; Jaccard 1.41), pseudo-region control (5.79/6.87 vs. 6.17/7.90; 37.6% same-origin), LUAD driver means (136.9/122.2/115.4; KW P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷), Augur concordances (0.442/0.564/−0.236), calibrated values (82.75/9.26 = 8.94; 13.56/9.08 = 1.49; 12.52/7.58 = 1.65), and the class-level BH q-values (0.010/0.017/0.075). The renumbered reference list is correctly executed where spot-checked.

## Questions to authors

1. In the McDonald–Kreitman parallel, what component of CKI corresponds to polymorphism and what to divergence? If none, why is MK cited as the structural parallel rather than simply acknowledging that the denominator is a constrained (not neutral) class with no population-genetic counterpart?
2. Will you revise the title and Fig 1a to remove "ω = Ka/Ks > 1 indicates positive selection" and the "theoretical baseline ω = 1", given the Discussion's own statements that the analogy is structurally inverted and that ω > 1 is "never... evidence of positive selection"?
3. Given cell-type-level ρ(ω, k_f) = 0.23 (95% CI [−0.08, 0.38]) and your own rule that "the honest default for ordering claims is k_f plus a design-matched null... rather than for ranking", why should the cross-organ ω ranking remain a main-text Table 1 rather than being replaced by the k_f ordering?
4. Given that a single global HRT Atlas HK set is used in every analysis and the astrocyte (9.26) and Bergmann-glia (9.08) baselines nearly coincide, do you still maintain that "the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific — housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types"? On what evidence?
5. Which caliber are the Discussion's composition-attenuation numbers (−0.5% pooled; BRCA −14.0%... LIHC +33.5%)? Please reconcile with the authoritative linear-caliber values (−1.2% pooled; BRCA −16.2%... LIHC +37.1%).
6. Is "increased monotonically with biological distance" sustainable given X: 23.0–31.6 vs. D: 30.1–61.7?
7. Why is there no symmetric disclaimer that ω far below baseline is not evidence of purifying/constraining selection, when the Introduction offers "values far below indicate strong functional constraint"?

## Score: 5/10

## Recommendation: **Major Revision**

The underlying methodological work is more honest than most in this genre, and the v49.10 sentence conceding the structural inversion is a genuine, if partial, improvement. But the fix as executed is half-cosmetic: the MK citation misattributes constrained-reference logic to a test whose standard is the same neutral synonymous class as Ka/Ks; the SI was left asserting "structurally similar"; the "ω above 1" anchor contradicts the paper's own calibration framework; and the title, Fig 1a, and Table 1 still market the inverted analogy and a denominator-driven ranking as headline results. None of these are unfixable, but they go to the paper's central framing and must be resolved before the work is suitable for publication.
