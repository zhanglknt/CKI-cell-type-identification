# Review — Nature Communications (Methodology)
## Reviewer 3 (Biostatistics) — Manuscript v49.10
**"CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics"**
Date: 2026-09-20

---

## Summary

This manuscript introduces CKI (ω = k_f/k_n), a ratio-of-divergences index that normalizes functional-gene JS divergence (k_f) against a housekeeping-gene baseline (k_n), and applies it across simulation, mouse/human atlases, TCGA bulk RNA-seq (5 cancer types, 3,567 samples), and a human brain atlas (31,764 cross-region pairs). v49.10 adds an explicit statement that the TCGA confidence intervals are percentile intervals ("95% CIs are the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resampled ratios", Methods) from a sample-level cluster bootstrap with endpoint-product pair reweighting.

The revision is statistically mature in many respects: the block-shuffle null with a pseudo-region negative control is a genuinely design-matched null; the brain screen now leads with its global verdict (expected 148.3 Strong candidates under the null versus 39 observed; minimum q = 0.520); the fixed-panel ablation, the linear-mapping TCGA re-computation declared "the authoritative caliber throughout", the downgraded Bergmann-glia claims, and the small-cluster bootstrap correction with a coverage study are all welcome. My remaining concerns are: (i) the studentized bootstrap-t that now carries several headline intervals is not described well enough to verify or reproduce; (ii) the TCGA headline ("CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers") rests on percentile intervals with no coverage validation, while the only design-matched P-values reported (all P > 0.9, anti-conservative fixed panel) test a different contrast than the reversal; and (iii) one MS-internal numeric inconsistency between Results and Discussion on the composition-adjustment analysis. All are fixable in a minor revision.

---

## Major concerns

### M1. The studentized bootstrap-t is invoked but never defined; the coverage study is not reproducible as described

The brain gradient and two class-mean CIs now rest on a studentized bootstrap-t: "the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient is 5.99 (95% CI [4.43, 7.69] under a studentized bootstrap-t region-clustered bootstrap, which attains nominal coverage at 6–7 clusters where the earlier percentile interval [4.12, 9.18] under-covers; Supplementary Note 2)" (MS Results), and "Bergmann glia [5.76, 28.59] (studentized bootstrap-t; the earlier percentile interval under-covers at 7 clusters; Supplementary Note 2)" (MS Results). However, neither the MS, the SI, nor the Guide defines:

1. **The pivot.** What is studentized — (θ* − θ̂)/SE*(θ*)? Is the interval of the form θ̂ ∓ t*·SE(θ̂)? For the gradient, which is a *ratio* of two class means with very different cluster counts (astrocytes, 108 regions; Bergmann glia, 7 regions), the variance estimator is non-obvious.
2. **The standard-error estimator.** Is SE* obtained from a nested (double) bootstrap, a jackknife-after-bootstrap, or a delta-method/analytic SE? Supplementary Note 2 states only: "0.953/0.951 for the studentized bootstrap-t, which is therefore the recommended replacement for every statistic resting on ≤ 7 region clusters (seed 20260905; B = 5,000 on the real data)."
3. **The Monte Carlo DGP.** The coverage study is described in one sentence: "A Monte Carlo coverage study (2,000 simulations × B = 999; nominal 95%) gives coverage 0.876 (7 clusters) and 0.873 (6 clusters) for the percentile interval, 0.816/0.806 for the wild (Rademacher) cluster bootstrap … and 0.953/0.951 for the studentized bootstrap-t" (SI Note 2). What data-generating process was simulated? A cluster bootstrap-t's coverage is pivotal-dependent; if the DGP does not reproduce the skewness (brain ω skewness = 2.22, excess kurtosis = 6.02 per MS Discussion) and the within-cluster dependence of the real ω landscape, the claimed 0.953/0.951 coverage does not transport to the real intervals. The DGP family, its parameters, and which statistic(s) were covered must be stated.
4. **Seed registry.** SI Note 2 reports "seed 20260905" for this study, but the MS states "random seeds were fixed at 42 throughout, except scripts 77/78/79, which used seed 20260903" and the Guide repeats the same exception list (Section 1.4; checklist item "Verify random seed = 42 in all analysis scripts (fixed exceptions: notebooks 77/78/79 use seed 20260903)"). The coverage-study seed (notebooks/89_cluster_boot_v45.py, seed 20260905) is absent from both, and the 777000 permutation-base seed appears only in the Guide.

A related internal tension: SI Note 2 recommends the studentized interval "for every statistic resting on ≤ 7 region clusters", yet the Bergmann-glia *joint calibrated* interval — "Bergmann glia ω_cal = 1.49, joint 95% CI [0.99, 2.12], no longer excluding 1" (MS Results), produced by the joint region-clustered bootstrap of notebooks/81 (B = 5,000) — appears to remain percentile-based and is not among the replacements listed in Note 2. Since the Bergmann numerator rests on 7 region clusters, this interval presumably inherits the ~0.88 coverage documented in the same Note. The conclusion "no longer excluding 1" (which drives the textual downgrade) could itself be sensitive to the interval type. Please clarify and, if percentile, replace.

**Required:** define the pivot and SE estimator, describe the coverage DGP, reconcile the seed statements, and apply (or explicitly exempt) the studentized correction for all ≤ 7-cluster statistics including the per-class calibrated ω_cal joint CIs.

### M2. TCGA headline inference: percentile CIs for a ratio of means of ratios, no coverage validation, and the design-matched P-values test a different contrast

The headline TCGA result is interval-based: "NN pairs were more divergent than TT pairs—the ratio of mean NN to mean TT ω ranked LUAD 2.46 (95% CI 2.13–2.86; sample-level cluster bootstrap, B = 1,000), KIRC 1.88 (1.63–2.16), LUSC 1.71 (1.38–2.09), BRCA 1.57 (1.34–1.84), and LIHC 1.10 (0.93–1.29)—with the bootstrap CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers" (MS Results). Three issues:

1. **Percentile is the weakest defensible choice for this statistic.** The estimand is a ratio of means of per-pair ratios; the pair-level ω is itself a heavy-tailed ratio estimator (SI Note 4: "The null distribution is heavy-tailed (skew 2.28, excess kurtosis 9.14, P99/P50 = 2.1)"). Percentile bootstrap intervals are only first-order accurate and carry no bias or skewness correction; for a ratio statistic with skewed components, BCa or studentized intervals are standard practice. The manuscript's own coverage study (SI Note 2) demonstrates 7–8 points of under-coverage for the percentile cluster bootstrap in the brain small-cluster case, yet no coverage validation, BCa sensitivity, or even a seed-stability check is offered for the TCGA intervals that carry the "four of five" headline. This matters most for the borderline intervals: LIHC 1.10 [0.93, 1.29] and the k_n component LIHC 1.35 [1.02, 1.88], whose lower bounds sit near 1. B = 1,000 also gives non-trivial Monte Carlo error on the 2.5th/97.5th percentiles — notably looser than the B = 10,000 used for the pair-level bootstrap CIs elsewhere (MS Statistics: "Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals use B = 10,000 resamples").

2. **Dyadic dependence.** The design — "tumor and normal samples were resampled with replacement independently, each pair reweighted by the product of its endpoint resampling weights, and the ratio recomputed per resample" (MS Methods) — is the correct resampling unit (samples, not pairs) and does propagate the dependence arising from each sample participating in "median 4–11 pairs per tumor across cancer types" (MS Methods). I consider the dyadic-dependence question adequately handled at the design level. However, endpoint-product weights are quadratic in the resampling multiplicities and hence heavy-tailed; an influence/jackknife diagnostic showing no single sample drives the LUAD 2.46 estimate would strengthen the claim, particularly given the seeded TT subsampling (2,000 pairs per cancer type), which the authors themselves note carries "a Monte-Carlo error of roughly 0.01–0.02 ratio units" (MS Methods).

3. **The permutation P-values do not test the reversal.** The only design-matched P-values in the TCGA analysis come from the per-cancer label-permutation test, which "permutes sample labels (tumor vs. normal)" (MS Discussion) and therefore tests the *aggregate tumor-versus-normal* contrast, not the NN-versus-TT ratio that constitutes the headline. That these are "all P > 0.9" under a null that is self-declared anti-conservative ("The TCGA per-cancer permutation test additionally holds the identity gene set fixed across permutations (a global HVG panel selected once on the observed grouping); because this fixed-panel null can only deflate the null k_f relative to re-selection, it is anti-conservative, and the corresponding TCGA P-values (all P > 0.9) should be read with this caveat", MS Methods) is consistent with the reversal direction but provides no inferential support *for* the NN/TT claim. The anti-conservatism disclosure itself is exemplary; the gap is that no design-matched test of the reversal contrast exists. A permutation test on the NN/TT ratio (e.g., permuting tumor/normal labels and recomputing both group means, or a cluster-bootstrap-based two-sided P from the resampled ratios) would close this. At minimum, the text should state explicitly that the reversal claim is CI-based only. Relatedly, the five exact per-cancer permutation P-values and their m = 5 BH-adjusted q-values are not tabulated anywhere in the SI text (the Guide points to results/tcga_bootstrap_results.csv and a q_value column); please add them to Supplementary Table 11.

**Required:** (i) BCa or studentized sensitivity (or a small coverage simulation) for the five NN/TT intervals and the k_n component intervals, with attention to LIHC; (ii) either a design-matched test of the NN/TT contrast or an explicit statement that the claim is CI-based; (iii) tabulate the five permutation P/q values.

### M3. Internal inconsistency: Discussion reports the superseded (softmax-caliber) composition-adjustment numbers

The MS declares the linear re-computation authoritative: "this linear re-computation is the authoritative caliber throughout" (MS Results). The Results composition check accordingly reports the linear-caliber values: "the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged after composition adjustment (−1.2%, bootstrap 95% CI −4.1% to +2.6%; per cancer type −16% to +37%)" (MS Results), matching SI Note 8's linear update ("pooled four-panel attenuation −0.8% (cluster-bootstrap median −1.2%, 95% CI [−4.1%, +2.6%]); per-cancer attenuation LIHC +37.1% … BRCA −16.2% …"). But the Discussion still reports the earlier softmax-caliber run: "the tumor-pair coefficient attenuates by −0.5% pooled (95% CI [−3.2%, +2.6%]), with marked per-cancer heterogeneity (BRCA −14.0%, LUSC −9.7%, LUAD −2.3%, KIRC +19.6%, LIHC +33.5%)" (MS Discussion) — values that match the superseded v41 analysis archived in SI Note 8 ("The reversal replicates exactly …") and Guide Section 5.7a. Two different vintages of the same statistic now appear in the same manuscript, one declared authoritative. The Discussion paragraph must be updated to the linear-caliber numbers or explicitly labelled as the sensitivity caliber.

## Minor concerns

1. **Ratio-bias characterization vs. its use in cross-class comparisons.** SI Note 4: "the bias is small and null-calibrated: median +0.2% across the 29 class × region groups … The largest upward bias sits in the smallest-denominator bin (k_n < 10⁻⁴: +6.5%; 10⁻⁴ ≤ k_n < 10⁻³: −8.3%; k_n ≥ 10⁻³: +1.5%) … this bias is absorbed into the baseline." Two gaps: (a) the largest *absolute* bin bias is actually the middle bin's −8.3%, which the text does not discuss; (b) "absorbed into the baseline" holds for calibrated ω_cal (same estimator, same k_n scale), but not for raw cross-pair or cross-class ω contrasts, where per-pair k_n spans the bins (CV = 97.52%) and class-mean k_n correlates with class size (ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043; MS Results). A class whose k_n sits mostly in the middle bin is differentially deflated relative to a high-k_n class by up to ~10 points. Please quantify (or bound) the contribution of bin-dependent ratio bias to the 6.10-fold class gradient, alongside the size-imbalance explanation already given.

2. **Brain screen framing — mostly adequate, two residual notes.** The global verdict now leads ("the expected number of Strong candidates is 148.3—the 39 observed lie 3.8-fold below the null expectation (P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0), and no candidate survives FDR correction (minimum q = 0.520)", MS Results), the tier–P coupling is disclosed ("the tier variable … and the per-pair permutation P-values are both monotone functions of the same observed and null ω values, so the tier-graded raw-P enrichment is partly guaranteed by construction", SI Note 13), and the resolution limit is stated ("q < 0.05 is unattainable unless ~635 of the 31,764 P-values sit at the permutation floor", MS Statistics; the arithmetic checks out: 9.99×10⁻⁴ × 31,764/0.05 ≈ 635). The "prioritized hypothesis list" label is therefore defensible. Residual: (a) the cell-type-stratified BH result — "the intra-cerebellar Bergmann-glia family (m = 21; minimum q = 0.042)" (MS Results) — is one of ten stratified families; the multiplicity of families is unaddressed, and q = 0.042 should stay explicitly nominal (the SI does say "nominally significant under stratified BH", SI Section 3.3; keep the same hedging in the MS); (b) BH across 31,764 dyadically dependent pairs is valid under PRDS but not arbitrary dependence (BY); the conclusion is insensitive here (min q = 0.520) but a one-line justification should be added.

3. **Multiple-testing family definitions.** Per-dataset families (m = 5 TCGA; m = 10 brain classes; m = 31,764 brain screen; m = 17 human; m = 15 mouse) with "no cross-family joint correction" (MS Discussion) are reasonable and transparently disclosed. The opposite-direction split (upper-tail class tests versus lower-tail screen) is correctly reported as separate families. The five TCGA CIs are unadjusted across cancer types; simultaneous (e.g., Bonferroni) intervals would be wider — the LIHC interval already includes 1, so only the framing matters; the exploratory label ("with 5 cancer types the TCGA results are exploratory", MS Discussion) largely covers this.

4. **LIHC Cox analysis (demoted to SI) — appropriate handling, minor reporting gaps.** The demotion of the null survival result (HR per SD 1.075 [0.882, 1.309], P = 0.475) to Supplementary Table 14 is appropriate. SI Section 3.13 describes the models well ("Cox proportional-hazards regression (statsmodels PHReg) with per-tumor ω standardized to unit SD … adjusted for AJCC stage (I–IV), Edmondson grade (G1–G4), age, and sex … with k_f-only, k_n-only, and tumor-normal-ω exposures as sensitivity models"). Missing: number of events, the proportional-hazards assumption check (e.g., Schoenfeld residuals), and the HR/CI/P values themselves in the supplied SI text. Please add events and the PH diagnostic to Table 14.

5. **Guide/MS consistency.** (a) Guide Section 5.7c still quotes the superseded percentile gradient interval "95% CI [4.12, 9.18] under the joint region-clustered bootstrap" without cross-referencing the studentized replacement [4.43, 7.69] in Section 5.9b; a reader following the Guide linearly will take the stale interval as current. (b) The seed registry issue under M1(4) also applies to the Guide checklist.

6. **Supplementary tables.** The supplied SI text contains legend sections only for Supplementary Tables 1–4; Tables 5–19 appear only in the Table of Contents. If the submitted SI indeed ships Tables 5–19 without legends, please add them (several — e.g., Table 11 NN/TT ratios, Table 14 Cox models, Table 19 threshold sensitivity — carry numbers discussed in the text).

7. **Bergmann-glia null-mean interpretation.** MS Results: "the Bergmann-glia block-shuffle null mean (21.9) lying far above its split-half baseline (9.7) reflects precisely this within-donor, within-region technical divergence" — a nice coherence point; consider stating the ratio (2.3-fold) and noting it is class-specific so readers do not generalize it to the other nine classes.

## Questions to authors

1. What is the exact studentized pivot and SE estimator behind [4.43, 7.69], [5.76, 28.59], and [25.93, 76.30]? Double bootstrap, jackknife-after-bootstrap, or analytic?
2. What DGP generated the 2,000 coverage simulations, and does it reproduce the skewness/dependence of the real region-clustered ω landscape? Which statistic(s) were covered?
3. Is the Bergmann-glia joint calibrated-ω CI [0.99, 2.12] percentile or studentized? If percentile, please recompute it under the studentized scheme your own Note 2 recommends.
4. Which contrast do the TCGA per-cancer permutation P-values (all P > 0.9) test — the aggregate tumor-vs-normal ω or the NN/TT ratio? Please provide a design-matched test of the NN/TT ratio itself, or state explicitly that the reversal claim is CI-based, and tabulate the five P/q values.
5. Can you provide a BCa or studentized sensitivity for the five NN/TT intervals (especially LIHC 1.10 [0.93, 1.29] and LIHC k_n 1.35 [1.02, 1.88]), or a small coverage simulation of the endpoint-weighted sample-level cluster bootstrap?
6. Why is the middle k_n bin's −8.3% ratio bias (larger in magnitude than the +6.5% "largest" bin emphasized in Note 4) not discussed, and does bin-differential bias affect the class-level gradient?
7. Please reconcile the seed statements: MS ("except scripts 77/78/79 … 20260903"), Guide (adds 777000), and SI Note 2 (seed 20260905).
8. Cox LIHC: how many events, and was the proportional-hazards assumption checked?
9. Please justify BH validity for the m = 31,764 dependent-pair family (PRDS argument) in one line.

## Score: 7/10

## Recommendation: **Minor Revision**

The statistical infrastructure (design-matched block-shuffle null with pseudo-region calibration, cluster-aware bootstraps with coverage-driven interval replacement, extensive honest sensitivity analyses) is strong and, in places, exemplary for this journal. The revision can be accepted after: (M1) the studentized bootstrap-t is defined and its coverage study made reproducible, including the seed registry; (M2) the TCGA interval inference is hardened (BCa/studentized sensitivity) and the contrast tested by the permutation P-values is clarified; (M3) the Discussion's superseded composition-adjustment numbers are corrected to the authoritative linear caliber. None of these require new experiments; all are documentation or bounded re-computation.
