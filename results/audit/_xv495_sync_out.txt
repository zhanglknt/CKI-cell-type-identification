We applied CKI to TCGA bulk RNA-seq data across five cancer types (LUAD, LUSC, LIHC, KIRC, BRCA) 10,11, totalling 3,567 samples, and asked how tissue-level functional divergence is reorganized when cancer develops. Because bulk RNA-seq averages over tumor, stromal, and immune compartments, ω here quantifies divergence between tissue states rather than between cell types; we therefore compare tumor–tumor (TT), normal–normal (NN), and tumor–normal (TN) pairs within each cancer type (Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. 4). A consistent pan-cancer reversal emerged: in all five cancer types, NN pairs were more divergent than TT pairs—the ratio of mean NN to mean TT ω ranked LUAD 2.46 (95% CI 2.13–2.86; sample-level cluster bootstrap, B = 1,000), KIRC 1.88 (1.63–2.16), LUSC 1.71 (1.38–2.09), BRCA 1.57 (1.34–1.84), and LIHC 1.10 (0.93–1.29)—with the bootstrap CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers (LIHC CI including 1, direction consistent in all five; pair-level Mann-Whitney P-values, which ignore dyadic dependence between pairs, are reported as descriptive only in Section 3.13 of the Supplementary Information). Component decomposition identified the mechanism: in every cancer type the housekeeping baseline was elevated in tumors—TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 1.3–3.3-fold (mean ratios; 95% CIs excluding 1 in all five cancers, e.g. KIRC 3.29 [2.54, 4.35], LIHC 1.35 [1.02, 1.88])—whereas TT k_f was equal to or higher than NN k_f (e.g. LUAD 0.282 versus 0.226). The reversal therefore reflects a more stable housekeeping baseline among adjacent non-tumor specimens, not smaller functional-gene divergence of tumor cells (Fig. 5a).
