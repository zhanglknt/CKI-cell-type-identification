## Summary

This is the fourth-round version of a computational-methods manuscript proposing CKI, a Ka/Ks-inspired ratio (ω = k_f/k_n) that normalizes functional-gene divergence against a housekeeping baseline for single-cell/bulk transcriptomic comparisons. The package combines: (i) the index itself with an explicit split-half calibration (ω baseline 7.70 [7.37, 8.02]) and design-matched permutation nulls; (ii) ground-truth simulation in two backgrounds (1,750 replicates each; AUC 0.80 / 0.91); (iii) real-data neutral-drift calibration on technical replicates (Kang 30 cross-lane pairs 0/30; brain drift ladder T1 FPR 28.6%, lowest among continuous metrics); (iv) a TCGA pan-cancer application (3,567 samples, five cancers) with a denominator-localized NN/TT reversal and an admixture-robust KRAS/EGFR driver decomposition in LUAD; and (v) a brain-atlas regional analysis (31,764 pairs) whose headline gradient is now triply qualified (6.10 uncorrected / 1.74 size-balanced [1.64, 1.84] / 3.68 span-matched).

From the editor's chair, v49.13 is the first version in this series that reads as *finished*. The three quantitative pillars in the cover letter match the manuscript numbers exactly; every headline claim I traced is either FDR-controlled or explicitly downgraded to hypothesis-generating; the limitations are not buried but integrated into the Results. The claim de-escalation trajectory (6.10 → 1.74 → 3.68; candidate screen → null expectation 148.3 vs 39 observed, min q = 0.520) costs the paper some headline punch but buys it defensibility, which at NC is the right trade for a methods article.

**Recommendation: Accept (pending three trivial technical fixes listed under Minor, verifiable at copy-edit without re-review). Score: 8.8/10.**

## Scope fit (Nature Communications)

- **Methods + multi-dataset application, broad interest: met.** The manuscript is a method, but not a bare method: it is validated across mouse (Tabula Muris), human (Tabula Sapiens), a ~3.3M-nucleus brain atlas, TCGA bulk (5 cancers), and a perturbation cohort (Kang IFN-β), with competitor context (MELD, scDist approximation, Augur). This crosses the NC bar of "methodological advance demonstrated on large-scale real data with biological insight" — the LUAD driver-class decomposition and the pan-cancer reversal give the cancer-genomics readership a concrete entry point, and the brain regional analysis gives the neuroscience-atlas community one.
- **Honest-positioning check: passed.** The Introduction states the central anchoring assumption once and quantifies its failure modes throughout (S1 functional-on-HK blind spot, N1/N2 drift controls, anchor-visibility boundary in the IFN-β demo). The Ka/Ks analogy is framed as heuristic, with the MK-style fourth-term correspondence explicitly carrying its caveats. No residual "conceptual shift" rhetoric — Discussion line 1 self-describes as "a recombination of established ideas," which is the correct register.
- **Residual scope caveat (not actionable):** the method's demonstrated value is specificity-first screening; ordering claims are repeatedly deferred to k_f with a design-matched null. A reader could ask whether ω itself earns its keep on real data beyond its numerator. The authors pre-empt this (Discussion: "the ratio earns its increment over k_f under controlled ground truth... on real-data orderings the increment is not established"). I judge this disclosure sufficient; it is a scope statement, not a flaw requiring revision.

## Minor issues

1. **Orphan / inconsistently formatted references.** Ref. 39 (Bakken et al., comparative motor cortex) is listed but never cited anywhere in the main text, legends, or Methods (verified by full-text search; the cross-species material in Supplementary Fig. 11 would be its natural anchor). Ref. 15 (Liberzon et al., MSigDB Hallmark) is referenced only as author–year "(Liberzon et al. 2015; see References)" inside Data Availability — inconsistent with the numbered style used for all other 56 references. Fix: cite ref. 39 at the Supplementary Fig. 11 mention or delete it; convert the Data-Availability mention of ref. 15 to a numbered citation.
2. **Supplementary Tables 5–19 are never cited from the main text.** The main text cites Supplementary Tables 1–4 and the SI-availability statement lists Tables 1–19; Tables 5–19 are cited only inside the SI. NC production prefers a main-text pointer (a single sentence in Methods or the TCGA/brain Results paragraphs, e.g. "full group statistics in Supplementary Tables 11–17"). One-line fix.
3. **A handful of threshold-format P-values where exact values are available.** Most P-values are reported exactly or at the permutation floor (which is properly justified), but "all P < 0.001" (metric–ω correlations, same-organ reversal Mann-Whitney) and the Discussion's "P < 10⁻³⁰⁰" could carry exact values per NC statistical-reporting guidance. Cosmetic.
4. **ORCID present only for the corresponding author.** NC encourages ORCID for all authors; the first author's is absent. Copy-edit item.

No other issues. I specifically looked for residual overclaim in the cover letter ("the first...") — the v49.13 phrasing ("the first per-comparison, design-testable implementation of a principled separation", with "to our knowledge") is calibrated and matches the Discussion's self-positioning. The CL is one page, opens with "Dear Editors,", and every number in it cross-checks against MS/SI (0.00 vs 0.55–0.58; AUC 0.80; 0/30; 36.7%/23.3%; 28.6% vs 35.7–45.2%; 1.04→1.76→1.80 vs 1.07→3.32→2.98; 1.10–2.46; 1.3–3.3-fold; 3,567 samples). One CL nicety: "1,750 replicates" is the per-background count (two backgrounds were run); the current wording is not wrong but "1,750 replicates per background across two backgrounds" would be tighter.
