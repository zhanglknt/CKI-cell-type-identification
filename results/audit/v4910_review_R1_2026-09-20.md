# Reviewer Report 1 (R1) — Single-Cell Methodology

**Manuscript:** CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics (v49.10, revised)
**Journal:** Nature Communications (Methodology)
**Reviewer role:** senior single-cell genomics methodologist
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Note:** This review judges the current (v49.10) text only. The two issues from the prior round — (i) the scDist execution question and (ii) ω denominator-dominance — are now handled by explicit disclosures ("the scDist results reported in this work were obtained with a Python approximation of scDist (the original is an R package and was not run here)"; "In the real data analyzed here, ω orderings were predominantly denominator-driven (brain class-mean ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.73; …)"). Those disclosures are verified present and adequate as disclosures; the concerns below concern what they reveal.

---

## Summary

The authors propose CKI, a ratio index ω = k_f/k_n of Jensen–Shannon divergences computed on identity genes (per-pair top-200 DE) versus a housekeeping anchor (HRT Atlas v1.0, 1,130 genes), evaluated on pseudobulks. The revision is unusually candid: it now contains adversarial simulations (functional-signal-on-HK, non-HK-anchored neutral drift, gene-identity swaps), a fixed-panel circularity ablation, a real-data drift ladder on technical replicates with per-pair n-matched nulls, a pseudo-region negative control for the block-shuffle null, and component (k_f/k_n) controls for every ordering claim. The prior reviewer's two complaints are resolved textually.

However, the cumulative effect of the authors' own controls is that the headline metric is denominator-dominated in every real-data ordering, structurally blind to anchor-located signal, and power-limited to an operating window (~50–200 cells) that excludes the atlas-scale pseudobulks on which the flagship analyses are run. The remaining single-cell-methodology foundations — pseudobulk aggregation and normalization choices, HK-anchor validation in snRNA-seq, and donor confounding in the Tabula Sapiens cross-organ analysis — are under-built relative to this candor. Several internal numeric inconsistencies persist across the main text (MS), Supplementary Information (SI), and Reproducibility Guide.

---

## Major concerns

**M1. The paper's own controls establish that ω's real-data orderings are denominator-driven; the value of ω over k_f + a design-matched null is not demonstrated on single-cell data.**
The Discussion concedes: "In the real data analyzed here, ω orderings were predominantly denominator-driven (brain class-mean ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.73; TCGA NN/TT inversion and the supplementary stratification vignettes predominantly denominator effects; the LUAD KRAS gradient as the k_f-stable exception; cross-organ cell-type ranking not reproduced under k_f-only; Supplementary Note 9), so the honest default for ordering claims is k_f plus a design-matched null, with ω reserved for its specificity-first property." The specifics are devastating to the ratio as an ordering statistic: the cross-organ ranking gives "per-cell-type mean ω and mean k_f are only weakly concordant (Spearman r = 0.23 …; r = 0.10 among the five well-sampled types)" and "NK cells rank most divergent under ω, second-most conserved under k_f" (Supplementary Note 9); the LIHC Edmondson and BRCA PAM50 gradients "reverse under k_f alone" and are demoted to "denominator-dominated vignettes" (Supplementary Note 9); and on the Kang perturbation "k_f alone separated perturbation from donor drift as well or better (rank AUC: ω 0.55–0.92, k_f 0.74–1.00, raw JS 0.79–1.00)," with "in CD14+ monocytes ω AUC fell to 0.55 while k_f retained 0.98." On the technical-replicate calibration the authors admit "k_f alone was likewise 0 of 30 — on this dataset ω adds no increment over its numerator." What is left is drift specificity — where marker Jaccard "separate[s] the tiers at least as well as ω (T3−T1 FPR gap 0.60 versus 0.56; T3/T1 ratio 4.0 versus 3.0)" at T1 FPR 19.9% versus ω's 28.6% — and the TCGA KRAS/EGFR decomposition, which is bulk RNA-seq and thus outside the stated single-cell scope. A methods paper must articulate at least one single-cell decision that ω changes relative to k_f with a design-matched null; currently none survives the authors' own analyses.

**M2. The scope argument (v49.10) does not fully cohere with the analyses it is meant to frame, although the AUC 0.786 reconciliation clause does resolve the narrow labeling tension.**
The new Discussion states: "CKI is designed to detect state changes within a given cell type, not to discriminate cell-type identity. Because the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific—housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types—the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type." The SI reconciliation clause reads: "the pure identity gene configuration (w1=1.0, w2=w3=0.0) achieved optimal cell type discrimination (AUC = 0.786, n = 703 mouse cell-type pairs); this was therefore adopted as the default scheme (a gene-set configuration criterion only; CKI itself is not designed for cell-type discrimination—see the main-text Discussion)." This clause does defuse the surface contradiction (optimizing a cell-type-discrimination AUC for a method disclaiming that purpose), and AUC 0.786 is itself consistent with "limited cross-type discrimination." Two residual problems: (i) the k_f gene-set configuration was chosen using an out-of-scope objective; no scope-appropriate objective (within-type perturbation detection) was ever swept; (ii) the scope claim that "the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type" directly undercuts the two cross-type headline results — the brain class gradient ("Mean ω spanned a 6.10-fold gradient across cell classes"; abstract: "1.74-fold size-balanced regional gradient") and the cross-organ conservation ranking across 17 cell types (Table 1) — both of which compare class-mean ω (hence class-mean k_n) across types. The hedges ("cross-type gaps in mean ω should be read as descriptive rather than calibrated differences") do not reconcile a headline claim with a design principle that predicts it is invalid.

**M3. Pseudobulk aggregation and normalization lack robustness checks, and the stated "+1 pseudo-count" equivalence is mathematically inexact for the mouse and human pipelines.**
The brain pipeline normalizes at the pseudobulk level: "Pseudobulks were computed as cell-count-weighted means of per-library (10x sample) mean expression vectors per group, then normalized (Scanpy normalize_total, target_sum = 10,000) followed by log1p at the pseudobulk level" (Methods). The Methods correctly disclose the aggregation-order split: "the brain pipeline applies log1p after averaging counts, softmax(log1p(mean counts)), matching the count-space formula exactly, whereas the mouse (Tabula Muris pilot) and human (Tabula Sapiens) pipelines average per-cell log1p values and normalize afterwards, softmax(mean(log1p))." But the general claim made in the same Methods section and in SI 1.1 — that the softmax "in count space is exactly the operation of adding a +1 pseudo-count followed by L1 normalization: p_i = (c_i + 1)/Σ_j(c_j + 1)" — is exact only for the brain/TCGA order. For mouse/human, the pseudobulk is a mean of log1p values, so softmax(mean(log1p(x))) L1-normalizes the geometric mean of (x+1) across cells — a strictly more peaked mapping that downweights genes heterogeneous across cells. Algorithm 1 (step 4 comment: "p_i = exp(log1p(c_i)) / sum_j exp(log1p(c_j)) = (c_i+1) / sum_j (c_j+1)") repeats the inexact identification. The authors speculate "This difference plausibly contributes, alongside dataset scale and gene-selection effects, to the non-transferability of the dataset-specific calibration constants" — a directly testable claim left untested. No single-cell analysis was repeated under an alternative normalization or aggregation (e.g., standard sum-of-counts pseudobulks, sqrt/SCT-style transforms); the linear-mapping recheck was performed only for TCGA. Cell-count effects are only probed via the simulation's fourfold-imbalance scenario and the drift-ladder size-binned FPRs; there is no analysis of pseudobulk standard error versus n for the k_n estimator itself.

**M4. The Tabula Sapiens cross-organ conservation ranking (Fig. 5, Table 1) is donor-confounded by construction.**
Methods state: "one pseudobulk was computed per cell-type entry from its largest donor (the donor with the most QC-passing cells); the method comparison therefore does not capture inter-donor variability." Each of the 59 cross-organ pairs therefore compares single donors resident in different organs. Yet Results claim "CD8+ T cells (mean ω = 9.93 ± 4.62, n = 6) and Plasma cells (mean ω = 10.20 ± 2.03, n = 6) were the most conserved." The authors' own brain drift ladder shows that donor-level drift alone drives ω to fire on "90.9% versus 98.4–98.7%" of T2 pairs — i.e., donor identity is a first-order driver of ω at exactly this magnitude scale. No donor-matched design, donor-drift tier, or multi-donor replication exists for Tabula Sapiens, and the k_f-only control (Supplementary Note 9) does not address it. The entire ranking (including the "most conserved" claim repeated in the abstract-level narrative) needs a donor-controlled redesign or an explicit downgrade.

**M5. The stated operating window contradicts the flagship application.**
The competitor benchmark concludes: "donor-paired power falls from 0.67–0.93 at 50 cells per donor per condition to ≈ 0 at 500 and above (operating window ~50–200 cells)," and the mechanism is stated in SI 3.11: "the permutation null of ω explodes with pseudobulk size (k_n → 0 in the null while top-200 selection keeps k_f elevated), so observed and null stop separating." Simultaneously, the brain misreporting rate "grows with group size—from 14.1% for pairs with at most 30 nuclei per library to 48.0% for pairs exceeding 500." The brain analysis, the paper's centerpiece, uses region pseudobulks aggregating thousands of nuclei — far outside the window in which ω's inference is claimed to function. The block-shuffle null changes the exchangeability unit (libraries, not cells) and partially rescues class-level inference, but the per-pair ω values feeding the gradient and the residual screen inherit exactly the small-k_n, size-dependent distortion the power analysis identifies. This tension needs explicit quantitative reconciliation, not parallel disclosure.

**M6. The HK anchor's dataset-dependence and snRNA-seq validity remain unvalidated beyond a circular sensitivity check.**
Reported analyses use the HRT Atlas v1.0 panel (good: pre-specified, "all reported analyses use the pre-specified reference"); the data-driven auto-detect (detection > 0.9, CV < 30th percentile) is disclosed as unused. But the only validation offered is "sensitivity analysis with alternative low-variance gene sets (r > 0.95)" / "using the top 10% lowest-variance genes as an alternative constrained set yields ω correlations of r > 0.95" (SI 1.2) — an alternative set defined by low variance in the same data, which is the same circularity criterion in different clothing, and r > 0.95 on ω (a heavy-tailed ratio) is a weak rank-stability statement. No external, independently derived HK panel (e.g., Eisenberg & Levanon HKgDB, cited as ref. 30 but not used) was compared. For the Siletti snRNA-seq data specifically, the anchor's behavior in nuclei (altered cytoplasmic transcript fraction, per-library aggregation of 10x samples) is unexamined; the authors concede "Donor-level PMI and RNA-integrity covariates were not available to us for this atlas, so a direct correlation between PMI and per-pair k_n could not be te[sted]" and that regionally differential degradation "could masquerade as regional structure in k_n." Given that the entire brain result is a k_n-driven class ordering (M1), the anchor's behavior in this modality is the load-bearing wall, and it is the least validated component.

**M7. Internal inconsistencies across MS, SI, and Guide (numeric cross-check).**
Numbers verified consistent across documents: 3,567 TCGA samples; 31,764 brain pairs / 108 regions / 886,808 nuclei; 4,851 analyzed Tabula Sapiens pairs from 99/102 entries (the Guide correctly explains "the 5,151-row phase33_v3 export is the pre-analysis 102-class inventory"); Strong-tier arithmetic (16+10+6+3+2+2 = 39); NN/TT ratios 1.10–2.46; Kang 0/30 vs 36.7%/23.3%; T1 FPR 28.6% vs 45.2% raw JS; gradient 6.10 → 1.74 (equal-n); AUC 0.804/0.908; simulation power figures. The following are inconsistent:
- **Min-cells filter:** SI 1.6: "Pseudobulk: mu = column-wise mean of X_log for all cells with the same cell type annotation, with a minimum of 10 cells per group," vs Methods: "at least 20 cells per entry (pseudobulk group); at least one donor/mouse contributing at least 10 cells" and SI 4.2: "at least 20 cells per entry." Ten or twenty — which produced the 99/102 Tabula Sapiens filters?
- **Gradient-CI provenance contradiction:** MS: "under per-class baselines the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient is 5.99 (95% CI [4.43, 7.69] under a studentized bootstrap-t region-clustered bootstrap, which attains nominal coverage at 6–7 clusters where the earlier percentile interval [4.12, 9.18] under-covers; Supplementary Note 2)" — but SI Note 3 states: "the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient is 5.99 (95% CI [4.12, 9.18] under a joint region-clustered bootstrap that resamples region pairs and split-half populations together, propagating both sources of calibration uncertainty; the narrower i.i.d.-numerator CI [4.86, 7.42] … is anti-conservative)." The identical interval [4.12, 9.18] is presented as the superseded under-covering interval in MS/SI Note 2 and as the recommended joint interval in SI Note 3 (the Guide's Section 5.7c agrees with SI Note 3). The point estimate stands either way, but the provenance of the authoritative interval is contradictory.
- **Threshold sweep text defect:** "thresholds of 10, 20 (reported), 50, and 100 nuclei retain 10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12" — four thresholds, three gradient values; the 100-nuclei gradient is missing.
- **Seed list:** Guide: "All analyses use random seed 42 throughout, with three fixed exceptions: notebooks/77…, 78…, and 79… use the fixed seed 20260903," but Methods/SI report "Random seeds: 20260918 (Kang), 42 (brain)" for the v49 drift-calibration scripts (nc49_pilot_kang_techrep.py). The Guide's exception list is incomplete for the newest analyses.
- **Monotonicity claim vs own numbers:** "ω values increased monotonically with biological distance (Fig. 2b, c)" is contradicted two sentences later by the X category (different type, different organ; range 23.0–31.6) sitting below the D category mean (43.19).

## Minor concerns

1. **Abstract overload.** The abstract's second sentence runs ~150 words with embedded statistics ("ratio 1.10–2.46, CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers—driven by a 1.3–3.3-fold elevated housekeeping baseline…"). It is accurate but unreadable; the specificity claim also appears without its conditionality (see M8 below, framed as a question).
2. **TCGA aggregate saturation.** "aggregate k_n ranged from 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ to 1.9 × 10⁻⁴, so aggregate ω saturates at k_f/10⁻⁴ in 3 of 5 cancer types" — the per-cancer permutation statistic is clipped in a majority of cancers, and that test is also disclosed as anti-conservative ("holds the identity gene set fixed across permutations … the corresponding TCGA P-values (all P > 0.9) should be read with this caveat"). Since the per-cancer test is uninformative on both counts, consider removing it rather than caveating it.
3. **k_n floor asymmetry.** The kn_floor = 1e-4 applies only to TCGA while "all single-cell analyses use the package default kn_floor = 0" with a positivity guard; SI 1.1 reports "only 1 of 31,764 pairs had k_n below 1e-4" in brain, and Supplementary Note 4 shows gradient robustness to small-denominator exclusion (6.10 → 6.52). This is adequately handled; the residual issue is that the same floor logic is not applied to the aggregate TCGA permutation statistics where it bites (minor point 2).
4. **scDist benchmark evidentiary weight.** The approximation is now disclosed in at least four places (Methods, Discussion, SI 3.11), and the disclosure language is exemplary ("absolute scDist distances should be compared against the original implementation before being cited quantitatively"). Still, a benchmark in which MELD "AUCs saturate (0.997–0.9998) with almost no dynamic range" and scDist is an approximation provides weak comparative evidence; running the actual R package on GSE96583 is a half-day task and would close the loop.
5. **Package default vs recommendation.** The package default is auto-detection ("use_reference = False by default," Guide 3.1) while the Discussion recommends the reference panel; consider flipping the default so the tool matches the paper's own guidance.
6. **Fig. 2e AUC bootstrap phrasing.** "bootstrap 95% CI 0.804 [0.777, 0.831]" mixes point estimate and CI notation; state "AUC 0.804, 95% CI [0.777, 0.831]."
7. **Bergmann-glia pair counts.** The reader encounters n = 21 pairs (class mean), 10 pairs (within-donor), 27 pairs (T1 ladder), and 2 split-half populations for the same class across MS/SI; each is correct in context but a summary table of per-class n by analysis would prevent misreading.

## Questions to authors

1. **Value proposition:** Name one single-cell analysis in the paper where ω changes a substantive conclusion relative to k_f with a design-matched null. If none, why should practitioners compute the ratio rather than the numerator?
2. **Operating window:** How do you reconcile "operating window ~50–200 cells" and the n-dependent misreporting (14.1% → 48.0%) with region pseudobulks of thousands of nuclei in the brain analysis? Can you show the class-level block-shuffle results survive a within-window (subsampled) pseudobulk design?
3. **Donor confound (Tabula Sapiens):** Will you rerun the cross-organ ranking with donor-matched or multi-donor pseudobulks (e.g., all donors with ≥100 cells, or donor-paired within-organ references)? Does "CD8+ T cells most conserved" survive?
4. **Aggregation order:** Please quantify the softmax(mean(log1p)) vs softmax(log1p(mean)) discrepancy (geometric- vs arithmetic-mean mappings) on one dataset — e.g., recompute the Tabula Sapiens calibration and split-half baseline under the brain aggregation order. Does the 7.67 → 9.73 baseline gap shrink?
5. **Consistency fixes:** Which min-cells filter (10 or 20) generated the reported Tabula Sapiens pairs? Which interval ([4.43, 7.69] studentized or [4.12, 9.18] joint) is authoritative for the calibrated gradient? Please supply the missing 100-nuclei gradient and update the Guide's seed exceptions.
6. **Anchor validation:** Would results change with an externally derived HK panel (e.g., Eisenberg & Levanon) rather than the data-variance-based alternatives tested? Is any per-library HK detection-rate audit available for the Siletti nuclei?

---

## Score: 5/10

(Strict Nature Communications Methodology bar: exceptional transparency, adversarial self-testing, and reproducibility infrastructure are real strengths; but the headline metric's real-data ordering value is refuted by the authors' own controls, the flagship analyses sit outside the method's stated inference window and scope principle, and key single-cell methodology choices — pseudobulk construction, donor design, anchor validation in snRNA-seq — lack the robustness analysis the rest of the paper demands.)

## Recommendation: **Major Revision**
