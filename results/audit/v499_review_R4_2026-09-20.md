# Review — Nature Communications (Methodology track)
**Manuscript:** "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics"
**Reviewer:** R4 (cancer genomics; TCGA / tumor transcriptomics)
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Scope of this review:** the TCGA pan-cancer application (Fig. 4), its purity/smoking/severity/survival analyses, sample provenance, and MS–SI numeric consistency. The single-cell pillars are commented on only where they bear on the cancer claims.

---

## Summary

The authors propose ω = k_f/k_n, a ratio of Jensen–Shannon divergences computed on identity genes (k_f, per-pair top-200 |Δ| genes) versus housekeeping genes (k_n, HRT Atlas), and apply it to five TCGA cohorts (LUAD, LUSC, KIRC, BRCA, LIHC; 3,567 samples, log2(TPM+1)). The headline cancer result is a "pan-cancer reversal": mean NN/TT ω ratios of LUAD 2.46 [2.13–2.86], KIRC 1.88 [1.63–2.16], LUSC 1.71 [1.38–2.09], BRCA 1.57 [1.34–1.84], LIHC 1.10 [0.93–1.29], with the reversal explicitly attributed to an elevated tumor housekeeping baseline (TT k_n 1.3–3.3× NN k_n), not to reduced functional divergence. A LUAD driver analysis separates KRAS-mutant (robust) from EGFR-mutant (admixture-explained) associations. The manuscript is unusually candid about its weaknesses, but the cancer-genomics arm has a conceptual scope problem, an undisclosed sample-provenance event that plausibly explains the LIHC null, residual composition/batch confounding that the authors' own sensitivity analyses do not exclude, and a set of MS↔SI numeric inconsistencies. I cannot recommend acceptance in present form.

---

## Major concerns

### 1. Category mismatch between the index's stated scope and the TCGA application; the tracked cell type is never identified

The v49.9 Scope statement says: *"CKI is designed to detect state changes within a given cell type… The intended domain of CKI is thus the comparison of biologically matched populations—the same cell type across organs, brain regions, or perturbation states—**as throughout the analyses reported here**"* (MS, Discussion). The italicized claim is false for the TCGA arm. A bulk tumor and its adjacent-normal tissue are not "the same cell type across states"; they are mixtures of epithelial, stromal, and immune compartments in systematically different proportions. The validity of the k_n anchor rests on housekeeping expression being constrained *within a cell type*; under a mixture model the HK pseudobulk is a composition-weighted average, so k_n shifts are expected from composition alone even with zero biological change. The manuscript concedes the resolution problem (*"ω here quantifies divergence between tissue states rather than between cell types"*, Results) but never answers the operative question: **which compartment is CKI tracking in each cancer?** For TT pairs the top-200 |Δ| panel will be dominated by whatever differs most between two bulks — proliferation/epithelial programs for tumor pairs, immune programs for NN pairs — i.e., the "identity genes" of a bulk tissue are not a cell type's identity genes. The authors' own Hallmark enrichment of the LUAD KRAS TT identity panels found *no program surviving correction (all q ≥ 0.24)* and recurrent members "dominated by individually variable, sex-linked and secreted markers" (SI 3.13) — direct evidence that bulk k_f carries no identifiable tumor-intrinsic program. The TCGA arm therefore violates CKI's own anchoring assumption by construction; presenting it as a validation pillar ("3,567 TCGA samples… a consistent pan-cancer reversal", cover letter, pillar three) is not justified without single-cell or deconvolution-based demonstration that the signal localizes to a definable compartment.

### 2. The LIHC null (ω = 1.10 [0.93–1.29]) is fragile, and the 32 re-assigned cancer-cell-line samples are nowhere disclosed

Three independent observations converge on LIHC as the weak link, and the review materials (MS, SI, cover letter) contain **no mention** of the sample-type correction that produced the current LIHC cohort:

- LIHC tumor n = 398, which exceeds the widely used primary-tumor-only LIHC count (~371–377). No sample-type code filtering (01 primary tumor vs. 02/06/07 vs. cell-line-derived samples) is described anywhere; Methods state only *"29 expression-matrix samples were excluded for missing barcode matching or pair coverage"* — an unverifiable catch-all.
- I understand that during pipeline revisions 32 cancer-cell-line (CC) samples previously counted as LUSC were re-assigned to LIHC (v49.2), with a KIRC block regenerated. Cell-line samples are composition-pure (no microenvironment): retaining them as "tumors" injects 32 zero-admixture outliers (~8% of the LIHC tumor set) directly into the k_n/admixture machinery. If they were instead removed, the before/after LIHC and LUSC estimates both change. Either way, this is a material provenance event that must be disclosed, with a sensitivity analysis showing the LIHC NN/TT ratio, the LIHC k_n elevation, and the Edmondson gradient **with the CC samples excluded**.
- The LIHC point estimates are borderline on their own terms: NN/TT ω 1.10 [0.93–1.29] includes 1; the k_n elevation is the weakest of the five (MS: 1.35 [1.02, 1.88]); and LIHC shows by far the largest composition attenuation in the authors' own check (+33.5% [+23.0%, +46.9%] four-panel; +37.1% [+27.1%, +51.5%] under the linear re-analysis, SI Note 8). Scaling the k_n elevation by the authors' own attenuation factor moves the LIHC k_n ratio to ≈ 1.2 with a CI almost certainly spanning 1 — i.e., the LIHC "consistent direction" may be entirely a composition/provenance artifact. The claim that *"direction [is] consistent in all five"* (MS) is doing more work than the data support for LIHC.

A per-cancer sample-type breakdown (with barcodes or at minimum counts by TCGA sample-type code), the exclusion/sensitivity analysis above, and an explicit statement of what happened to the 32 samples are required.

### 3. Purity/composition confounding: ANCOVA on a noisy proxy plus a "high-purity half" check does not settle the question, and the batch axis is untested

- The sole adjustment is an ESTIMATE-style stromal+immune score. The correlations reported (per-tumor k_n vs. admixture, r = −0.23 to −0.42) guarantee *residual* confounding: with a noisy covariate, OLS/ANCOVA adjustment is attenuated (the authors themselves state the attenuation estimates "are lower bounds on the true composition contribution" and that "a marker-panel regression [cannot] exclude composition as the driver", SI Note 8). No consensus-purity estimate (ABSOLUTE/CPE), no per-cancer purity-density diagnostic, and no adjustment of the adjacent-normal arm (normals also vary in composition) is provided.
- The "low-admixture half" robustness (NN/TT rises in 5/5; CI caliber still 4/5: LUAD 2.46→2.86 [2.41–3.45]; LIHC 1.10→1.17, CI still includes 1) is reassuring in direction but is the weakest possible evidentiary tier: a dichotomized subset analysis on the same confounded data.
- More damaging, the per-cancer attenuation is strongly heterogeneous (LIHC +33.5/37.1%, KIRC +18.3/19.6%, BRCA −24/−26.2%, LUAD/LUSC ≈ 0), so the pooled "−0.2% [−3.1%, +2.6%]" figure that the MS foregrounds (*"essentially unchanged"*) masks the fact that in 2 of 5 cancers composition covariates absorb a substantial share of the tumor-pair k_n coefficient. The MS sentence *"The reversal is therefore not an artefact of stromal or immune admixture"* overstates the SI.
- The tissue-source-site/batch axis — a well-known TCGA property whereby tumor and adjacent-normal aliquots differ systematically in source site and processing — is acknowledged (*"we did not test a batch-matched subset"*) but left untested. A shared source site and processing chain is a fully sufficient explanation for why NN pairs have low k_n (similarly handled, similar composition), and the TT elevation may partly track technical strata rather than "housekeeping dysregulation". A TSS-matched or plate-balanced sensitivity is a minimum requirement for a bulk-tissue k_n claim.
- The external healthy reference (GTEx) is deferred to future work; acceptable as a limitation, but it means the "tumor-specific housekeeping elevation" cannot currently be separated from field effects in cancer-patient adjacent tissue.

### 4. The headline reversal has no valid design-matched significance test, and the aggregate test is degenerate in 3 of 5 cancers

The paper's inferential identity is "nulls that respect the experimental design". Yet for TCGA: (i) the per-cancer tumor-vs-normal permutation P-values are *"all P > 0.9"* (MS Methods) and are anti-conservative by construction (fixed identity panel across permutations, so the true P is even higher); (ii) the aggregate statistic itself saturates the TCGA-only denominator floor — *"aggregate k_n ranged from 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ to 1.9 × 10⁻⁴, so aggregate ω saturates at k_f/10⁻⁴ in 3 of 5 cancer types"* (MS Discussion) — i.e., in three cancers the aggregate ω is pinned by an arbitrary numerical guard; (iii) the headline ratios therefore rest solely on a sample-level cluster bootstrap of pair means with no P-value. The floor itself is applied only to TCGA (kn_floor = 1×10⁻⁴) while the severity vignettes were re-run *"at kn_floor = 0"* (SI Note 9) — an unexplained within-paper inconsistency in the denominator guard. For a methodology paper, "the method's own test finds nothing (P > 0.9, floor-saturated), so we report descriptive ratios with bootstrap CIs" should be stated in the Results, not only in Methods/Discussion.

### 5. Severity gradient (LIHC Edmondson 78.2/76.8/77.9/72.6): not percentages, non-monotone, and the quoted P belongs to a different quantity

For the record: 78.2, 76.8, 77.9, 72.6 are **per-tumor mean ω values** (mean of intratumoral TT pairs) across Edmondson grades G1–G4 (288 LIHC tumors), not percentages. Three problems: (i) the trend is non-monotone (G3 77.9 > G2 76.8) and the G1→G4 decline is ≈ 7% relative — a small effect leveraged by n; (ii) the headline P attached to this vignette in the SI, *"k_f increases with grade (JT P = 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁵)"*, is the P for **k_f**, not for the ω gradient (whose JT P is reported as "≈ 0", with the tail direction unstated); the two must not be conflated, and any presentation of "78.2/76.8/77.9/72.6 with JT P = 6.9e-15" misattributes the k_f statistic to the ω gradient; (iii) the grade-specific denominators are unbalanced — SI 3.6 notes Edmondson G4 n = 11 — so the G4 point (72.6, the entire "decline") rests on 11 tumors; a leave-one-grade-out or G1+G2 vs G3+G4 sensitivity is needed to show the trend is not carried by the tiny G4 bin. The authors' own verdict that this is a "denominator-dominated vignette" whose ordering reverses under k_f alone is correct and should further temper any severity-gradient language.

### 6. Smoking analysis: only LUAD, binary ever/never, no stage adjustment, and no direct smoking–ω estimate

- The analysis exists only for LUAD. LUSC — the most smoking-driven of the five cancers, where a smoking-field effect on the reversal is *a priori* most plausible — has no smoking stratification at all. If smoking status does not carry the divergence association (as claimed for LUAD), demonstrating the same in LUSC materially strengthens the pan-cancer claim; its absence is conspicuous.
- Adjustment is a binary ever/never indicator with 13% missingness (427/492 tumors); pack-years (available for 356 patients) were *"too sparse to model"* — so residual confounding by dose remains. The joint model includes age, sex, and admixture but **not stage**; KRAS/EGFR classes can differ in stage distribution, and stage is a standard confounder for any tumor-transcriptomic contrast.
- The smoking coefficient itself (ever vs. never main effect on ω, k_f, k_n) is never reported — only that adjustment leaves the KRAS contrast "essentially unchanged" (Δω +16.8 → +13.9 → +13.6). If smoking genuinely has no main effect, say so with the estimate; if it does, the KRAS–smoking enrichment (94% vs. 86%/63%, χ² = 30.3, P = 2.7×10⁻⁷) makes partial mediation plausible.
- EGFR n = 61 (~53 with smoking data) is thin for a covariate-adjusted null claim; the "EGFR dissolved under purity adjustment" conclusion (all adjusted P > 0.4) is a non-significance result in a small subgroup and should be framed as absence-of-evidence.

### 7. Survival analysis: appropriately handled, but tested only in the weakest cancer

The null Cox result (per-SD ω HR 1.07 [0.88–1.31], P = 0.48; adjusted for stage, grade, age, sex) is reported with suitable restraint in MS and SI — this is the correct way to handle a null. Two questions remain: (i) why only LIHC — the cohort whose reversal CI already includes 1? A prognostic test in a cancer where the signal exists (e.g., LUAD or KIRC) is the more informative experiment, and restricting to LIHC looks like selecting the analysis most likely to be "safe"; (ii) Supplementary Table 14 (the Cox model set, including the k_f/k_n/TN-ω sensitivity models, n, and events) is referenced but its contents are absent from the SI as provided — the listwise-deletion n and event count are unverifiable.

### 8. MS↔SI numeric inconsistencies (cross-check results)

1. **k_n elevation, MS vs. SI Note 8.** MS Results: *"TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 1.3–3.3-fold (mean ratios… e.g. KIRC 3.29 [2.54, 4.35], LIHC 1.35 [1.02, 1.88])"*. SI Note 8: *"The reversal replicates exactly (median TT/NN k_n ratio 2.18, 2.53, 2.18, 3.70, and 2.79 for LUAD, LUSC, LIHC, KIRC, and BRCA)"*. "Replicates exactly" is not compatible with 1.35 vs. 2.18 (LIHC) and 3.29 vs. 3.70 (KIRC). If the distinction is ratio-of-means vs. median-of-ratios on different pair tables (35,306 vs. 25,306 pairs), define the two statistics explicitly and harmonize the language; the LIHC discrepancy (1.35 vs. 2.18) is too large to wave through.
2. **Cross-organ ranking text vs. its own numbers (Table 1).** MS: *"CD8+ T cells… and Plasma cells… were the most conserved, followed by Neutrophils and NK cells"*. The reported means are CD8 9.93, Plasma 10.20, Neutrophil 14.22, Macrophage 15.49, **NK 17.28** — NK is the *most divergent* well-sampled type, not one that "follows" the conserved ones; SI Note 9 itself says "NK cells rank most divergent under ω". Direct MS–SI contradiction.
3. **LUAD k_f adjusted P.** Fig. 4 legend: *"adjusted P = 0.009 and 0.029"* for the KRAS k_f contrast; the MS text reports only P = 0.009 (admixture model) and states both components are "likewise retained" in the joint model without a value. Supplementary Table 17 (contents absent from the SI as provided) presumably carries 0.029 — state it in text.
4. **kn_floor inconsistency.** Main TCGA pipeline kn_floor = 1×10⁻⁴; severity vignettes re-run at kn_floor = 0 (SI Note 9), shifting ω levels "upward by about 5–20". One floor policy, justified, should apply throughout.
5. **Cross-reference errors.** SI Note 9 refers to the cross-organ ranking as "Table 2 / Fig. 5" — the manuscript's Table 1 is that ranking. SI 3.12 twice points the drift-ladder replication to "main-text Fig. 4d/4b,c" — the Kang and ladder panels are Fig. 3d and Fig. 3b,c (Fig. 4 is the TCGA figure).
6. **SI tables absent.** Supplementary Tables 5–19 are listed in the TOC but their contents are not in the SI document provided (only CSV pointers). For a review copy this blocks verification of Tables 11–18 (NN/TT ratios, driver-group means, Cox models, purity sensitivity, smoking adjustment, severity gradients).

## Minor concerns

1. The "paired tumor-normal" analysis has n = 2–5 per cancer type and is descriptive only; consider dropping it rather than carrying an uninterpretable stratum.
2. Per-tumor ω is a mean over pairs that include tumors of *other* driver classes ("group means are not formed from disjoint pair sets"; median 4–21 pairs/tumor). The within-group bootstrap does not model this cross-class pair sharing; a disjoint-pairs sensitivity (class-restricted TT pairs only) would strengthen the KRAS result.
3. The abstract's framing "tumor specimens less divergent than adjacent non-tumor tissue" remains misleading at first read, since TT k_f ≥ NN k_f in all five cancers (e.g., LUAD 0.282 vs. 0.226) — only the ratio reverses, via the denominator. Consider "less divergent *relative to their elevated baseline*".
4. Severity "P ≈ 0" (SI Note 9) is ambiguous notation for a Jonckheere–Terpstra P-value (near 0 or near 1, and which tail?); state explicitly.
5. LUAD WT ever-smoker rate of 86% is plausible but high; confirm the cBioPortal tobacco_smoking_history coding (categories 1–5 collapse rule) in Methods.
6. The LIHC Edmondson and BRCA PAM50 vignettes are appropriately demoted to "denominator-dominated" status; the cover letter's unqualified reference to "a consistent pan-cancer reversal" should carry the same qualifier.
7. "3,567 samples entering the pair-level analysis" reconciles with the per-cancer counts (569+592+455+832+1,119), but please state the 3,596-sample starting set and account for the 29 exclusions by cancer and sample type.
8. Supplementary Note 8's myeloid-panel addition after initial review ("added in a second revision because the initial three-panel check omitted myeloid markers") is commendable transparency; similar transparency is expected for the CC-sample re-assignment (Major concern 2).

## Questions to authors

1. Provide the per-cancer breakdown of TCGA sample-type codes in the analysis set. Were any non-primary-tumor samples (recurrent, metastatic, cell-line-derived) included as "tumor"? Specifically: what happened to the 32 cancer-cell-line samples re-assigned from LUSC to LIHC in the v49.2 correction — retained or excluded? Show the LIHC NN/TT ratio, LIHC k_n elevation, Edmondson gradient, and the LUSC ratio with these samples removed.
2. Which cellular compartment does the per-pair top-200 panel track in each cancer's TT and NN pairs? Provide compartment enrichment (e.g., epithelial/immune/stromal marker overlap) of the identity panels, or a deconvolution-based localization of the k_n/k_f signals.
3. Why is the per-cancer permutation test (P > 0.9, fixed-panel, anti-conservative) not reported alongside the headline ratios in Results, and what is the justification for kn_floor = 1×10⁻⁴ in the main pipeline but kn_floor = 0 in the severity vignettes?
4. Reconcile the LIHC/KIRC k_n elevations: MS 1.35 and 3.29 (mean ratios) vs. SI Note 8 median ratios 2.18 and 3.70 ("replicates exactly"). Define both statistics and correct the wording.
5. Provide a batch/tissue-source-site sensitivity: restrict NN and TT pairs to within-TSS (or plate-balanced) comparisons and report the k_n elevation and NN/TT ratio.
6. Report the smoking main effect (ever vs. never) on ω, k_f, k_n in LUAD; add stage to the joint model; and extend the smoking analysis to LUSC.
7. For the Edmondson gradient: give per-grade n (G4 = 11?), the exact JT P and tail for the ω trend, and a sensitivity excluding G4.
8. Why was the survival analysis run only in LIHC? Provide the Cox model table (n, events, per-covariate HRs) and, if feasible, the equivalent LUAD/KIRC models.
9. Confirm the survival estimate rounding: MS 1.07 [0.88–1.31] P = 0.48 vs. the pipeline output HR 1.075 [0.882, 1.309] P = 0.475.
10. Supply Supplementary Tables 5–19 within the SI document itself.

---

## Score: 4/10

A candidly written methodology paper with real statistical self-awareness, but the cancer-genomics pillar — the part most likely to attract readership — currently rests on an out-of-scope application of the index to bulk mixtures, an undisclosed sample-provenance correction that plausibly manufactures the LIHC null, composition/batch confounding that the authors' own sensitivity analyses bound but do not exclude, a headline result with no valid design-matched test, and several MS↔SI numeric contradictions.

## Recommendation: **Major Revision**

(If the CC-sample sensitivity shows the LIHC estimates — or the LUSC/KIRC values touched by the regenerated blocks — move materially, the appropriate disposition is rejection and resubmission after the TCGA arm is rebuilt on a clean, documented sample set.)
