# Review Report — Reviewer 1 (Single-Cell Genomics Methodology)

**Manuscript:** "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics" (v49.9)
**Venue:** Nature Communications (Methodology track) — simulated strict review
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Materials reviewed:** Main manuscript (MS.txt), Supplementary Information (SI.txt), Table 1 data (TABLE1.tsv), Cover letter (CL.txt)

---

## Summary

The authors propose CKI, a ratio of two Jensen–Shannon divergences: k_f on per-pair-selected "identity" genes over k_n on housekeeping (HK) genes, heuristic­ally framed by Ka/Ks. The engineering discipline is unusually strong — design-matched block-shuffle nulls, adversarial drift controls, a fixed-panel ablation, and honest disclosure that the brain candidate screen yields no FDR-surviving hits. However, the manuscript's own component decompositions show that in every real-data application ω is dominated by its denominator and adds nothing over its own numerator k_f, which the authors themselves designate "the honest default for ordering claims"; the newly added v49.9 Scope paragraph is asserted without evidence and directly contradicts both the HK definition used throughout and the paper's own cell-type-discrimination validation; and the benchmarking rests on straw-man baselines with the genuine competitors (scDist, Augur) either approximated or statistically inert. Cross-checking revealed a Results-versus-Discussion inconsistency in the authoritative TCGA composition-adjustment caliber. I cannot support publication in anything like the current form.

---

## Major concerns

### 1. ω carries no independent signal in real data; the index reduces to 1/k_n by the authors' own decompositions

This is the central problem for a methodology paper. In every real-data application, the manuscript's own numbers show ω tracks the denominator, not the "functional divergence" it is sold as measuring:

- **Brain:** "class-mean ω tracks k_n (Spearman ρ = −0.73, P = 0.02) but not k_f (ρ = 0.09, P = 0.80) ... the gradient chiefly reflects housekeeping stability across regions, not functional-gene divergence" (MS, Results). The 6.10-fold headline gradient is "a composite signal dominated by the k_n denominator (k_n contributes 3.21-fold versus 2.03-fold from k_f)" (MS, Discussion).
- **TCGA:** pair-level "Spearman ρ(log ω, log k_f) = +0.04, ρ(log ω, log k_n) = −0.82; log-scale variance of k_n accounts for ≈ 72%" (MS, Discussion); the pan-cancer NN/TT reversal is explicitly denominator-driven ("TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 1.3–3.3-fold ... whereas TT k_f was equal to or higher than NN k_f").
- **Cross-organ (Table 1 / Fig. 5):** "per-cell-type mean ω and mean k_f are only weakly concordant (Spearman r = 0.23, ... 95% CI [−0.08, 0.38], P = 0.37)", while SI Note 9 reports ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.31 with "95% CI [−0.61, −0.17], excluding zero — the interval estimates support a denominator-driven ordering". NK cells "rank most divergent under ω, second-most conserved under k_f".
- The Discussion itself concedes: "the honest default for ordering claims is k_f plus a design-matched null, with ω reserved for its specificity-first property—rejecting HK-anchored neutral drift—rather than for ranking" (MS, Discussion).

Yet the title, the Abstract ("quantifying functional divergence"), Table 1, and Figures 5–6 all present ω *orderings* as the biological deliverable. On the only real perturbation with ground truth (Kang IFN-β), ω is strictly **worse** than its own numerator: "rank AUC: ω 0.55–0.92, k_f 0.74–1.00, raw JS 0.79–1.00; in CD14+ monocytes ω AUC fell to 0.55 while k_f retained 0.98" (MS, Results). Nowhere in real data do the authors show a biological conclusion where ω outperforms k_f — only a calibration statistic (T1 misreporting 28.6% vs 37.6% for k_f). If k_f is the honest statistic, the paper should be rewritten around k_f with ω demoted to a drift screen; if the authors disagree, they must exhibit a real-data regime where the ratio adds value. "Specificity-first screen" is a property of the test, not of the index's biological content.

### 2. The v49.9 Scope paragraph is asserted, not demonstrated — and contradicts the manuscript's own evidence

The new paragraph states: "Because the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific—housekeeping gene sets may differ across cell types—the k_n baseline is only directly comparable within the same cell type, so limited cross-type discrimination of ω is expected by design" (MS, Discussion). Three problems:

1. **The premise is unsupported and internally contradicted.** Every reported analysis uses a single global HRT Atlas v1.0 set shared by all pairs: "k_n uses the global HK set (same for all pairs)" (SI, Algorithm 2). The HK definition used throughout is genes that "maintain stable expression **across cell types** and conditions" (SI 1.2). An anchor defined by cross-type stability cannot be invoked as cell-type-specific without evidence, and no per-cell-type HK derivation or citation is provided.
2. **The manuscript's own calibration data show near-identical anchors across cell types:** class-specific split-half baselines span only "7.58–12.52, a 1.65-fold spread", and the two endpoint classes' baselines "nearly coincide, 9.26 vs 9.08" (MS, Results) — against a 6.10-fold ω gradient. The data say the anchor is approximately cell-type-invariant; the Scope paragraph needs the opposite.
3. **The "not to discriminate cell-type identity" framing conflicts with the paper's own validation.** The parameter sweep was optimized and reported on cell-type discrimination: "the identity-only configuration ... achieved the best cell-type discrimination (AUC = 0.786, ... Supplementary Table 1)" (MS, Results); Fig. 2c reports D-category ω = 43.19 vs control 7.70; Tabula Sapiens different-type pairs average ω = 24.87 vs 15.83 for same-type pairs. Cross-type discrimination is demonstrated in the Results, not absent. The Scope paragraph therefore does not "delineate" scope; it retroactively reframes a weakness (cross-type ω orderings are denominator-driven and scheme-specific) as a design principle while leaving the contradictory discrimination-based validation in place. Either provide quantitative evidence for cell-type-specific anchors (derive per-type HK sets and show k_n incomparability across types), or delete the claim and harmonize it with Supplementary Table 1 and Fig. 2c.

### 3. Pseudobulk aggregation order differs across datasets; the calibration-transferability conclusion is confounded

The Methods disclose: "the brain pipeline applies log1p after averaging counts, softmax(log1p(mean counts)) ... whereas the mouse (Tabula Muris pilot) and human (Tabula Sapiens) pipelines average per-cell log1p values and normalize afterwards, softmax(mean(log1p))" (MS, Methods). These are different estimators (mean of logs ≤ log of means, by Jensen), and the brain adds a third variant — "cell-count-weighted means of per-library mean expression vectors ... followed by log1p at the pseudobulk level" (MS, Methods, brain dataset) — while mouse/human use an unweighted per-cell mean that ignores donor/library structure (pseudoreplication). The paper's transferability conclusion — "the mouse factor transfers to Tabula Sapiens but not to the brain atlas" (7.70 vs 7.67 vs 9.73) — is drawn across exactly this pipeline boundary: mouse and human share mean-of-logs, brain does not. The non-transferability may be a pipeline artifact, not a dataset property, and the authors' own sentence ("This difference plausibly contributes ... to the non-transferability of the dataset-specific calibration constants") concedes this without quantifying it. Missing robustness checks: (i) no unified-pipeline recomputation of the three calibration constants; (ii) no sum-of-counts pseudobulk (the field standard for pseudobulk DE), no scran/deconvolution size factors, no downsampling normalization; (iii) cell-count effects are examined only in the brain equal-n control — which erased ~70% of the headline ("the gradient falls from 6.10-fold to 1.74 (95% CI [1.64, 1.84])") — and no equivalent control exists for mouse/human/TCGA. Also, SI 1.6 states "a minimum of 10 cells per group" while MS Methods states "at least 20 cells per entry" — reconcile.

### 4. The +1 pseudo-count dominates the probability mapping and is untested; degenerate-denominator handling is ad hoc

The mapping p_i ∝ (c_i + 1) is applied to CP10k-normalized values where most genes have mean expression ≪ 1; the +1 therefore squashes the majority of genes toward uniformity and compresses dynamic range by orders of magnitude. Crucially, the compression is **differential across the ratio's arms**: HK genes are highly expressed (pseudo-count negligible), whereas per-pair top-200 |Δ| genes include many low/moderate expressers (pseudo-count dominant). The k_f/k_n scale — and hence every ω value in the paper — is set by an untested constant. The only defense offered is: "Key parameters (the +1 normalization pseudo-count, the ε = 1e-9 numerical guard, ...) were chosen based on practical considerations rather than formal optimization; sensitivity analyses showed robustness to parameter variation, but a systematic simultaneous parameter sweep was not performed" (MS, Discussion) — no pseudo-count data are shown anywhere. The Dirichlet dimensionality simulation (Supp. Fig. 13) does not fill this gap; SI Note 15 itself admits it "does not simulate the variance-based gene selection mechanism that generates the ω inflation". On the denominator floor: single-cell analyses run uncapped (kn_floor = 0) with minimum observed brain k_n = 9.2 × 10⁻⁵, and SI Note 4 finds +6.5% ratio bias in the k_n < 10⁻⁴ bin with "skew 2.28, excess kurtosis 9.14" null tails; in TCGA, "aggregate ω saturates at k_f/10⁻⁴ in 3 of 5 cancer types" (MS, Discussion) — i.e., the floor partially *determines* the aggregate analysis. A pseudo-count grid ({0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}) and a principled floor (e.g., data-driven k_n quantile) with full sensitivity reporting are required.

### 5. Housekeeping-gene definition: circular reassurance, an unvalidated shipped auto-detector, and an untested bulk→snRNA-seq transfer

Three issues. (i) The claimed robustness check — "the lowest 10% variable genes as a constrained set yielded ω correlations r > 0.95" (MS, Discussion) — replaces one expression-stability set with another derived from the same data by the same criterion class; this is near-circular and cannot validate the anchor. The standard external lists (e.g., Eisenberg & Levanon, cited as ref. 30) are never used: report gene-set overlap and ω-rank robustness for HRT vs Eisenberg vs the auto-detected set. (ii) The package ships detect_housekeeping_genes() (detection rate > 0.9 + CV < 30th percentile, "combined" mode), which is dataset-dependent by construction and circular if applied to the compared populations; it is "not used here" (MS, Discussion) and validated nowhere. Detection-rate and CV thresholds on log1p data are depth-, cell-count-, and mean-expression-dependent; if shipped, it must be validated or removed. (iii) The HRT Atlas is built from bulk RNA-seq, yet it is applied to single-nucleus data (brain; 1,115 of 1,130 genes matched; 1,099 in Kang; 1,064 in simulation) without any transfer check — nuclear-vs-bulk composition biases (the manuscript itself raises mitochondrial/nuclear degradation in the PMI caveat) can masquerade as structure in k_n.

### 6. Benchmarking is against straw men; simulation specificity is partly definitional; power collapse threatens the brain inference

- The headline "false-positive rate 0.00 versus 0.55–0.58 for JS and cosine" compares ω against all-gene raw JS/cosine under a null **defined on CKI's own anchoring assumption** (drift placed on HK genes). SI Note 1 concedes "the type-I and AUC advantages above are therefore conditional on that assumption rather than evidence for it". Raw JS and cosine make no HK/functional distinction — detecting HK drift is not a "false positive" for them. The fair baselines (JS restricted to non-HK genes; a matched standardized score) are absent. The N1 adversarial control is welcome but the Abstract and cover letter still lead with the rigged comparison, uncaveated.
- The real competitors are crippled: "the scDist results reported in this work were obtained with a Python approximation ... the original is an R package and was not run here" (MS, Discussion); MELD saturated ("AUC 0.997–0.9998, leaving no gradient to compare"); Augur is a null result presented as complementarity ("ρ = 0.442 (P = 0.200) ... With n = 10 classes these correlations are descriptive only"). No comparison to the actual standard for "comparing two populations" — pseudobulk DE (edgeR/DESeq2/muscat) — is provided.
- Simulation detection thresholds are set at each metric's own q95 under pure resampling, then "FPR" is compared under HK-drift perturbations that are not a null for the comparators; and the claimed specificity is dataset- and scale-dependent in real data (Kang 0/30, Wilson CI up to 11.4%; brain T1 28.6%, rising "from 14.1% ... to 48.0%" with group size) — versus FPR 0.00 in simulation. "ω raised no false reports" (Abstract/CL) oversells precision.
- Power: "donor-paired power falls from 0.67–0.93 at 50 cells per donor per condition to ≈ 0 at 500 and above (operating window ~50–200 cells)" (MS, Results), mechanistically because "the permutation null of ω explodes with pseudobulk size (k_n → 0 in the null while top-200 selection keeps k_f elevated)" (SI 3.11). That mechanism is not specific to the donor-paired design — brain region pseudobulks vastly exceed 500 nuclei — yet the power analysis is confined to Kang. Demonstrate that the brain block-shuffle per-pair tests retain power at atlas-relevant pseudobulk sizes, or the brain inference rests on an untested regime of the method's own operating window.

### 7. Internal inconsistencies across MS Results, MS Discussion, SI, and Table 1

- **Composition-adjustment caliber mismatch (Results vs Discussion).** Results: "the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged after composition adjustment (**−1.2%, bootstrap 95% CI −4.1% to +2.6%; per cancer type −16% to +37%**)" — matching the authoritative linear-normalization SI update (LIHC +37.1%, KIRC +18.3%, BRCA −16.2%, LUAD −1.6%, LUSC −11.1%). Discussion: "the tumor-pair coefficient attenuates by **−0.5% pooled (95% CI [−3.2%, +2.6%])** ... (**BRCA −14.0%, LUSC −9.7%, LUAD −2.3%, KIRC +19.6%, LIHC +33.5%**)". The manuscript declares the linear recomputation "the authoritative caliber throughout" (MS, Results), yet the Discussion quotes the legacy softmax-caliber numbers, unlabeled. Fix the Discussion to the authoritative values.
- **Wrong SI cross-references.** SI 3.12: "the replication is shown in main-text **Fig. 4d**" and "main-text **Fig. 4b,c**" — those are TCGA panels; the correct references are Fig. 3d and Fig. 3b,c.
- **Wrong table number.** SI Note 9 refers to the cross-organ ranking as "**Table 2** / Fig. 5"; the manuscript's cross-organ table is Table 1.
- Verified consistent (no action): Table 1 values reproduce MS exactly (CD8+ T 9.93 ± 4.62, n = 6; Plasma 10.20 ± 2.03; Macrophage 15.49 ± 8.46, n = 15; Endothelial 26.25, Erythrocyte 29.36, n = 3; pair counts sum to 59; weighted mean over Table 1 = 15.83, matching "mean ω = 15.83, n = 59 pairs"); pair partition 1,038 + 3,754 + 59 = 4,851 = C(99,2); TCGA 35,306 pairs decompose exactly (2,000 TT + 2,000 TN + 15,306 complete NN); brain class nuclei sum to 886,808; 82.75/13.56 = 6.10; 148.3/39 = 3.8; BH arithmetic q = 0.010/0.017/0.075 checks out; MS/SI simulation and drift-ladder numbers (0.553/0.580; 28.6/45.2/44.1/40.6/37.6/35.7/19.9; 1.04→1.76→1.80 vs 1.07→3.32→2.98) agree verbatim.

## Minor concerns

1. SI 3.2 still reports the Bergmann-glia pair-level bootstrap CI "[11.31, 16.14], mean 13.56" without flagging that Supplementary Note 2 supersedes it with the studentized cluster interval [5.76, 28.59]; the stale interval excludes the class baseline (9.08) that the corrected one does not.
2. MS Results threshold sweep: "thresholds of 10, 20 (reported), 50, and 100 nuclei retain 10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12" — three gradient values for four thresholds; the min-50 gradient is missing.
3. Precision policy is inconsistent: ω_cal is "reported to at most two significant figures as order-of-magnitude estimates", yet raw ω means are quoted to four significant figures throughout (82.75, 13.56, 37.76).
4. Cover letter/Abstract "ground-truth simulation (1,750 replicates)" vs Methods/SI "1,750 replicates **per background**" (two backgrounds) — ambiguous.
5. Fig. 4 caption "adjusted P = 0.009 and 0.029" vs text "Δk_f +0.012, P = 0.009 ... smoking-plus-admixture adjustment (omega +13.6, P = 3.3 × 10⁻⁴)" — which P belongs to which adjustment is not recoverable from the caption.
6. MS reports TCGA k_n elevation as "1.3–3.3-fold (mean ratios)" while SI Note 8 reports "median TT/NN ratio 2.2–3.7x" (e.g., KIRC mean 3.29 vs median 3.70; LIHC mean 1.35 vs median 2.18) — both labeled, but the casual reader will conflate them; state both in one place.
7. The Tabula Sapiens method comparison uses "one pseudobulk ... from its largest donor", discarding inter-donor variability; the S-vs-D hierarchy (15.83 vs 24.87) and the organ-reversal claim inherit this single-donor restriction without a sensitivity analysis.
8. The "ω negatively correlated with all four standard metrics" Results section is retained as a centerpiece although the paper itself shows it is "partly a ratio artifact of the k_n denominator"; consider demoting it to the SI.
9. Per-pair k_n estimator comparison is quoted with two different statistics in two places (class-level ρ = 0.09 in MS Results vs pair-level ρ = 0.142 in SI 3.5/Supp. Fig. 7) without cross-labeling.
10. SI 4.5 describes "the droplet arm ... two 10x Genomics lanes" while MS Results describe batch 1 as "three 10x lanes"; the batch structure (calibration vs stimulation subsets) should be disambiguated in one sentence.
11. The GitHub repository is named "CKI-cell-type-identification" — at odds with the v49.9 claim that CKI is "not to discriminate cell-type identity".
12. The equal-n control reduces every class to 4,118 nuclei — the committed-OPC size — meaning the "size-balanced" gradient estimate of 1.74 is estimated at the noisiest class's scale; report an intermediate-n variant.

## Questions to authors

1. Recompute all three calibration constants (mouse 7.70, human 7.67, brain 9.73) under a single unified pseudobulk pipeline (sum-of-counts + one normalization). Does the "transfers to human but not brain" conclusion survive, or is it an aggregation-order artifact?
2. Provide k_n, k_f, and ω sensitivity to the pseudo-count over {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} on each dataset, including the split-half baseline and the brain gradient. If ω's scale moves materially, how should users choose this parameter?
3. Provide one real-data example in which ω supports a biological conclusion that k_f alone (with a design-matched null) does not. If none exists, why is ω rather than k_f the manuscript's index?
4. Substantiate or retract "the housekeeping anchor is cell-type-specific": derive per-cell-type HK sets from your data, quantify their overlap, and show how cross-type k_n comparison fails. Reconcile with Supplementary Table 1 (AUC = 0.786 cell-type discrimination).
5. Why does the Discussion quote legacy softmax-caliber composition numbers (−0.5% [−3.2%, +2.6%]; per-cancer +33.5/+19.6/−14.0/−9.7/−2.3) after the Results declare the linear pipeline authoritative (−1.2% [−4.1%, +2.6%]; +37.1/+18.3/−16.2/−11.1/−1.6)?
6. Does the ω permutation-test power collapse at pseudobulk n ≳ 500 affect the brain block-shuffle per-pair tests? Provide a power analysis at brain-relevant group sizes under the block-shuffle null.
7. For TCGA, aggregate ω "saturates at k_f/10⁻⁴ in 3 of 5 cancer types": which reported TCGA quantities depend on the aggregate (floor-bound) analysis versus the pair-level table (floor hit once in 35,306 pairs)?
8. Benchmark against a fair minimal baseline — JS divergence restricted to non-HK genes, and one pseudobulk DE framework (e.g., edgeRQLF or muscat) on the Kang design — and report where ω stands.
9. Justify applying a bulk-RNA-seq-derived HK reference (HRT Atlas) to single-nucleus data, and report ω-rank robustness under HRT vs Eisenberg & Levanon vs auto-detected HK sets.
10. Clarify the min-cells rule (MS: ≥ 20 cells per entry; SI 1.6: "minimum of 10 cells per group") and state the gradient value at the min-50 threshold in the threshold sweep.

## Score: 3/10

## Recommendation: Reject

The audit trail, null engineering, and self-criticism are well above the field's norm, and a k_f-centered rewrite with a unified pipeline and fair baselines could become a solid methods paper. But as submitted, the index is denominator-dominated in every real dataset, the v49.9 scope claim is contradicted by the manuscript's own definitions, validation, and calibration data, the benchmarking is rigged toward straw-man comparators, and the Results and Discussion disagree on the authoritative TCGA numbers. These are flaws of substance, not presentation, and they exceed what revision can credibly fix within one cycle at this venue.
