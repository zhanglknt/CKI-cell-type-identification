## Summary

The manuscript introduces CKI, a Ka/Ks-inspired ratio ω = k_f/k_n that normalizes functional-gene divergence (per-pair top-200 |Δ| identity genes) against a housekeeping-gene baseline (HRT Atlas v1.0), positioning itself as a specificity-first divergence index rather than a cell-type discriminator. The evidence chain — split-half calibration (ω = 7.70), ground-truth simulation (AUC = 0.80), real technical-replicate drift ladders (Kang 30 pairs; brain T1 2,161 pairs), TCGA pan-cancer reversal, and a brain regional gradient with a hypothesis-generating candidate screen — is unusually thoroughly stress-tested, with adversarial circularity scenarios (functional-on-HK, neutral-on-nonHK), fixed-panel/leave-pair-out ablations, donor-stratified nulls, and small-cluster bootstrap corrections all reported against the authors' own claims.

My verification found every spot-checked number to reproduce, including independent recomputation of: simulation AUC at δ = 1 (0.849 vs. claimed 0.85), overall AUC (0.804 vs. 0.80), brain class-level ρ(ω, k_n) = −0.733 and ρ(ω, k_f) = 0.091 (claimed −0.73/0.09), the 6.10-fold gradient with k_f 2.03× / k_n 3.21× decomposition, the "3 of 39" global-k_n survivor count (exactly 3 recomputed), the Bergmann-glia stratified-family min q = 0.042, drift-ladder FPRs (28.6/35.7–45.2%), and the cross-organ CD8+/Plasma means. The scientific positioning (specificity-first, bounded scope, complementarity with Augur) is stated with rare candor.

## Strengths

1. **Benchmark internal fairness is good.** All six metrics in the ground-truth simulation share the identical code path, gene set, and per-metric null-95 thresholds calibrated on the same 200 baseline replicates (Supplementary Note 1, Methods 5.8); the drift ladder evaluates all seven metrics on identical pairs against identical per-pair n-matched nulls (Table 10 caption explicitly notes the paired design). This is methodologically clean.
2. **Honest adversarial self-testing.** The functional-on-HK blindness (S1), neutral-on-nonHK controls (N1/N2, Note 6), MELD/scDist comparison where CKI loses (AUC 0.05–0.13 at G = 500, Table 8), and the k_n-permutation mathematical floor (0.524 vs. observed 0.089, Note 5) go well beyond typical benchmark practice.
3. **The bounded-scope/HK-anchor declaration is prominent and repeated** (Introduction para. 4, Results "Perturbation-response boundaries", Discussion, Limitations iv) — exactly where a reviewer would demand it.
4. **Circularity of `pairwise_absdiff` top-200 is quantified, not hidden**: median 1.61-fold k_f inflation vs. leave-pair-out, rank ρ = 0.937, absolute ω flagged as upper-bound scheme-specific estimates (Note 9).
5. **Statistical hygiene**: cluster-aware bootstraps replacing independence-assuming intervals, studentized bootstrap-t for ≤ 7 clusters, donor-stratified nulls, permutation-resolution limits of the pair-level FDR explicitly framed.

## Major comments

**M1. The headline discrimination claim (ω AUC 0.80 vs. k_f 0.72) lacks a paired test of the AUC difference.**
Location: Results "Ground-truth simulation" (AUC = 0.80 versus 0.72 k_f; DeLong 95% CI [0.770, 0.838] reported for ω only); Supplementary Note 1 (AUC interval methods).
Rationale: The paper's central specificity-first argument reduces to "ω > k_f in functional-vs-neutral discrimination while k_f alone suffices for ordering claims elsewhere." A point estimate gap of 0.088 on the same 850 replicates (600 signal / 250 neutral, 3 module seeds — I recomputed 0.804 vs. 0.716) needs a paired DeLong test or, better, the already-implemented module-seed cluster bootstrap applied to the *difference*. Without it, "best discrimination among six metrics" is a point-estimate statement. Severity: **Major (easily addressable — the machinery exists in `scripts/nc52_stats_resampling.py`).**

**M2. The drift-ladder FPR advantage is reported only descriptively; a paired discordance test is needed for the headline "lowest misreporting" claim.**
Location: Results "Real-data neutral-drift calibration" (T1: ω 28.6% vs. 35.7–45.2%); Supplementary Table 10 caption (which itself notes "a McNemar-style paired framing applies to any cross-metric test" — but no such test is reported).
Rationale: On T1 (2,161 pairs) every metric is scored on identical pairs against identical per-pair nulls, so the FPR differences are within-pair contrasts; a McNemar (or donor-clustered logistic) test on discordant pairs would establish whether the ω vs. raw-JS gap (28.6% vs. 45.2%) and the ω vs. k_n gap (28.6% vs. 35.7%) exceed chance, and a donor-level cluster (only 4 donors) should be modeled, since the same 4 donors drive all pairs. Relatedly, the Kang arm (0/30) has a Wilson upper bound of 11.4% — disclosed, but the abstract's "lowest false-report rate" phrasing would benefit from the paired-test qualification. Severity: **Major.**

**M3. Benchmark breadth on the *neutral-drift* task is limited to classical metrics; no modern baseline is evaluated where the paper's headline advantage lives.**
Location: Abstract; Results Fig. 3; Supplementary Note 3.12.
Rationale: The five comparison metrics on the drift task are raw JS, cosine, Spearman, marker Jaccard, and CKI's own components. MELD and the scDist approximation are benchmarked only on the mean-shift detection task, where CKI loses (Table 8 — reported honestly). Evaluating at least one embedding/perturbation-response baseline (e.g., the same scDist approximation, which is already implemented) on the T1 drift-ladder FPR task would show whether the specificity advantage is specific to classical distance metrics or general. Also note marker Jaccard *beats* ω on the T1 FPR statistic (19.9% vs. 28.6%, Table 11); the text handles this fairly ("misreporting claim restricted to the continuous divergence metrics"; Jaccard's weaker T3 sensitivity 1.41 vs. 1.80), and the abstract's "among continuous divergence metrics" is technically accurate — but the qualifier should survive into the Discussion's practical-rules paragraph, where it is currently implicit. Severity: **Major (scope/positioning; experiments optional but the framing adjustment is required).**

**M4. The Augur complementarity argument is underpowered and rests on a self-validated port.**
Location: Discussion ("rankings are moderately concordant (ρ = 0.442 versus class-mean ω; 0.564 versus k_f)"); Supplementary Note 15.
Rationale: n = 10 classes, P = 0.200/0.090 — both correlations are statistically indistinguishable from zero, and pyaugur 0.1.0 is validated only by the port's self-reported benchmark (disclosed). The "complementary rather than redundant" conclusion is reasonable and the text says "descriptive only", but "moderately concordant" overstates a null result; at minimum state explicitly that the concordance is not statistically established, or enlarge the class set (e.g., Tabula Sapiens cross-organ condition labels, where n = 17+ cell types are available) to make the test informative. Severity: **Moderate–Major (framing; one sentence of softening or one added analysis).**
