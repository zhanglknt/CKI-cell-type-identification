Results
Decomposing transcriptomic variation
CKI takes two cell populations, each a pseudobulk expression vector (mean expression across cells), and applies the same metric (Jensen–Shannon divergence) in three steps on the same matrix, so the ratio is internally calibrated (Fig. 1).
Step 1: baseline divergence rate k_n: pseudobulk vectors restricted to housekeeping (HK) gene indices, +1 pseudo-count, L1 normalization; k_n is the JS divergence between the two HK-gene probability distributions. Because HK genes should not differ systematically between biologically equivalent populations 13, k_n captures baseline technical and physiological noise.
Step 2: functional divergence rate k_f on cell-type identity genes: top-2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs; Seurat, HK excluded) for the Tabula Muris full pairwise matrix (Supplementary Fig. 1), and the pair’s top-200 differentially expressed genes (absolute mean difference, HK excluded) for all other analyses, with k_n computed per pair on the shared HK set (fixed-panel ablation, Results).
Step 3: ω = k_f/k_n. Statistical inference uses permutation testing (B = 1,000 for all datasets): group labels are permuted (cell labels for mouse and human, sample labels for TCGA) or, for the brain, library-to-region assignments block-shuffled; empirical P-values, standardized effect sizes, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR are computed within each dataset (Methods).
A parameter sweep on Tabula Muris (703 pairs, 6 organs) showed that adding pathway enrichment scores to k_f does not improve discrimination (identity-only AUC = 0.786; Supplementary Fig. 1, Supplementary Table 1).
Calibration confirms baseline behavior
We calibrated CKI on the Tabula Muris FACS dataset 8 (SmartSeq2, 15,057 cells, 22,308 genes, 6 organs), with housekeeping genes from HRT Atlas v1.0 13 (mouse column): top-2,000 HVGs for the full pairwise matrix (703 pairs, Supplementary Fig. 1), and the hybrid per-pair scheme for the pilot (Fig. 2).
Control comparisons randomly split the same population into halves across six FACS control populations (Fig. 2a): 50 split-half replicates per population (300 ω values) stabilized the baseline at mean ω = 7.70 (two-stage bootstrap over the six populations, 95% CI [6.38, 9.82]; the 300-replicate interval [7.37, 8.02] is pseudo-replicated and superseded; leave-one-population-out range 6.75–8.08; Section 3.10 of the Supplementary Information). No control comparison reached significance (all P > 0.05): biologically equivalent populations are recognized as undiverged.
Beyond controls, ω increased monotonically with biological distance (Fig. 2b, c): same cell type across organs (mean ω = 21.31, n = 4) sat below different cell types within an organ (mean ω = 43.19, n = 3), with cross-organ cross-type comparisons highest (2–4 pairs per category). k_f drives this: from controls to inter-cell-type comparisons, k_f rose ~400-fold (0.0015 → 0.63–0.68) while k_n rose only 72–118-fold—CKI measures functional divergence, not total difference.
Because the empirical baseline deviates from the theoretical ideal (ω = 1), we introduce ω_cal = ω_obs / 7.70. Split-half calibration inside each dataset shows the factor to be dataset-relative (brain atlas 9.73; Tabula Sapiens 7.67, indicative only across pipelines); class-specific baselines leave the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient essentially unchanged, and a ratio-estimator audit confirms the calibration absorbs ratio bias (Supplementary Notes 2–4). The widened baseline interval limits ω_cal to roughly one decimal of resolution (Supplementary Note 3).
Correlation structure between CKI and standard metrics
We extended CKI to the Tabula Sapiens human atlas 9 (108,136 cells; 99 filtered cell-type entries, 4,851 pairs across six organs), using the same hybrid scheme with HRT Atlas v1.0 HK genes 13 (human column) (Fig. 2d). Human ω ranged from 1.35 to 87.69 (mean 21.61, median 19.65); cross-dataset ω comparisons are rank-based only (Discussion). The biological hierarchy was preserved: same cell type across organs (mean ω = 15.83, n = 59) sat below different cell types within an organ (mean ω = 24.87, n = 1,038).
On all 4,851 human pairs, ω correlated negatively with all four standard metrics (raw JS, Spearman, cosine, marker Jaccard; Spearman r = −0.36 to −0.46, all P < 10⁻¹⁴⁵), the four forming a tight positive cluster (pairwise r = 0.57–0.93). The negative sign is partly a ratio artifact: k_f correlated positively with the standard metrics (r = +0.43 to +0.72) and k_n more strongly (r = +0.69 to +0.81), and conditional on k_n all four turned positive (partial r = +0.11 to +0.54, all P < 1 × 10⁻¹⁴).
CKI was the only metric scoring same-organ different-cell-type pairs above different-organ different-cell-type pairs (Mann-Whitney P = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁸); all four standard metrics showed the opposite pattern. Decomposition tempers the functional reading: same-organ pairs have indistinguishable k_f (P = 0.60) but lower k_n (P = 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁶)—a more stable within-organ housekeeping baseline, not greater functional specialization (Supplementary Note 9).
Ground-truth simulation: specificity versus sensitivity
No real dataset provides pairs with known functional divergence, so we injected perturbations into a real background (Tabula Muris FACS marrow B cells; pre-injection divergence zero; Methods). Under pure neutral housekeeping drift, raw JS and cosine exceeded their null thresholds in 55% and 58% of replicates, ω in none; an injected functional module was detected only when strong (0 of 150 replicates at δ = 1, 13% at δ = 2). ω is structurally blind to modules placed on HK genes (detection 0.000 at every δ; k_n fired at 0.61–1.00), yet under expression-matched low-variance non-HK drift ω stayed at its calibrated false-positive rate (0.000–0.067) while raw JS and cosine inflated to 0.81–1.00 (Supplementary Notes 1, 5).
ω best discriminated functional (δ ≥ 0.25) from neutral perturbations (Fig. 2e; AUC = 0.80 versus 0.72 k_f, 0.64 raw JS, 0.58 cosine, 0.21 k_n; DeLong 95% CI [0.770, 0.838], module-seed cluster bootstrap [0.771, 0.836]): the standard metrics’ apparent power is purchased with false positives on neutral drift. Rank discrimination and thresholded power diverge (marrow: AUC 0.85 at δ = 1 despite 0/150 detections at the null-95 threshold; Supplementary Note 1). The ratio also conferred robustness to technical asymmetry (fourfold cell-count imbalance: ω −32%, k_f +67%, cosine +108%)—a specificity-first screen rejecting neutral drift at the cost of bounded power for weak-to-moderate signals (Supplementary Note 1).
The design fully reproduced in a second background (skin keratinocyte stem cells; AUC(ω) = 0.908 versus AUC(k_f) = 0.859; Fig. 2e), with background-dependent power bounds and higher k_f power under fourfold imbalance (Supplementary Note 1).
Real-data neutral-drift calibration on technical replicates
We tested the same specificity on real technical replicates (per-pair size-matched cell-shuffle nulls, B = 200; Methods). In the first batch of the IFN-β PBMC dataset 14, the 30 same-donor, same-condition cross-lane pairs are clean technical replicates: ω was fully calibrated (0 of 30 above its null 95th percentile; k_f likewise 0 of 30), whereas raw JS misreported 36.7% and cosine 23.3% (Fig. 3d).
The design scales to the brain atlas (606 10x libraries) as a three-tier drift ladder of library-level pairs 12: T1, same donor, region, and cell type (2,161 pairs); T2, different donors within a region (1,089); T3, different regions within a donor (1,656). On T1, ω misreported least among the continuous divergence metrics (FPR 28.6% versus 37.6–45.2% for the others; Fig. 3b, c), and its calibration gradient is the shallowest across the ladder (T3 ratio 1.80 versus 2.98 raw JS; Section 3.12).
Two qualifications temper the brain result. The absolute ω FPR is not zero and grows with group size (14.1% below 30 nuclei per library to 48.0% above 500; raw JS 28.6% to 72.5%)—a library-level technical component the anchor absorbs only partially. A minority of classes show gene-specific library effects the anchor cannot absorb (Section 3.12). Neutral-drift immunity thus transfers to real data only as a relative-calibration advantage.
A sanity check on unused data is consistent with this: on the CELLxGENE Microglia supercluster 12 (91,838 nuclei), sample-matched microglia-versus-CNS-macrophage pairs (n = 35) separate cleanly from neutral half-splits (n = 40; ω 21.83 ± 7.20 versus 1.30 ± 0.36; Mann-Whitney P = 5.5 × 10⁻¹⁴, descriptive—pairs overlap across four donors; AUC = 1.00), the margin carried by k_f (Supplementary Note 16; Supplementary Fig. 14).
Real perturbation demonstration: IFN-β-stimulated PBMCs
On a real perturbation with unknown effect on the housekeeping anchor—PBMCs from eight donors, control versus 6-hour IFN-β stimulation 14 (24,413 cells, six cell types; Methods), condition fully confounded with lane—we read this as a relative architecture demonstration. The perturbation was visible at the ω level in all six cell types, but k_f alone separated perturbation from donor drift as well or better (rank AUC: ω 0.55–0.92, k_f 0.74–1.00); in CD14+ monocytes ω fell to 0.55 while k_f retained 0.98, because stimulation raises housekeeping expression itself (median k_n 1.2–5.7-fold) and the inflating denominator partially cancels the functional signal (Supplementary Fig. 3; Supplementary Note 6).
Benchmarking against perturbation-response metrics
We benchmarked CKI against MELD (v1.0.2) and scDist (Python approximation) on the Kang IFN-β dataset 14. CKI and MELD agreed on effect direction in 6 of 6 cell types, but MELD’s within-type separation was near-saturated (AUC 0.997–0.9998). In an additive mean-shift simulation, MELD and the scDist approximation detected every configuration (sensitivity 1.00), whereas CKI ω rose from AUC 0.52 to 0.79 as the shift grew on 100 genes but collapsed to 0.05–0.13 on 500 genes, where the anchor responds (k_n AUC = 1.000) and annihilates the ratio; users seeking maximal detection power for broad perturbations should use MELD, scDist, or CKI’s own k_f component.
Fixed-panel ablation: robust rankings, scheme-specific ω
Non-circular panels left rankings essentially untouched (pair-level ρ = 0.937 leave-pair-out, 0.918–0.931 other panels; class means ρ = 0.90–0.99), and the astrocyte-to-Bergmann-glia gradient was preserved and amplified (6.10-fold reported; 6.53-fold leave-pair-out), with class-level significance largely robust under scheme-matched block-shuffle nulls (Supplementary Note 7). Circular selection inflated k_f by a median 1.61-fold versus leave-pair-out (grand mean ω 38.55 reported; 26.5 leave-pair-out; 6.5 fixed), so absolute ω values are upper-bound, scheme-specific estimates: rank-based conclusions are robust, but the tier cutoffs (ω < 15/25/35) do not transfer (Supplementary Note 7).
A pan-cancer map of tissue-level divergence in tumors
We applied CKI to TCGA bulk RNA-seq across five cancer types (LUAD, LUSC, LIHC, KIRC, BRCA) 10,11, totalling 3,535 samples. At bulk resolution, ω quantifies divergence between tissue states; we compare tumor–tumor (TT), normal–normal (NN), and tumor–normal (TN) pairs within each cancer type (Fig. 4; Supplementary Fig. 4). A pan-cancer reversal emerged: NN pairs were more divergent than TT pairs in all five (mean NN/TT ω: LUAD 2.46, KIRC 1.88, LUSC 1.71, BRCA 1.57, LIHC 1.11; cluster-bootstrap CI excluding 1 in four of five, LIHC excepted). All TCGA results exclude 32 cell-line-derived aliquots found by barcode audit (Section 1.7 of the Supplementary Information). Decomposition identified the mechanism: TT k_n exceeded NN k_n by 1.3–3.3-fold in every cancer type while TT k_f was equal to or higher—the reversal reflects a more stable housekeeping baseline among adjacent non-tumor specimens, not smaller functional-gene divergence of tumor cells (Fig. 4a).
Stratifying LUAD tumors by driver mutation (61 EGFR-mutant, 120 KRAS-mutant, 311 wild-type; Fig. 4b), KRAS-mutant tumors showed the highest tissue-level divergence (mean ω = 136.9 versus 115.4 wild-type and 122.2 EGFR-mutant; Kruskal-Wallis P = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷; Dunn–Holm P ≤ 0.008 for both KRAS contrasts, P = 0.39 for EGFR–wild-type; label-permutation confirmed; Section 3.13). The KRAS elevation was carried predominantly by a lower housekeeping baseline (~84% of the log-ω gap) with an accompanying functional component (KRAS versus EGFR under k_f alone, P = 0.015). Adjusting for ESTIMATE stromal/immune admixture left the KRAS–wild-type difference significant (adjusted log-ω ratio 1.19, 95% CI [1.12, 1.26]) but abolished the apparent EGFR elevation (all adjusted P > 0.4); adjusting for smoking, alone or jointly with admixture, or with age and sex, left the contrast essentially unchanged (whole-tumor permutation of the adjusted models, B = 10,000: KRAS P ≤ 0.001, all EGFR contrasts P ≥ 0.28; Section 3.13 of the Supplementary Information). TP53 co-mutation and histological subtype were not adjusted for, so residual confounding remains; stratifications beyond LUAD are denominator-dominated vignettes (Supplementary Note 9).
Four controls bound the interpretation (Supplementary Note 8). Composition adjustment left the pooled tumor-pair k_n coefficient essentially unchanged (−1.3% pooled, 95% CI −4.8% to +2.0%), and high-purity-half comparisons increased the TT k_n elevation in all five cancer types, so admixture can only weaken, not create, it. All TCGA statistics were recomputed under the authoritative linear probability mapping; only LIHC is mapping-sensitive (linear 1.11 [0.94, 1.30] versus softmax 1.29 [1.09, 1.53], the latter excluding 1 in all five; Supplementary Table 5), and the housekeeping floor (k_n ≥ 10⁻⁴) is never reached, leaving every ratio identical across floors 0–10⁻⁴ (Supplementary Note 8). LUAD KRAS identity panels showed no MSigDB Hallmark enrichment 15 after correction (all q ≥ 0.24), and composition-adjusted regressions retained the TT ≥ NN k_f ordering in all five. TCGA “normal” samples are adjacent non-tumor, and per-tumor ω was not associated with survival (ex-CC LIHC Cox, stage categorical: ω HR per SD 1.08, 95% CI 0.88–1.34, P = 0.467, n = 272 with 79 events; k_f model likewise null, HR 1.07, P = 0.560): we report all TCGA findings as tissue-level divergence at bulk resolution, pending single-cell or deconvolution-based validation.
A GTEx healthy reference supplies the missing external comparison (Supplementary Note 8): in lung, liver, and breast, healthy k_n sits below adjacent-normal k_n, which sits below tumor k_n (pooled healthy/adjacent 0.63 [0.60, 0.67]; tumor/adjacent 1.49 [1.43, 1.55])—adjacent non-tumor is intermediate, not a field-effect plateau. Kidney is the exception (GTEx cortex k_n ≈ tumor; n = 28, high variance).
CKI ranks cell types by cross-organ conservation
Of the 4,851 Tabula Sapiens pairs, 59 are same-cell-type cross-organ comparisons (Fig. 5; Table 1; Supplementary Fig. 5). Among well-sampled types (n ≥ 5 pairs; Table 1), CD8+ T cells (mean ω = 9.93 ± 4.62, n = 6) and Plasma cells (mean ω = 10.20 ± 2.03, n = 6) were the most conserved, followed by Neutrophils and NK cells, with Macrophages (n = 15) intermediate; Endothelial cells and Erythrocytes were the most divergent, consistent with organ-specific endothelial programs 16, though both rest on n = 3 pairs; sparsely sampled types carry no reliable signal.
The cross-organ ranking agreed little with standard-metric rankings (Spearman r = −0.40 to 0.02, n = 59 pairs; Supplementary Table 2), because CKI explicitly normalizes. A k_f-only control qualifies this (Supplementary Note 9): per-cell-type mean ω and mean k_f are only weakly concordant (r = 0.23, 95% CI [−0.08, 0.38], n = 17), the extremes reproduce but the middle does not. The ranking is thus a composite of functional divergence and baseline differences, and because the k_n anchor is not calibrated across cell types, cross-type gaps should be read descriptively (Discussion).
Brain regional analysis reveals divergence gradients
We applied CKI to the Siletti et al. human brain atlas 12 (~3.3 million nuclei, 108 regions), focusing on 888,263 non-neuronal nuclei in 10 major classes (supercluster_term does not resolve neuronal subtypes). ω was computed for all same-cell-type cross-region pairs (31,764; Fig. 6; Supplementary Fig. 6; Supplementary Table 3); vascular cells and fibroblasts are heterogeneous superclusters, so their class-level ω values are supercluster averages.
Class-mean ω spanned a regional gradient from Bergmann glia and vascular cells (both 13.56) to astrocytes (82.75 ± 44.98, n = 5,778 pairs)—a full-data endpoint gradient of 6.10-fold, an uncorrected upper bound. Two controls converge on a robust intermediate value: a span-matched control (21 intra-cerebellar pairs) yields 3.68 (donor-level bootstrap [1.67, 3.97]) and a combined span- and size-matched control yields 3.66 (donor-level bootstrap [1.92, 3.78]; leave-one-donor-out always > 1). The size-only equal-n control (1.74) is not donor-robust (95% CI [0.80, 2.34]; four donors) and is relegated to a sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Note 10). RNA-quality proxies do not drive the gradient: (class, library)- and (class, region)-level adjustment for detection depth, UMI counts, and mitochondrial fraction leaves it at 6.33–6.45 (Supplementary Note 10).
A cell-type-level block-shuffle null permuting library-to-region assignments (B = 1,000; Methods) tested whether the gradient exceeds chance: regional structure significantly raised mean ω for 4 of 10 classes (astrocytes at the permutation floor; OPCs, committed OPCs, fibroblasts P ≤ 0.030), 3 of 10 surviving Benjamini-Hochberg correction; a donor-stratified null leaves the same 3 of 10, and ependymal cells—the one class moving against the conservative direction—still do not survive (stratified q = 0.052). Microglia, oligodendrocytes, and Bergmann glia sit at or below the null expectation.
Three analyses probe the gradient’s robustness. First, restricting to same-donor region pairs preserved the extremes and a 4.50-fold gradient (Supplementary Note 12). Second, the astrocyte-versus-Bergmann-glia contrast is predominantly a k_n effect (k_f differs 2.0-fold; mean k_n 3.2-fold): class-mean ω tracks k_n (Spearman ρ = −0.73) but not k_f (ρ = 0.09), and under a k_f-only ordering astrocytes rank third of ten.
Third, class-mean k_n correlates negatively with class size (Spearman ρ = −0.648, P = 0.043), while class-mean ω is uncorrelated with all tested confounders (P ≥ 0.091; Supplementary Note 10). The equal-n residual is carried predominantly by the functional term (astrocyte/Bergmann-glia k_f ratio 2.09 equal-n, 2.03 full-data), whereas the k_n ratio reverses (equal-n 1.29, astrocyte higher; full-data 0.31)—the full-data Bergmann-glia k_n elevation is largely a class-size artefact.
The ordering is compatible with known cell biology—tissue-resident vascular cells and fibroblasts lowest 17,18, regionally specialized astrocytes highest 19—but it is k_n-dominated and does not by itself establish functional specialization.
A residence/migration framework finds no support: OPCs—the most actively migrating non-neuronal population—show the second-highest mean ω (40.62), and the screen shows no oligodendrocyte-lineage concentration (fold 0.77, P = 0.92).
Anomalously similar pairs: a hypothesis-generating screen
Low ω for a cell type across two regions indicates similarity beyond baseline expectation, with non-exclusive candidate mechanisms (developmental origin heterogeneity, colonization-route boundaries 20–22, postnatal migration 23,24). Under a design-matched null recomputing the full selection rule on every block-shuffle permutation, the expected Strong-candidate count is 148.3 across the 31,764-pair pool—the 39 observed lie 3.8-fold below expectation (P(null count ≥ 39) = 1.0), and no candidate survives FDR correction (minimum q = 0.520). The catalogue is thus a prioritized hypothesis list, inheriting the atlas’s four-donor structure (Supplementary Note 11).
The screen uses a multiplicative model, expected_ω = μ_ct × μ_pair / μ_grand (Methods): a residual (observed/expected) below 1 marks a cell type less differentiated between two regions than expected from its plasticity and the pair’s divergence. Three tiers: Strong (residual < 0.3, ω < 15, lowest ω in the pair), Moderate (residual < 0.5, ω < 25), Weak (residual < 0.75, ω < 35; Supplementary Fig. 8).
Criteria identified 39 (0.12%) Strong, 1,171 (3.69%) Moderate, and 5,381 (16.94%) Weak candidates among 31,764 comparisons (Supplementary Table 4). Under the block-shuffle null (B = 1,000; Supplementary Fig. 9), 31 Strong candidates showed raw P < 0.05; the only sub-nominal stratified family is intra-cerebellar Bergmann-glia (m = 21; minimum q = 0.042). The microglial concentration (16 Strong) does not survive the design-matched null (fold 0.31, P = 0.990): we make no class-composition claim.
A negative control confirms the null is calibrated by library-level, not regional, structure: splitting libraries of every region into pseudo-regions and re-running the identical test on 127,756 pseudo-pairs (Supplementary Note 12; Supplementary Fig. 10) gave near-nominal cross-region tail rates but a 37.6% lower-tail rate for same-region halves.
The catalogue shows spatial patterning without establishing anatomical themes. Microglial candidates (16 of 39) concentrate on visual-relay and orbitofrontal dissections (Supplementary Note 11), and mature-oligodendrocyte candidates (10, all raw P < 0.05) show a thalamo-temporal orientation crossing rather than following the dorsoventral origin boundary 25 (Supplementary Note 13); sparse candidates came from astrocytes, fibroblasts, and ependymal cells 26,27, none from Bergmann glia, vascular cells, or choroid plexus 26,28. But the set is estimator-sensitive—only 3 of 39 Strong candidates survive a switch to a global-k_n estimator—so all entries are strictly hypothesis-generating 19.
An earlier per-pair label-shuffle implementation was anti-conservative (36.3% of P-values at the floor) by ignoring the 10x-library block structure (Supplementary Note 12).
