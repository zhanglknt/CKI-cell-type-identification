# Review R3 — Biostatistics
## Manuscript: "CKI is a Ka/Ks-inspired index quantifying functional divergence in single-cell genomics"
**Venue:** Nature Communications (Methodology track) · **Reviewer:** R3 (biostatistics) · **Date:** 2026-09-20

---

## Summary

The manuscript proposes CKI, a ratio index ω = k_f/k_n of two Jensen–Shannon divergences (identity-gene rate over housekeeping-gene baseline), and validates it across simulation, technical replicates, Tabula Muris/Sapiens atlases, TCGA bulk RNA-seq (3,567 samples, five cancers), and the Siletti brain atlas (31,764 cross-region pairs). The statistical self-audit is unusually thorough for this journal: design-matched permutation nulls (block-shuffle), a pseudo-region negative control, a Monte Carlo coverage study motivating studentized bootstrap-t intervals at 6–7 clusters, a ratio-estimator bias characterization, a fixed-panel circularity ablation, k_f-only ordering controls, and frank demotion of null or denominator-driven results (LIHC Cox model, Edmondson/PAM50 severity vignettes, brain candidate screen). Internal numerical consistency is high. My principal concerns are inference-validity gaps in three headline machines: (i) the TCGA NN/TT "4 of 5 CIs exclude 1" claim rests on an underspecified, coverage-unvalidated dyadic cluster bootstrap of a ratio-of-mean-ratios, while the design-matched permutation tests are all null (P > 0.9) under a self-declared anti-conservative fixed panel; (ii) the 0.953/0.951 studentized-t coverage claim is not verifiable because the simulation's data-generating process and studentization method are undescribed; (iii) the brain screen's FDR verdict is uninformative by construction at B = 1,000, m = 31,764 — a fact the authors disclose but do not remedy. I also document where numbers quoted in the review brief do not match the manuscript (the manuscript itself is consistent on those points).

## Major concerns

### 1. TCGA NN/TT inference: unvalidated bootstrap for a ratio-of-mean-ratios carries the headline, while the design-matched test is silent

Quoted (MS Results): *"LUAD 2.46 (95% CI 2.13–2.86; sample-level cluster bootstrap, B = 1,000), KIRC 1.88 (1.63–2.16), LUSC 1.71 (1.38–2.09), BRCA 1.57 (1.34–1.84), and LIHC 1.10 (0.93–1.29)—with the bootstrap CI excluding 1 in four of five cancers."*
Quoted (MS Methods): *"tumor and normal samples were resampled with replacement independently, each pair reweighted by the product of its endpoint resampling weights, and the ratio recomputed per resample."*

- The **interval type is never stated** for these CIs (percentile? studentized? BCa?). For a ratio statistic the percentile method is not transformation-respecting; and the authors' own Supplementary Note 2 shows percentile cluster intervals under-cover badly in another design (0.876/0.873 at 7/6 clusters vs nominal 0.95). No coverage validation — analytical or simulated — is provided for *this* estimator.
- The "reweight each pair by the product of endpoint resampling weights" scheme is a **nonstandard dyadic (weighted) bootstrap**. Pairs share samples (median 4–11 pairs per tumor), so ordinary pair resampling would be wrong; the instinct is right, but the first-order validity of this weighting for a *ratio of means of ratios* needs justification or a matched simulation.
- Meanwhile the per-cancer permutation tests are *"all P > 0.9"* under a fixed-HVG-panel null the authors themselves call *"anti-conservative relative to per-permutation re-selection"* (MS Methods). The pan-cancer reversal therefore currently rests on unvalidated CIs alone. The upper-tail P > 0.9 is expected under the reversal, but the corresponding **lower-tail or two-sided design-matched test of the reversal is never reported** — with per-permutation gene re-selection it would be the natural confirmatory analysis.
- Fragility accounting (means 1.10–2.46): LIHC [0.93, 1.29] already includes 1 and is disclosed as *"mapping-sensitive"* (1.10 linear vs 1.31 softmax). BRCA (lower bound 1.34) and LUSC (1.38) tolerate at most a ~34–38% multiplicative bias in the ratio-of-ratios before their CIs would touch 1; LUAD (2.13) and KIRC (1.63) are robust to any plausible bias. The manuscript should state this margin explicitly rather than leaving "4 of 5" as an unqualified caliber.
- Required: (a) state interval type; (b) coverage simulation for the endpoint-reweighted bootstrap under a design-matched null; (c) two-sided permutation test of the reversal with per-permutation re-selection; (d) explicit bias-tolerance statement per cancer.

### 2. Ratio-of-estimators inference: honest but incomplete bias characterization; coverage claim unverifiable as described

Quoted (SI Note 4): *"the bias is small and null-calibrated: median +0.2% across the 29 class × region groups (pooled −1.8%)… The largest upward bias sits in the smallest-denominator bin (k_n < 10⁻⁴: +6.5%…)"*
Quoted (SI Note 2): *"0.953/0.951 for the studentized bootstrap-t, which is therefore the recommended replacement for every statistic resting on ≤ 7 region clusters (seed 20260905; B = 5,000 on the real data)."*

- **Verification vs the review brief:** the brief's "1.3-fold median ratio bias" appears nowhere in MS/SI. The disclosed ratio bias is +0.2% median (pooled −1.8%), characterized on 1,450 brain split-half replicates as E[k_f/k_n] vs E[k_f]/E[k_n]. The "1.3" figures in the manuscript are different quantities: the brain-internal calibration baseline 9.73 being "~1.3-fold above the mouse-derived factor" (MS; SI Note 3) and the TCGA TT/NN k_n elevation of "1.3–3.3-fold". Disclosure of the actual ratio bias is honest and delta-method-tracked (ρ = 0.99/1.00).
- However, Note 4 characterizes the **single ratio** on brain calibration replicates. It does **not** quantify: (a) bias of the TCGA NN/TT **ratio of mean ratios** (the estimator behind Major 1); (b) interaction with the TCGA-only kn_floor = 1×10⁻⁴ (aggregate k_n 3.0×10⁻⁵–1.9×10⁻⁴, so *"aggregate ω saturates at k_f/10⁻⁴ in 3 of 5 cancer types"*); (c) differential circular-selection floors between TT and NN pairs (k_f inflation median 1.61-fold, IQR 1.27–2.07 — disclosed — cancels in the ratio only to first order).
- **Studentized bootstrap-t for a ratio:** methodologically the right choice among the candidates compared, and preferable to percentile/wild at 6–7 clusters (wild is crippled by 2⁶–2⁷ sign combinations, as the authors note). But the coverage claim **0.953/0.951 is not verifiable from the text**: the Monte Carlo data-generating process (cluster sizes, within-cluster dependence, skew/kurtosis matched to the real ω distribution — brain skew 2.22, excess kurtosis 6.02) and the **studentization method** (inner bootstrap? delta-method SE?) are not described. With 2,000 simulations the MCSE is ≈ 0.49%, so 0.953 is statistically consistent with nominal — credibility therefore hinges entirely on DGP realism. Bootstrap-t is also known to be tail-sensitive under heavy skew; a skew-robustness variant of the simulation is needed.
- Near-zero-denominator handling is otherwise responsible: kn_floor = 0 (positivity guard only) for single-cell, 1 of 31,764 brain pairs below 1×10⁻⁴, and exclusion of small-k_n pairs moves the gradient *up* (6.10 → 6.52), i.e., the headline is conservative in this respect.

### 3. Permutation design: sharp-null vs functional claim; one-sided default; error-control unit

Quoted (MS Methods): *"The one-sided default is appropriate because our hypothesis is directional: we test whether observed ω exceeds the null expectation, not whether it differs in either direction."*

- **Null specification.** Label permutation tests sharp exchangeability of the full expression distributions; rejection licenses only "labels are not exchangeable," not specifically "functional divergence in excess of the HK baseline." The authors partly concede this ("a significant ω rejects label exchangeability under that null, and attribution to functional divergence further requires excluding library-level confounders"), but the null that would directly license the functional claim — a k_n-matched or baseline-constrained null — is never constructed. For the TCGA analysis the fixed-panel deviation makes the test anti-conservative *and* it returns P > 0.9 everywhere, so the TCGA permutation family is uninformative in both directions.
- **One-sided default.** For within-cell-type state comparisons, both directions (divergence vs constraint) are a priori plausible; the brain pair screen in fact uses the *lower* tail while class-level tests use the *upper* tail. Direction choices are defensible per family but must be demonstrably pre-specified; a blanket "directional hypothesis" justification is insufficient, and the practice interacts with the calibration claim ("No control comparison reached significance (all P > 0.05, one-sided)") — a non-significant one-sided test is weak evidence of equivalence, as the authors themselves note (TOST would be stronger).
- **Per-dataset BH as the error-control unit.** Families are m = 10 (brain classes), 15 (mouse), 17 (human), 5 (TCGA), and 31,764 (brain pairs), with *"no cross-family joint correction"* — disclosed and defensible given opposite directional hypotheses. But numerous additional test families go uncorrected and are only partially flagged as post-hoc: drift-ladder tiers (B = 100/30/30 per pair), residual-enrichment hypergeometrics (P = 6.0×10⁻⁴, 9.6×10⁻³¹, Cochran–Armitage z = 61.0), Dunn–Holm batteries, admixture/smoking-adjusted OLS contrasts (P = 0.003–0.009), and the JT/KW severity tests (*"nominal and carry no multiplicity correction"* — disclosed for Note 9 only). The TCGA "4 of 5 CIs exclude 1" headline carries no multiplicity statement at all; with m = 5 the family is small and the statement should be made.
- **Brain screen burden (m = 31,764, B = 1,000).** The authors' arithmetic is correct: the BH threshold for the smallest P (0.05/31,764 ≈ 1.6×10⁻⁶) lies ~600-fold below the permutation floor (9.99×10⁻⁴), so q < 0.05 requires ~635 floor P-values or B ≈ 6×10⁵. The reported outcome (minimum q = 0.520; 31 of 39 Strong candidates at raw P < 0.05) is therefore **uninformative by construction** — the design cannot reject, so "none survived FDR" carries no evidential weight against candidates. The honest remedy is a targeted high-B re-run (or continuous null model) for the 39 Strong candidates; the global raw-P excess (1,960 vs 1,588 expected; P = 0.011, Bonferroni 0.022) and the design-matched Strong-rule expectation (148.3 expected vs 39 observed) are informative summaries and are the right kind of analysis — but "minimum q = 0.520" should not appear as if it were a deliberative FDR evaluation. **Verification vs the brief:** the brief's "30 Strong / 16 FDR-significant" appears nowhere; MS and SI uniformly report **39 Strong, 31 raw P < 0.05, zero FDR-surviving**, consistent across MS (Results, Discussion), SI §3.3, Note 13, and Table 4. (The "36.3% at the floor" figure refers to the superseded label-shuffle implementation, properly disclosed.)

### 4. Severity-gradient statistics: legitimate test, unacceptable reporting; brief misattributes the P-value

Quoted (SI Note 9): *"LIHC Edmondson grade: mean ω is highest in G1, roughly flat across G2–G3, and lowest in G4 (78.2, 76.8, 77.9, 72.6 for G1–G4; Jonckheere-Terpstra P ≈ 0), but k_f increases with grade (JT P = 6.9 × 10⁻¹⁵) and k_n increases in parallel (JT P ≈ 0), so the ω gradient is a denominator effect."*

- The JT test is computed on **per-tumor values (n = 288 LIHC tumors), not on four aggregate percentages** — so the test is meaningful in principle, contrary to the "four numbers" reading. However: (a) *"P ≈ 0"* is not acceptable reporting — give exact P or P < 10⁻¹⁶; (b) per-grade n is not reported (only "Edmondson G4 n = 11" appears, in the caveats section); (c) the trend is **non-monotone** (76.8 → 77.9 across G2–G3), so a monotone-alternative test is driven by G1 vs G4 and should be paired with the omnibus KW; (d) the gradient is denominator-driven by the authors' own k_f-only control — correctly demoted to the SI as a "denominator-dominated vignette." Note the **P = 6.9×10⁻¹⁵ attaches to k_f increasing with grade, not to the ω gradient**; the manuscript does not conflate the two, and neither should any citation of it.
- The LIHC Cox result is appropriately downweighted and reported as a genuine null in the main text: *"per-tumor ω was not associated with overall survival in LIHC (Cox hazard ratio per SD 1.07, 95% CI 0.88–1.31, P = 0.48, adjusted for stage, grade, age, and sex)… not a prognostic marker"* (consistent with the brief's 1.075 [0.882, 1.309], P = 0.475 at stated rounding). Keeping the null in the main text is commendable. Missing: n/events after listwise deletion, proportional-hazards diagnostics, a power statement, and the k_f-only/k_n-only/TN-ω sensitivity models — **Supplementary Table 14 is listed in the TOC but its contents are absent from the SI as provided** (the SI ends after Tables 1–4; Tables 5–19, including Table 18 severity gradients and Table 15 purity sensitivity, have no content in the document).

### 5. Purity ANCOVA: adequate as sensitivity analysis, not as the "not an artefact" verdict

Quoted (MS Results): *"per-tumor k_n correlated negatively with admixture in every cancer type (r = −0.23 to −0.42), so admixture can only weaken, not create, the TT k_n elevation… The reversal is therefore not an artefact of stromal or immune admixture."*
Quoted (SI Note 8 update): *"pooled four-panel attenuation −0.8% (cluster-bootstrap median −1.2%, 95% CI [−4.1%, +2.6%]); per-cancer attenuation LIHC +37.1% [+27.1%, +51.5%], KIRC +18.3% [+13.9%, +23.4%], BRCA −16.2%…"*

- ESTIMATE scores are computed from the **same expression matrix** as k_f/k_n, so the covariate is mechanically coupled to the outcome; single-covariate linear OLS adjustment with a noisy proxy guarantees **residual confounding** (attenuation toward no adjustment). The low-admixture-half sensitivity (LUAD 2.46 → 2.86 [2.41–3.45]; LIHC 1.10 → 1.17, CI still including 1) is the more convincing control and is properly reported.
- The pooled "essentially unchanged (−1.2%)" framing sits awkwardly next to the disclosed per-cancer heterogeneity (LIHC +37%, KIRC +18%: composition *does* absorb substantial variance in 2 of 5 cancers). The main text does quote the range (−16% to +37%), but the per-cancer statements, not the pooled near-zero, are the honest headline. "Not an artefact" overstates what observational adjustment can show; the Discussion's softer "bounds rather than settles" is the right register and should govern the Results wording too.

## Minor concerns

1. Calibration CI drift: mean ω = 7.70 with 95% CI [7.37, 8.02] (MS, twice) vs bootstrap B = 10,000 [7.38, 8.02] (SI Note 3). Trivial, but unify one caliber.
2. Brain threshold sweep sentence gives three gradient values for four thresholds: *"thresholds of 10, 20 (reported), 50, and 100 nuclei retain 10, 10, 10, and 8 classes with gradients of 6.60, 6.10, and 4.12"* — the threshold→gradient mapping is ambiguous (Table 19 content absent from SI).
3. Borderline permutation P-values carry Monte Carlo error (±≈0.011 at P = 0.03, B = 1,000): fibroblasts P = 0.030 / q = 0.075 sits close to 0.05; report MC confidence intervals for borderline P/q.
4. SES on heavy-tailed nulls (skew 2.28, kurtosis 9.14) is reported as descriptive — good — but a median/MAD-based robust SES would be more stable for the skewed classes.
5. Dunn–Holm "manual implementation, tie-corrected rank variances" — verify against a reference implementation (e.g., scikit-posthocs) and state so.
6. Pseudo-region negative control: is the real-minus-pseudo tail excess (6.17% vs 5.79% lower; 7.90% vs 6.87% upper) significant given that both runs share the same libraries (dependence between the two P-value sets)?
7. Table 1: sparse block (n = 1) handled honestly ("SD —"); endothelial/erythrocyte SDs at n = 3 carry minimal information — consider suppressing.
8. Cover letter overstates relative to the MS: *"ω alone did not false-trigger (false-positive rate 0.00)"* omits the manuscript's own bounds (0/150, upper bound 0.0198; pooled 2/250 [0.001, 0.029]). Carry the bound, not the point.
9. KRAS–WT k_f contrast: Dunn P = 0.097 (ns) vs bootstrap difference 0.011 [0.002, 0.019] (excludes 0) — two unadjusted tests of the same contrast pointing differently; explain or harmonize (the adjusted P = 0.009 is the one relied upon).
10. "BH thresholds… are all above the minimum resolvable permutation P-value… so resolution is sufficient for every group-level test" (MS Statistics) is correct for m ≤ 17 but note this logic inverts for the pair screen; cross-reference to avoid confusion.
11. Reproducibility: Python 3.14.4 / scanpy 1.12.1 versions and seeds are documented (good); confirm the pinned environment actually ships in the Dockerfile.

## Questions to authors

1. What interval type (percentile / studentized / BCa) are the TCGA NN/TT cluster-bootstrap CIs? Provide a coverage simulation for the endpoint-reweighted dyadic bootstrap, and report the two-sided (or lower-tail) per-cancer permutation P for the reversal with per-permutation gene re-selection.
2. Describe the Note 2 coverage-simulation DGP fully: cluster count/sizes, within-cluster dependence, marginal distribution and skew/kurtosis matching to real ω, and the studentization method. How does 0.953/0.951 degrade under DGP misspecification?
3. Quantify the bias of the NN/TT ratio-of-mean-ratios itself (including kn_floor = 10⁻⁴ interaction and differential circular-selection floors between TT and NN pairs), and state the resulting bias-tolerance margin for BRCA and LUSC.
4. Was test direction fixed before data inspection for every family? If not, which families used data-driven direction, and what is the effective error rate?
5. Why was B = 1,000 retained for the m = 31,764 screen when your own arithmetic shows FDR is unresolvable? Will you run a targeted high-B (≈10⁵–10⁶) or continuous-null analysis of the 39 Strong candidates?
6. Report per-grade n and exact JT P-values (Table 18); replace "P ≈ 0"; and supply the missing Supplementary Tables 5–19 contents (notably Table 14 Cox models, Table 15, Table 18).
7. Cox model: n/events after listwise deletion, PH diagnostics (e.g., Schoenfeld), and a power statement for HR per SD = 1.07.
8. Given LIHC +37.1% and KIRC +18.3% composition attenuation, will you replace the pooled −1.2% headline with per-cancer statements in the main text, and temper "not an artefact" accordingly?
9. For the label-permutation families, can you construct a baseline-constrained (k_n-matched) null so that rejection licenses the *functional* claim rather than generic non-exchangeability?

## Score: 5/10 (strict)

## Recommendation: **Major Revision**

The methodology is careful and the self-audit culture exemplary, but three pieces of statistical machinery central to the headline claims require work that goes beyond text edits: (i) validated inference for the TCGA ratio-of-mean-ratios (interval type, coverage, a design-matched two-sided test with re-selection); (ii) a verifiable coverage simulation (DGP + studentization) supporting the 0.953/0.951 claim and a bias analysis of the ratio-of-ratios actually used; (iii) a resolvable multiplicity design for the brain screen (targeted high-B), plus restoration of the missing Supplementary Tables 5–19 and reporting hygiene ("P ≈ 0", per-stratum n, one-sided-default justification). I would be favorable toward acceptance after these are addressed.
